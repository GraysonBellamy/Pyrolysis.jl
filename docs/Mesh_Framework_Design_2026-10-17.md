# Mesh framework design and implementation plan

**Date:** 2026-10-17 · **Status:** design, nothing applied · **Scope:** the mesh, state and Jacobian architecture the package commits to before extending beyond 1D

Companions: `reports/Pyrolysis mesh motion alternatives.md` (the literature survey this builds on, with its four research notes), `MultiD_Extension_Assessment_2026-09-26.md` (what is 1D by construction), `Performance_Improvement_Plan_2026-09-26.md` (the Jacobian nonlocality measurements), `Gpyro_V2_Comparison_2026-09-26.md` (the burnout deficit). `file:line` pointers refer to `main` at 17bc477.

## Verdict

1. **Keep the cell, change what the solver stores.** The solid-Lagrangian, mass-carrying cell is shared by every production fire code and is not the problem. The integrator should carry per-cell component masses, an energy state (temperature by default, the enthalpy inventory behind the state mapping for the relative-flux work), node positions in every dimension, and the closure's local algebraic unknowns (the velocities themselves in 1D, one multiplier per cell in the potential closure). Concentrations and temperature become derived quantities that feed the kinetics, property and surface-balance code unchanged, through one state-mapping function that is the only reader of the raw states (§3.2).
2. **Mesh velocity is the solid velocity plus a soft correction.** `w = v_s + c`, with `c = 0` while cells are healthy and a smooth equidistribution correction where a layer is vanishing. Charring decks run with `c = 0` and reproduce today's results. It is the only option in the survey that could remove burnout events without creating voids, and it is the 1D form of the smoothing operator every multi-D ablation code uses.
3. **Every nonlocal coupling becomes a local algebraic equation.** The node-velocity prefix sum becomes a bidiagonal closure on algebraic nodal velocities; the Beer–Lambert suffix becomes face intensities; the variable-cross-section scalars become algebraic unknowns; the multi-D solid-velocity closure is the same nodal-velocity block with a sparse operator. The system is an index-1 mass-matrix DAE. The installed `KenCarp4` steps it; the constraint checks, the consistent initialization and the re-initialization at events are the project's own (§3.8). Lifting is the default because the exact alternative, a bordered solve, needs every coupling enumerated by hand; the Phase 0 spike compares both. The Jacobian pattern is banded in every geometry, planar or radial, which is what makes colored sparse automatic differentiation cheap.
4. **Colored sparse AD is the Jacobian.** Pattern built from the mesh connectivity and checked against dense AD, coloring once, a stencil-sized number of dual passes per Jacobian. The 8.6k-line operator layer and the reduced-versus-full strategy split are deleted once the Phase 0 timing shows the colored passes within the wall-time gate; if they are not, the hot operators keep analytical kernels as a fast path verified against AD, never as the source of truth.
5. **Deactivation is the terminal event, not the burnout strategy.** A vanishing layer collapses once, at a mass-fraction threshold, into a masked cell with fixed state size. The correction's purpose is to make this one event per layer, and none for residue-forming materials; that is a hypothesis with acceptance criteria (§3.5), not a property of the monitor.
6. **Kernels are face-based from the first line.** Faces carry area and normal, cells carry volume, flux kernels take a full symmetric tensor, and material orientation and history are fields carried by the solid, never read from the mesh. 1D planar, 1D radial, axisymmetric and planar 2D are mesh generators over one kernel set and one state representation.
7. **Build the new core beside the old one, then delete the old one in one commit.** The current 1D code is the oracle during the build. An in-place refactor would have to keep every intermediate state working and would breed compatibility layers.
8. **Rejected for the core:** fixed Eulerian grids, level-set and diffuse-interface surfaces, Lagrange-remap and FDS-style renoding, Landau front-fixing, h-adaptivity, particle methods, and a Gpyro-style inert floor. Fixed-grid deactivation remains the fallback for topology change (perforation, dripping, edge burn-through). A layer that vanishes beneath a surviving skin is outside the design (§3.5).

**What is permanent and what is provisional.** No numerical method can be promised never to change. The commitments are the interfaces: faces and geometry with tags, the state mapping, kernels that return flux and source contributions, the closure interface, the three deactivation policies, solid-carried material fields, and the linear-solver interface over physics blocks. The numerical choices behind them are provisional and evidence-gated: the activated correction, mass-threshold deactivation, algebraic lifting against a bordered solve, the integrator with its restart and per-step-flag policies, and the AD-versus-analytical Jacobian. Fixed topology and one sparsity pattern hold within a solve segment; a topology change ends the segment and starts a new integrator on a new pattern (§3.8).

Rough single-developer effort, 1D suite kept green: measurements and contracts three to four weeks; new 1D core 8 to 12 weeks; burnout floor 4 to 6 weeks; cutover 6 to 8 weeks, since 30.7k lines of tests and the guides move with the core and the 2D and 3D demonstrations precede the deletion; 2D column mode 4 to 6 weeks on top; general 2D closure and smoothing 6 to 8 weeks more. About 6 to 7 months to a 1D cutover, 10 to 11 months to general 2D.

## 1. Findings

### 1.1 What the survey established

The survey covered the fire codes (FDS, ThermaKin, Gpyro, OpenFOAM), sixty years of ablation codes (CMA through CHAR, PATO and Icarus), and the fixed-grid, level-set, cut-cell and particle literature. Its conclusions, checked against the sources during this review:

| Finding | Evidence | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Every mainstream fire code uses the same mass-carrying solid-Lagrangian cell; they differ only in how they remediate degenerate cells (FDS remap, ThermaKin rezone, Gpyro and OpenFOAM inert freeze, Pyrolysis.jl merge) | FDS `wall.f90`, ThermaKin2Ds Reference v2 §3, Gpyro Tech Ref §4.1/§6.4, `reactingOneDim.C` | The design choice is the remediation policy, not the cell |
| No production code puts node motion under adaptive error control; converged codes agree within 1 to 3% whatever the mesh strategy | 2011 Ablation Workshop, PATO vs FIAT, ITRAC vs CMA | No accuracy gain is available on converged 1D cases; the differences are in cost, burnout, strong swelling and multi-D |
| The burnout cost is the merge itself, not the step reset: the keeper cell lands off the quasi-steady profile and the predictor misses the Newton basin | Technical Reference §14.3; 61% of V-BO-01 steps are post-park recovery; larger resets doubled the step count | Cheaper resets cannot fix it; the event must be removed or made rare |
| Letting cells collapse (1e-10 to 1e-13 m) or freezing them inert both fail on an adaptive implicit solver: step collapse and 20 to 40% Newton failures, or void films that distort the energy path (V-BO-05) | Performance plan §2; V-BO-05 | A finite inert floor is not an option |
| A continuous-ALE monitor keeps every cell between `S/(N M_max)` and `S/(N α)` at exact equidistribution, `S = ∫M`: the floor `α` bounds cells from above, the monitor's maximum bounds them from below, and both scale with `S(t)`, so a vanishing layer's cells shrink with it | Huang & Kamenski 2018; Budd, Huang & Russell 2009 | Cells are bounded relative to the layer, not absolutely; one terminal event per layer is the target, not a theorem |
| The prefix-sum coupling through the node velocity makes the exact Jacobian 60 to 90× more expensive; thickness states remove it for planar 1D, but radial face areas and volumes are functions of the radius, which is the same prefix sum | Performance plan §5.1; Technical Reference §15 | Lift the node velocity to an algebraic unknown with a local closure instead; exact Jacobian at roughly the reduced cost in every geometry |
| Intumescence (20 to 100×) has no published fixed-grid model and no published grid-convergence study; material-coordinate schemes lose char resolution as 1/M | Cirpici, Larsen, Lucherini, Ogrin | Measure before building; graded initial meshes are the free mitigation |
| 2D needs a solid-velocity closure regardless of mesh strategy; multi-D practice places interior nodes by pseudo-elastic, Laplacian or spring solves; merging has no 2D analogue | CHAR, PATO, KATS, Amaryllis; FDS User Guide §8.4.2 | The 1D correction is the 2D operator's precursor; deactivation replaces merging |

### 1.2 Additional findings from this review

- **Velocity-based moving meshes already derive the mesh velocity from conservation through a potential.** Baines, Hubbard and Jimack write the Eulerian conservation law as a spatial equation for the velocity, impose a curl condition, and solve an elliptic equation for a velocity potential; Bochev, Liao and de la Peña prove no tangling for this construction when the boundary motion does not tangle. Cao, Huang and Russell derive mesh velocities from the geometric conservation law with a monitor controlling cell volumes and note the method "bears a close relation with the Lagrangian method." These are the multi-D templates for `w = v_s + c`.
- **No published pyrolysis or ablation code runs continuous ALE through burnout to zero residue.** Re-searched here; the gap stands. The nearest precedents are Bonnerot and Jamet's conservative front-tracking finite elements for Stefan problems with appearing and disappearing phases (1981), and Hubbard's 2025 conservative ALE moving-mesh method for implicit moving-boundary problems. Both treat a vanishing phase as an event. Expect at least one terminal event per layer, not zero.
- **ThermaKin writes its equations in concentrations but stores masses.** Equations 15 to 17 of the reference are in ξ and T; §3 states that each element "is characterized by masses of components and temperature. These parameters serve as primary object descriptors." Gpyro stores mass per area and enthalpy. FDS is the one mainstream code that stores concentrations and rescales thickness.
- **`KenCarp4` steps diagonal mass-matrix DAEs, but the installed stepper does not treat the algebraic rows specially.** `OrdinaryDiffEqSDIRK` 2.9.6 divides the explicit first stage by the mass-matrix diagonal (`generic_imex_perform_step.jl`, `_mmdiag`/`_mmdiv`) and builds the `algebraic_vars` mask only for `ImplicitEuler` (`sdirk_caches.jl:188`); the ESDIRK error estimate `Σ b̃_i z_i` sums every row. `OrdinaryDiffEqCore` 7 defaults initialization to `CheckInit`, which rejects an inconsistent state rather than fixing it, and callbacks that modify `u` re-run `initialize_dae!`. The nonlinear solver's convergence test reads the same `internalnorm` (`OrdinaryDiffEqNonlinearSolve/src/newton.jl:551-555`), so a norm that masks the algebraic rows lets the Newton stop with the constraint unconverged: on `ẋ = 0`, `0 = z² − (1 + t)` with `KenCarp4` at fixed steps of 0.1 and tolerances of 1e-10, the masked norm reports success with a constraint residual of 2.9e-4 after 50 Newton iterations, against 1.8e-12 after 326 with the full norm. The performance plan rejected the lifted-ODE form (option B′) for its effect on error control, initialization and events; those costs are real, and this design pays them explicitly: the algebraic rows stay in the norm, constraint residuals are checked outside the error estimate, the consistent sweep runs at `t₀` and after every deactivation, and a Phase 0 spike compares `KenCarp4`, `FBDF`, `Rodas5P` and a bordered solve on a toy DAE with state jumps.
- **The Beer–Lambert suffix would defeat column coloring on its own.** Row *i* of the energy block depends on every cell above it, so the top cell's columns appear in every energy row and all those columns conflict pairwise: column coloring then needs of order *n* colors, as expensive as dense AD. Lifting the face intensities to algebraic unknowns with a local recurrence makes the pattern banded. The same applies to the variable-cross-section global scalars, whose single dense row is written by hand (it is a mean).
- **The semi-discrete GCL does not survive time stepping where volume is nonlinear in position.** On a sphere with a prescribed radial velocity and a uniform concentration, `KenCarp4` leaves a concentration error of 3.4e-5, 2.2e-6 and 1.4e-7 at steps of 0.2, 0.1 and 0.05 although the semi-discrete identity is exact: the integrator advances `x` and `m` by the same quadrature, but `V(x)` is cubic in `x`. Planar 1D is exact because `V` is linear in `z`. Free-stream preservation in radial and multi-D geometry is therefore a convergence property of the method's order, not an identity, and is tested as such.
- **The Julia sparse-AD pipeline is production-ready for the passes, not for the pattern.** SparseMatrixColorings for coloring and DifferentiationInterface over ForwardDiff for the colored passes are installed, and the preparation cost is paid once per problem structure. Global sparsity tracing is not used: a global tracer carries no value, so it cannot evaluate the surface Newton's termination test (`src/Discretization/boundary_temperature.jl:215`) or the upwind switches in `Discretization` (17 value-dependent branches), and rewriting every kernel with `ifelse` and fixed iteration counts for the tracer's sake would shape the code around a tool. On a structured mesh the pattern is a function of connectivity alone, so it is built by hand and verified at small size to contain the dense-AD pattern. The project already keeps the residual dual-clean and lets duals pass through the surface Newton (`boundary_temperature.jl:170-330`).
- **Multi-D swelling closures in current codes are mechanics solves.** PATO's pyromechanics module (Lahouze et al. 2025) combines elastic, thermal-expansion and pyrolysis strains in a volume-averaged porous solid, incrementally, validated on shrinking wood cylinders. Ogrin et al. restrict intumescent expansion to the direction normal to the substrate on a fixed reference mesh. ThermaKin2Ds reaches 2D by deforming column by column with spatially non-uniform swelling (Swann et al. 2019). The column mode and the elastic eigenstrain closure are the two forms in use. They are different models. With an isotropic eigenstrain `θ/3·I` the volumetric strain rate equals `θ` only where the stress trace vanishes or the Poisson ratio is 1/2, so under a non-uniform `θ` the elastic closure lets the cell volume depart from the free density law and `ξ = m/V` becomes an outcome of mechanics; that is PATO's model, and it is physically right where swelling is constrained. For anisotropic materials (wood) the prescribed strain-rate tensor is not compatible in general, so no velocity field realizes it exactly and a mechanics closure is the only general one. The volume-constrained potential form is the isotropic volumetric case that reproduces the 1D core. §3.4 keeps both behind one interface.

### 1.3 Corrections to the earlier summary

The earlier summary agreed with the survey in substance. Three statements need correction:

- "Deactivated cells … smooth in parameters." Deactivation is a threshold event. What buys smoothness is the correction making events rare or absent.
- "Per-cell masses plus cell volumes." Volumes are functions of node positions in every dimension, radial 1D included, so the nodes are the state everywhere and locality comes from the algebraic velocity rows.
- "Merge and park → deactivated cells from day one." Park-and-mask already is fixed-size deactivation. The change is the floor, not the mechanism.

## 2. The current design, as built

- **States:** `T` and `ξ_j` per cell, node positions `z` (ALE), optional `χ` (variable cross-section); block-major layout in `src/Problem/state_layout.jl:18-22`.
- **Mesh velocity:** bottom node fixed, `w_k = Σ_{i<k}(θ_i − θ_A)Δz_i` accumulated upward (`src/Residual/residual.jl:329-373`), θ from `volume_change_rate` (`src/Physics/volume_change.jl:106`). Solids and liquids mesh-bound; gas mesh-bound with dilation only; `w_override` hook with a limited T-advection kernel for verification (`src/Discretization/ale_fluxes.jl`).
- **Burnout:** cells below 5% of initial thickness are parked, merged into the interior neighbour with enthalpy conserved, masked in a fixed-size state, and the integrator is handed a proposed step of 1e-6 s (`src/Solver/callbacks.jl:420-487`, `src/Adaptivity/surface_depletion.jl`, `cell_merge.jl`, `cell_park.jl`). One cell parks per callback: in V-BO-04 all 40 cells cross the threshold at once and the run pays 35 park events.
- **Jacobian:** `Structured` backend walking hand-derived operators (`src/Jacobian/structured_backend.jl:300-590`), structured prefix/suffix/rank-1 couplings densified at emit (`structured_couplings.jl`), a dense cells × nodes geometry block (`operators/geometry.jl:228-270`), a dense n² sparsity lookup (`fast_sparsity_map.jl:27-55`), no coloring, dense ForwardDiff as the oracle. Production under ALE uses the reduced (`ApproxSolve`) strategy; `DirectSolve` with UMFPACK stays available.
- **Kernels:** 1D stencils with index arithmetic; the divergence reads one face area for both faces (`src/Discretization/finite_volume.jl:46-88`); BC consumers hard-code `top` and `bottom`. Kinetics clamps `ξ` at zero (`src/Physics/kinetics.jl:63`) and porosity is clamped to `[0, 1]` (`src/Properties/mixture_rules.jl:400`). On a 1.10-stretched 100-cell mesh whose smallest cell is 22 nm, the first step's Newton drives the trial `χ̄` to an enormous negative value and the lateral shrinkage law returns a non-positive cross-section before any step is accepted (Phase 0 item 3): the global scalars have no trial-state guard.
- **Size:** `src/` 31.8k lines, of which Jacobian 8.6k, Discretization 3.4k, Experimental 2.9k, Solver 2.7k, Problem 2.6k, Adaptivity 1.8k, Geometry 1.2k, Physics 1.1k, Residual 0.6k; Materials, Properties, BoundaryConditions and Diagnostics 6.4k together. `test/` 30.7k lines.

## 3. The target design

### 3.1 Continuum model

Unchanged. The governing equations, kinetics, property models, surface energy balance, blowing and absorptivity treatments, gas transport (Darcy–Fick, flux form) and the energy ledger stay as derived in the Technical Reference. The discrete unknowns change; the physics they feed does not.

### 3.2 Discrete unknowns

Per cell *i*:

| Unknown | Symbol | Equation | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Component masses | `m_{ij}` | `dm_{ij}/dt = ω_{ij} V_i + Σ_f F_{ij,f} A_f` | Reactions plus face fluxes (gas transport; relative solid flux when `c ≠ 0`). Exact conservation by construction. No dilation term. |
| Energy state | `T_i` by default; `H_i` behind the state mapping | Inventory form: `dH_i/dt = −Σ_f q_f A_f + Σ_f Σ_j F_{ij,f} h_j(T_f) A_f + Q_rad + Q_rxn(T_i) V_i + Σ_j ω_{ij} h_j(T_i) V_i`; temperature form: `(Σ_j m_{ij} c_{p,j}) dT_i/dt` equals the same right-hand side minus `Σ_j h_j(T_i) dm_{ij}/dt` | Temperature is the default state: it needs no inversion, so its trial-state contract is a temperature evaluation extension plus a floored heat-capacity sum. The two forms agree semi-discretely, and the time integrator does not preserve that agreement: summed enthalpy is a linear invariant that any Runge–Kutta step conserves to round-off, while the temperature form conserves energy only to the method's order whenever `c_p` depends on temperature or the composition changes (two closed cells with `c_p = T` and `c_p = 1` exchanging heat under `KenCarp4`: energy error 2.0e-4, 1.4e-5 and 9.5e-7 at steps of 0.2, 0.1 and 0.05, against round-off for the inventory form). That error is the same order as today's production code and its error control, so it is a convergence obligation with a quantitative energy-balance gate in both forms, not a loss relative to today. The enthalpy inventory is the alternative behind the state mapping for the relative-flux work, where conservation to round-off matters; it costs the regularized inversion defined below (a bracketed Newton that took up to 82 iterations and two to eight times the temperature form's wall time in the Phase 0 probe, so a warm start from the previous temperature is an implementation requirement), and reaching round-off in practice also needs converged solves and stage-level accounting. Measured on the nonlinear caloric probe, both forms converge at fourth order with comparable temperature error, the inventory ledger holds at 1e-16, and the temperature-form ledger error falls from 3e-6 to 1e-8 as the tolerance tightens from 1e-6 to 1e-10. Neither form is superior across robustness, conservation and cost; the default is provisional. Sensible enthalpy `h_j(T) = ∫ c_{p,j} dT` from a common reference. The flux term is the enthalpy carried by every face mass flux, gas transport and relative solid flux alike, with the same `F_{ij,f}` as the mass row. `T_f` is the distance-weighted face temperature for the gas flux, which makes the expanded gas term the centred second-order discretization the current `S_conv` uses (cell Péclet about 0.02, so centred is stable), and the limiter-reconstructed upwind state for the relative solid flux when `c ≠ 0`; isothermal advection is exact for any `T_f` because the mass fluxes are shared. The temperature-form source `S_conv` (`src/Physics/convection.jl`) is the advection piece of this same divergence and does not appear: adding it would count the gas transport twice. `Q_rxn(T)` is the heat of reaction quoted at the local temperature, as now, and the `Σ_j ω_{ij} h_j` term is what makes that convention exact in inventory form; expanding `dH/dt` recovers the current temperature equation term by term, and that expansion is checked against the ledger with gas transport on. The surface balance keeps no gas-enthalpy term: the gas leaves through the exposed face at `h_g(T_s)` inside the flux sum. Either form is a two-function change (state mapping, below), and the Phase 1 gate runs both. |
| Node positions | `x_n` | `dx_n/dt = w_n` | Every dimension. 1D planar and radial use the same row; positions are never reconstructed from thicknesses. |
| Closure unknowns and nodal velocities | `w_n`; `φ_i` in the potential closure | `0 = Σ_f A_f (w·n)_f − (θ_i − θ_A) V_i − s_i` per cell; `w = W⁻¹ Dᵀ φ` locally where `φ` is used, `W` the nodal lumped volumes | Algebraic, every dimension. With `c = 0` both fluxes vanish and the row is today's. | Face intensity (Beer–Lambert) | `I_f` | `0 = I_{f⁻} − I_{f⁺} exp(−α_i Δ_i)` | Algebraic, linear, local. The energy row of cell *i* reads `I` at its two faces only. |

Derived per cell: `V_i` from the node positions (and the cross-section scalar `A` where the lateral law is active), `ξ_{ij} = m_{ij}/V_i`, `T_i` from `H_i`, mass fractions, porosity, gas pressure (ideal gas, as now).

**State mapping.** Kernels never read `m`, `H` or `x` from the state vector. One function maps the state to the derived cache (`T`, `ξ`, `V`, porosity, pressure, face metrics) and one assembles the time-derivative rows from the flux and source contributions the kernels return. Replacing `T_i` by `H_i` as the state, or the mass states by concentrations, touches those two functions and the tolerance vector and nothing else. This is a requirement on the module layout; the Phase 1 gate checks it by running the suite once with `H` as the state.

Thickness states were considered for 1D and rejected: `h_i` is local only while face areas are constant. In cylinder and sphere geometry every area and volume depends on the radius, which is the cumulative sum of thicknesses, and the dense lower-triangular coupling returns. Positions plus algebraic velocities are local in every geometry and are the representation 2D and 3D use.

Error control: relative tolerance as now, plus a vector absolute tolerance per row. In the inventory form the derived temperature of a cell obeys `δT ≈ (δH − Σ_j h_j δm_j) / (Σ_j m_{ij} c_{p,j})`, so absolute tolerances at the level of a cell's initial inventory loosen `T` of a vanishing cell by `m_i(0)/m_i`, a factor of a hundred to a thousand at the deactivation threshold. The absolute tolerance of every inventory row of a cell is therefore at most `rtol · ε_m` times a positive cell scale: the cell's total initial solid mass for the mass rows, that mass times `c_p` times a reference temperature difference for `H`. Per-species or reference-state scales are not used, because a product species starts at zero mass and sensible enthalpy at the reference temperature is zero. Algebraic rows stay in the error norm; constraint residuals are checked outside it (§3.8). Positivity and trial states: the contract has two parts. Every residual evaluation at any real-valued state returns finite values and finite derivatives, because Newton iterates leave the domain before any step can be rejected; and the physical vector field never points out of the non-negative domain, because a rejection rule cannot make an intrinsically non-positive solution positive. Different pieces meet the two parts, and the guarded quantities are listed, not implied. Depletion protection keeps the structure the current kinetics has (`src/Physics/kinetics.jl:57`): the rate is multiplied, per reactant, by a one-sided limiter `g(ξ_j/ξ_th)` that is exactly zero at and below zero concentration and `C¹` through it (built by joining a `C¹` onset with zero value and zero slope at `x = 0` onto the current `tanh(x)` factor at a stated `x₀` of order one, so that calibrated rates are unchanged above `x₀ ξ_th` and the calibration gate covers only the onset interval; an onset such as `x²/(1 + x²)` used everywhere would lower the current factor by 17% at `x = 2` and 1% at `x = 10`), so an absent reactant is never consumed whatever the reaction order, order zero included, and the fractional-order singularity at depletion never appears. A smooth base substitution alone cannot do this: any base positive at zero lets absent reactants react, and it leaves `ξ^0 = 1` untouched. Finite evaluation uses a finite base for the power, `δ · softplus(ξ/δ)` with `δ ≪ ξ_th`, which equals `ξ` to within `δ` wherever the limiter is not negligible and only has to be finite where the limiter has zeroed the rate, so the calibrated kinetics are unchanged outside the limiter's own region. Porosity passes through a smooth floor. The temperature form needs two pieces: the heat-capacity sum is floored smoothly, and the temperature that every temperature-sensitive kernel evaluates is a smooth clamp of the trial temperature into the accepted bracket `[T_min, T_max]`, the property bracket already used by the property laws, applied also to the Arrhenius factor, the gas density and radiation, which today read raw `T` (`exp(−E/RT)` at `T = 0` has a NaN derivative and at `T < 0` is infinite). The admissibility predicate reads the unclamped temperature. The inventory form needs an extended temperature map, not a floored denominator: `T(H, m)` is the unique root of `H = Σ_j m_j⁺ h_j(T) + κ(m) · (T − T_ref)`, with `m_j⁺` a one-sided `C¹` positive part that is exactly zero at and below zero mass, exactly `m_j` at and above a scale `δ` set at the mass absolute tolerance, and a smoothstep onset between (`m_j⁺ = m_j (3t² − 2t³)`, `t = m_j/δ`, for `0 < m_j < δ`; the slopes match at both joints), and `κ` a smooth switch that is zero where the physical heat-capacity sum is above its floor and positive below it. The relation is then monotone with a root for every real `H` and has derivatives by the implicit function theorem. Its region of exact agreement with the physical relation is stated precisely: every state in which each species is either absent or at least `δ` and the heat-capacity sum is above twice its floor, so that `κ = 0`; that is every physical state the tolerance can distinguish, and the harness holds a counterexample with the capacity switch active to mark the boundary. It cannot agree everywhere, since a nonnegative differentiable positive part with `p(0) = 0` has zero slope there and so cannot equal `m` on all of `(0, ∞)`; inside the onset a species perturbs the cell's energy by at most about `δ · c_{p,j} · ΔT / 4`, below tolerance by the choice of `δ`. A rational positive part that only approaches `m` was tried and rejected: its `δ²/m` deviation on present species gave a 0.01 K error at a hundred `δ`. Cell volume is floored smoothly for `m/V`, the face metrics and the activation switch, and the global scalars of the variable-cross-section model are guarded the same way: the column mean `χ̄` that feeds the lateral law is clamped smoothly into `[0, 1]` for evaluation, since today's unguarded law returns a non-positive area on an out-of-domain trial `χ̄` (Phase 0 item 3). The admissibility predicate for `isoutofdomain` reads the unfloored quantities: negative mass, physical porosity outside `[0, 1]`, non-positive cell volume. Tests: an initially absent reactant stays at zero through a reacting step; per-species depletion at orders 0, 0.5 and 1; zero and negative trial temperatures give finite values and derivatives in every kernel; a physical state with an absent species and the others at or above `δ` reproduces the physical caloric relation exactly, and a species inside the onset within the stated energy bound; derivatives of every extension against dense AD; the depletion kinetics on the Tier 1 cases. No hard clamp remains in the residual.

### 3.3 Mesh velocity

`w = v_s + c` at every node.

- **Solid velocity `v_s`.** The per-cell volume balance of §3.2, which in planar 1D is the strain integral `v_{s,k} = Σ_{i<k} θ_i h_i` written one node at a time and in radial 1D carries the face areas. Multi-D: the balance rows plus the closure (§3.4).
- **Correction `c`.** A relaxed equidistribution (MMPDE5-type) of a monitor `M = α + β·(solid mass per length) + γ·(reaction-rate term, optional)`, with response time `τ`. At exact equidistribution every cell satisfies `S/(N M_max) ≤ h_i ≤ S/(N α)` with `S = ∫M`: the floor `α` bounds cells from above, which stops them growing without limit where there is no mass, and the monitor's maximum `M_max ≤ α + β ρ_max` bounds them from below. Both bounds scale with `S(t)`, so a vanishing layer's cells shrink with the layer; the bound is relative, never absolute. With relaxation (`τ > 0`) even that is a heuristic, and the cited nonsingularity theorem needs its own coercive functional and compatible discretization, so the minimum cell size is measured in Phase 2, not assumed. The mass term keeps nodes near-Lagrangian wherever composition varies.
- **Soft activation.** `c` is multiplied by a smooth switch in `log(h_i/h_i(0))` that is ≈0 above about 30% of the initial thickness and →1 below about 10%. No hard switch anywhere; hard switches are nondifferentiable and stiff. The thresholds sit below the layer's design shrinkage, which the material declares: a char that keeps 20% of its thickness by design must not activate the correction, so 30% and 10% are defaults, not constants.
- **Pinned and Lagrangian nodes.** Back node pinned. Surface node moves with the physical surface velocity. Layer-interface nodes Lagrangian (`c = 0`).
- **Relative fluxes.** When `c ≠ 0`, every inventory crosses faces at `(v_s − w)·n`. Species mass fluxes use a second-order reconstruction with the van Albada limiter, which is smooth; minmod and van Leer are excluded because their kinks show up in finite-difference gradients. The enthalpy carried across a face is `Σ_j F_{j,f} h_j(T_f)` with the same `F_{j,f}` as the mass rows and `T_f` the same reconstructed state the limiter used for the solid flux; energy is never reconstructed separately. An isothermal composition profile advected across the mesh then stays isothermal to round-off, which is a Phase 2 test, not a tolerance check. The pore gas is carried with the skeleton, consistent with the gas-frame derivation; the deleted gas sweep term does not return. These fluxes are a continuous Lagrange–remap: the diffusion that the rejected event-based remap pays at each event is paid here continuously, in smooth form, and only where `c ≠ 0`. That is the trade, stated plainly.
- **Discrete GCL.** `dV_i/dt = Σ_f (w·n)_f A_f` holds exactly in the semi-discrete system when face sweep rates are computed from the same node velocities that move the nodes; in planar 1D it is the identity `dh_i/dt = w_{i+1} − w_i`, and free-stream preservation (uniform ξ stays uniform under mesh motion) is exact in time as well, because `V` is linear in `z` at fixed area; with the variable-cross-section law `V = A(t) h` is not jointly linear and the exactness claim lapses. In radial and multi-D geometry `V` is nonlinear in the positions and the time integrator leaves an `O(Δt^p)` free-stream error (§1.2). That is accepted and tested as a convergence property. Carrying `V_i` as a state would make it exact at the price of a drift between `V_i` and `V(x)`, and is not done.

With `c ≡ 0` the scheme discretizes the same continuum equations as today's Lagrangian mesh, in different variables and with a conservative gas-enthalpy flux in place of `S_conv`. That is a convergence obligation, not an identity of trajectories: the centred face temperature makes the gas term today's discretization for constant flux on a uniform grid, but the variables, the error weights and the flux form differ. Charring decks must reproduce current results to tolerance, and the tolerance is the gate.

### 3.4 Multi-D solid-velocity closure

The closure is an interface with two implementations, selected per problem. Both produce nodal velocities from local algebraic unknowns of the DAE, and both reduce to the 1D rows in one dimension (the mechanics one with the uniaxial eigenstrain the 1D model implies). The interface exposes the unknowns, the operators, the boundary treatment and the rank conditions; nothing global is solved inside the residual.

**Volume-constrained closure (default).** The condition is that the solid velocity satisfies the discrete volume balance of every cell exactly, `Σ_f A_f (v_s·n)_f = (θ_i − θ_A) V_i`, because `θ` is the free density law and `ξ = m/V` feeds every property model. The balance gives one row per cell for two (2D) or three (3D) unknowns per node, so a further condition closes it:

- **Column mode.** The lateral velocity components are prescribed (zero, or a lateral strain law), leaving the per-column 1D rows. Exact by construction. This is ThermaKin2Ds' and the multi-D assessment's first step and covers laterally uniform cone and CAPA samples.
- **Potential form.** Let `D ∈ ℝ^{N_c × d·N_n}` be the discrete divergence that maps the free node velocities to the face-sweep sum of every cell, with the pinned nodes' columns removed. The closure is the velocity of minimum discrete `L²` norm that satisfies every balance: `w = W⁻¹ Dᵀ φ` with one multiplier `φ_i` per cell, `W` the diagonal of nodal lumped volumes, and `D W⁻¹ Dᵀ φ = (θ − θ_A) V + s`. The `φ_i` are the algebraic unknowns of the DAE (one per cell instead of `d` per node), and `w_n` is a local function of the multipliers of the cells sharing node *n*; both rows are local, so no global solve hides inside the residual. `D W⁻¹ Dᵀ` is a cell-to-neighbour-cell operator, tridiagonal in 1D (where the balance rows alone already fix `w`), symmetric positive definite whenever `D` has full row rank. One free face is necessary, not sufficient: the rank is computed for the actual operator and boundary conditions in Phase 0, and a fully pinned body, which would need `Σ (θ − θ_A) V = 0`, is excluded. The constrained minimum of `wᵀ W w`, the discrete `∫|v|² dV`, is the discrete form of `v = ∇φ`, `∇²φ = θ` with `∂φ/∂n = 0` on pinned faces and `φ = 0` on free faces (the surface moves along its normal, as in every 1D case): the Baines–Hubbard–Jimack construction. With the side walls constrained it is column mode. The weight is not optional. With the Euclidean norm, two unit squares side by side under uniform expansion, bottom pinned and lateral motion suppressed, get top velocities `(2/3, 4/3, 2/3)` instead of `(1, 1, 1)`, because the shared node appears in two balance rows; every balance passes and the flat top grows a peak. With the lumped-volume weights `(1/2, 1, 1/2)` the motion is uniform. Phase 0 therefore tests affine motion on connected cells, not the balances alone. Bochev, Liao and de la Peña prove their weighted version tangle-free under their boundary assumptions; that motivates the choice but does not cover shrinking boundaries with deactivation, so mesh validity (positive volumes, bounded cell Jacobians) is asserted at every accepted step in multi-D and tested through burnout.

**Mechanics closure.** Linear elasticity with the reaction strain rate as an eigenstrain (isotropic `θ/3·I`, or the anisotropic tensor for wood), a pseudo Poisson ratio, traction-free exposed surfaces, pinned back; the scalar form of PATO's pyromechanics. Its volumetric strain rate is `θ + (1 − 2ν)/E · tr σ̇`, so the volume follows the free density law only where the material is unconstrained; elsewhere density is an outcome of the mechanics, which is physically right for constrained swelling. For anisotropic materials the prescribed strain-rate tensor is not compatible in general, so no velocity field realizes it exactly and this is the only general closure. It is a different physical model from the 1D core and is labelled as such; N×1 reproduces the 1D core only under the volume-constrained closure.

The multi-D correction `c` uses the same cell operator `D W⁻¹ Dᵀ` with the MMPDE monitor as its source, the multi-D form of the 1D equidistribution in §3.3. One operator assembly, two sources.

The closure unknowns (`w` in 1D and column mode, `φ` in the potential form, nodal displacement rates in the mechanics closure) are algebraic unknowns of the DAE so that every row of the system stays local and the Jacobian pattern stays sparse. The linear solve for the closure happens inside the integrator's Newton, not as a lagged sub-step; the survey's two data points on coupling (Amar, Blackwell and Edwards; Chen and Gokcen) both favour tight coupling.

### 3.5 Burnout and deactivation

- A cell deactivates when its total solid mass falls below a fraction `ε_m` of its initial solid mass. The threshold is on mass, not thickness, because masses are the states and because with the correction active the thickness of a vanishing layer's cells is bounded relative to the layer, not absolutely. `ε_m` is set by the gate, starting at 1e-2: today's park threshold is 5% of thickness, a vanishing cell's conduction coefficient grows as `1/h²`, and the Newton has to survive the cells at the threshold.
- Deactivation is three explicit policies, and every event names the cells and nodes it changes. **Conservative transfer** (1D and independent columns): the receiver absorbs the dead cell's geometric volume together with its residual inventories, exactly the current merge (`src/Adaptivity/cell_park.jl:90`): the receiver's far face moves to the dead cell's far face, so its concentrations become volume-weighted convex mixes and its temperature follows from the merged enthalpy. Admissibility is not automatic: with temperature-dependent intrinsic densities, which the component API allows, two pore-free cells at different temperatures can merge to a negative porosity (unit cells at 300 K and 500 K with `ρ_A = 300/T`, `ρ_B = 1` and unit `c_p` give `T = 400`, `V = 2`, `φ = −1/6`). The rule is therefore that a merge never destroys pore space: the merged volume is the larger of the summed geometric volumes and the merged skeletal volume `V_s(T) = Σ_j m_j/ρ_j(T)` plus the summed pre-merge pore volumes, so the retained gas keeps at least the pore volume it had, at a finite pressure which Darcy transport then relaxes, and the created volume is exactly the skeletal thermal expansion at the merged temperature, which is the density law applied rather than an error. Two rules that look equivalent fail from admissible inputs: expanding only to `V_s` sets the porosity to zero and the gas pressure `P = nRT/(V − V_s)` to infinity (the same two cells at 5% porosity with gas give a skeletal volume of 133/60 above the geometric 2), and expanding to `V_s/(1 − φ_floor)` with the smaller pre-merge porosity does the same whenever one input is pore-free and the other holds gas. Venting the retained gas to the ambient with its enthalpy on the ledger is the documented alternative. The created volume is written to the ledger with its own budget, and the tests are gas-containing merges, a pore-free donor included, with their pressures. Moving the receiver's face by the transferred material volume instead would not preserve porosity (adding pore-free material at its intrinsic volume to a cell of porosity `φ` lowers `φ`), and preserving it would need `ΔV = ΔV_solid/(1 − φ)` per material with temperature-dependent densities; the volume merge avoids the question. **Moving-boundary collapse** (1D and independent columns only): the dead cell's nodes then coincide with the receiver's new far face, its rows are masked (zero derivative, identity row in the Jacobian), and the state size and the sparsity pattern never change. Independent columns means a non-conforming column mesh with its own nodes per column, ThermaKin2Ds' layout, coupled by lateral face fluxes; on a conforming mesh the collapse would shrink the neighbouring column's cells without transferring their inventories, so a conforming mesh gets element death only. **Fixed-grid element death** (conforming multi-D): the dead cell keeps its geometry, its rows are masked, its live faces become boundary faces carrying the surface balance, no node moves, and the residual inventory is written to the ledger as a documented loss rather than transferred, so no receiver's porosity changes. `ε_m` bounds the lost solid mass only; the dead cell's gas inventory (a full cell's pore gas on a fixed grid) and its sensible enthalpy get their own budgets, each stated relative to a positive reference scale (the sample's initial mass, and that mass times `c_p` times a reference temperature difference, since sensible enthalpy at the reference temperature is zero) and checked by the ledger. Admissibility (porosity in `[0, 1]`, positive volumes) is asserted after every event.
- Every cell below the threshold at the same step deactivates in the same callback, with or without `c`. V-BO-04's 35 events become 3, one per cascade of 20, 10 and 5 cells, with 17% fewer steps and every quantity of interest within tolerance (Phase 0 item 7); V-BO-02 does not change, because the cell under a parked top cell is never itself below threshold at the same step. The gain is real only where cascades co-qualify, and that part needs no correction.
- The correction is meant to bring all cells of a vanishing layer to the threshold together, so that the layer ends in one event and the layer-interface node below becomes the exposed surface. That does not follow from the monitor: equidistributing `α + β·(mass per length)` gives `α h_i + β m_i ≈ constant`, not equal mass fractions, and graded meshes, heterogeneous layers and non-uniform reaction all defeat synchronization. One event per layer is a hypothesis with the Phase 2 acceptance criteria; the fallback is several events per layer, each handled by the policies above. Residue-forming materials never deactivate.
- When no live receiver remains in a column (the sample is gone, or the layer below is not a receiver), the residual mass is written to the ledger as a documented loss bounded by `ε_m` of the layer's mass, and the run ends with a return code or continues on the substrate, as the case declares.
- Deactivation is supported where the dead cells form a contiguous block that touches an exposed boundary: top-down burnout, perforation, edge burn-through. A layer that vanishes beneath a surviving skin (the V-BO-05 configuration) would need the stencils to chain through dead cells, which changes the pattern, or a void model with gap conduction and radiation. The run stops with a documented return code there, as the fixed mesh does today.
- Reaction rates are multiplied by a smooth function of the cell's remaining mass fraction below a few times `ε_m`, so the approach to the threshold is not stiff.
- Multi-D: element death as above. Perforation and edge burn-through are representable at the "modest to poor surface resolution" Sandia reports for element death; that is the accepted fallback, not the primary path.

### 3.6 Kernels and geometry

- **Mesh types.** Faces carry area, unit normal, the two cell ids (0 for a boundary) and a boundary tag. Cells carry volume, face ids and node ids. Generators: 1D planar, 1D radial (cylinder, sphere), 2D structured axisymmetric (r–z) and planar, later 3D hexahedral. The existing `MeshTopology{D}` and `MeshGeometry{D}` scaffolds (`src/Geometry/topology.jl:24, 76`; `mesh_geometry.jl:32, 45`) already describe this.
- **Divergence.** Loop faces, scatter `±F_f A_f` into the two cells, divide by `V`. The shared-area assumption in `divergence_1d!` disappears with it.
- **Transport.** Conduction and Darcy–Fick fluxes written for a full symmetric tensor from the first line: the two-point normal part and the cross-diffusion part are evaluated in the same residual, so the AD Jacobian carries both and deformed cells never need a kernel change. The cross term vanishes only for a diagonal tensor on axis-aligned cells; the kernel evaluates it always, so a rotated tensor on an orthogonal grid runs through the same code.
- **Radiation.** Beer–Lambert per column with the lifted face intensities; per-face incident flux and view factor (cone `q(r)`). P1 radiation, if kept, is an algebraic block `G_i` with Marshak boundary rows, which this design accommodates without a separate solver.
- **Boundary conditions.** Consumers iterate over tags; one surface Newton per boundary face; blowing and absorptivity are per-face scalars as now. Axis tag for r–z; side-wall tags for holders.
- **Properties.** `k` and `κ` per component as symmetric tensors in a material reference frame. Each cell carries its reference orientation, which is constant, and a deformation gradient `F` with `dF/dt = (∇v_s) F` along the solid motion; the physical tensor is built from the convected material axes: the material declares a priority order for its axes (wood: longitudinal grain, then radial, then tangential); the primary axis convects exactly as `F a_1` normalized, the second is `F a_2` projected orthogonal to it, the third is their cross product, and the principal values are kept, so `k = Σ_i k_i ê_i ê_iᵀ`. The priority is needed because an orthogonal frame cannot preserve two convected directions at once: under `F = diag(2, 1)` initially perpendicular 45° and 135° directions become non-orthogonal. This is the constitutive choice that fibres follow material lines, since char conductivity is set by fibre direction, not by stretch. The polar rotation of `F` is not the same thing: under `F = diag(2, 1)` the rotation is the identity while a 45° fibre turns to `atan(1/2)`. The Piola push-forward `J⁻¹ F k_ref Fᵀ`, which also scales the values, is the documented alternative. Stretch off the principal axes rotates fibres even under the irrotational closure. Nothing is read from the cell's shape, because mesh deformation is not material deformation once `w ≠ v_s`. The face flux contracts the physical tensor with the face normal. Tests: a rigid 90° rotation of a `diag(4, 1)` material gives `diag(1, 4)`; a 45° fibre under `diag(2, 1)` stretch ends at `atan(1/2)` with unchanged principal values; a perpendicular 45°/135° pair under the same stretch keeps the primary direction exactly and the transverse one by the declared projection; a stationary anisotropic material under pure mesh redistribution keeps its fluxes to round-off.
- **History fields.** Every solid-carried field names its reference frame, its carrier and its evolution: `χ` as the extensive pair of §3.2; `F` per cell with its velocity-gradient source; orientation as a reference-frame constant. Intensive fields `a` are carried as the packed inventory `Q = m_s a` with the solid mass flux and divided back, and the packed row carries the carrier's reaction source `a · (dm_s/dt)_rxn`, since `dQ/dt = a dm_s/dt + m_s da/dt` and a constant field must stay constant while the carrier reacts away (without the source it doubles when half the carrier is consumed). `F` adds its velocity-gradient source `m_s (∇v_s) F`. The mass-flux machinery then transports every field, mass-weighted averages are conserved, and with `c = 0` nothing moves relative to the cells. Tests: history transport under `c ≠ 0` with reactions off, and a constant field under reaction with transport off.

### 3.7 Jacobian

- Sparsity pattern built from the mesh connectivity (cell, face and node adjacency, the boundary rows, the global-scalar rows), computed once per problem structure and memoized; the masked-cell rows are part of the pattern from the start. A small-size test checks that the hand pattern contains the dense-AD pattern for every kernel family and every physics option, so an upwinding switch or a new term cannot drop an entry unnoticed. Tracing is not used (§1.2).
- Column coloring by SparseMatrixColorings; colored ForwardDiff passes through DifferentiationInterface; a `jac!` closure and `jac_prototype` handed to `ODEFunction`. The known dense rows (variable-cross-section means) are added after the colored pass.
- The expected color count is the stencil width times the variables per cell (masses, energy, intensity, and the node position and velocity that cell shares): measured in 1D on a model verified by containment against the production residual (Phase 0 item 4) as `3NC + 9 + 2·bl + 7·χ` with `c = 0` and `5NC + 13 + 2·bl + 11·χ` with the five-cell species reconstruction active, where `bl` and `χ` flag lifted intensities and the variable cross-section: 15 to 24 for two components and 20 for V-BO-01's three with `c = 0`, 23 to 36 and 30 with the correction active, 24 to 51 for five components, constant in the cell count. A first model that gave cell rows only their own two nodes undercounted by two to twelve: conduction reads the neighbouring cell centres and the neighbours' volumes, so a cell row reaches nodes `i−1` to `i+2`, and χ is two inventories. In 2D and 3D the analogous counts are of order `5·(NC+5)` and `7·(NC+6)` before the second-neighbour terms below. Each color is one dual partial, measured at 0.36 to 0.76 residual-equivalents (Phase 0 item 5), so a width-16 pass costs about nine residuals and a 1D Jacobian one to two passes. In multi-D the dependency graph is wider than the face stencil: second-order reconstruction, the cross-diffusion terms and shared nodes add second neighbours, so these counts are lower bounds; the hand-built pattern includes those terms and the color count is measured, not assumed. Sparse assembly is not solution cost: factorization fill, memory and Newton iterations are measured at matched accuracy at the Phase 1 and Phase 4 gates.
- Dense ForwardDiff on small problems is the oracle, verified once per kernel family; the per-entry 1e-12 comparison suite goes.
- Phase 0 measured the per-color overhead on the current residual and the lifted color count from the pattern; their product, which uses the current residual's dual cost because the lifted residual does not yet exist, puts the colored-AD Jacobian with `c = 0` at 0.9 to 1.24× the structured assembly on Eulerian decks, 1.1 to 1.3× the ALE reduced assembly and 0.02 to 0.09× the ALE exact assembly (a fifteenth at 100 cells, a fiftieth at 400), and with the correction active at 1.25 to 1.8× the Eulerian and ALE reduced assemblies. A real colored Jacobian on the current Eulerian pattern matched the structured one to round-off at 0.8 to 1.04× its cost. Affordability is therefore provisional: exactness at about the reduced assembly's price on the proxy, confirmed or refuted at the Phase 1 gate by timing the lifted assembly itself, which has more rows per cell and extra global rows. If that gate fails, the hot operators (conduction, gas transport) keep analytical kernels as an optional fast path verified against the AD path. The AD path remains the definition of correctness.

### 3.8 Time integration and linear solve

- `ODEFunction` with a diagonal mass matrix (ones on differential rows, zeros on algebraic rows), `jac!`, `jac_prototype`, colorvec unused (we color ourselves). `KenCarp4` stays the provisional default, with the raw error estimate. The smoothed estimate was measured on the Phase 0 ladder and fails both ways: on the reduced (eliminated) form it under-estimates the stiff error and accepts too few steps, missing the 1e-5 accuracy budget at tol 1e-6 on every size (error 7.6e-3 at 8 cells) where the raw estimate passes; on the lifted form it is over-conservative, spending about 1900 RHS calls at 96 cells where the raw estimate spends 1460. Every one of those runs returned a success code, so completion is not evidence of accuracy. The algebraic rows stay in the error norm: the nonlinear solver's convergence test uses the same `internalnorm`, and a mask lets the Newton stop with the constraint unconverged (§1.2). Time-step error control, nonlinear convergence and constraint accuracy are three separate checks: the step-update callback evaluates the algebraic rows at the accepted state and rejects the step, or stops with a return code, when the constraint residual, scaled by its own equation's scale (a volume rate `|θ| V_i + Σ_f A_f |w_f|` for a balance row, the incident intensity for a radiation row, `A₀` and the strain scale for the cross-section rows), exceeds `rtol`; the state's tolerance does not apply, since a balance residual has units of volume per time and the velocity has length per time. `initializealg = NoInit()`: the project runs its own consistent sweep (face intensities by the recurrence, nodal velocities by one solve with the closure) at `t₀` and inside every callback that modifies `u`, since the stepper re-enters initialization there, and verifies the constraint residual after each sweep. `FBDF` and `Rodas5P` are the alternates, and on the lifted ladder with analytical Jacobians `FBDF` leads work-precision (96 cells, tol 1e-8: 22 ms and 6.7e-9 error, against 89 ms and 5.7e-9 for the raw `KenCarp4` and 43 ms and 1.9e-9 for `Rodas5P`). The event-dense re-run (108 configurations, 100 events, production tolerances `rtol 1e-6`, `abstol 1e-8`, colored AD) reversed that ranking and showed that the restart policy after an event is part of the integrator contract. `Rodas5P` computes a Jacobian every step (831 in that run), but its cost is colors times steps: the lifted ladder colors in 4, so a Jacobian costs about 12 RHS evaluations and `Rodas5P` wins (183 ms, error 3.4e-8, qualified under both restart policies). On the production lifted pattern the measured count is 15 to 36 (Phase 0 item 4; 24 to 51 for five components), which on the current-residual proxy multiplies its per-step Jacobian cost by 2.2 to 4.8 relative to the 4-color ladder; the eliminated residual would need 96 colors, which is why the cost argument depends on the formulation. On today's decks with the reduced, inexact Jacobian `Rodas5P` takes 3 to 127 times `KenCarp4`'s accepted steps depending on the deck (Phase 0 item 2), so it is a candidate only with the exact AD Jacobian the lifted form provides. A multistep method resets to order one at every event, and `FBDF` with the solver-chosen restart step misses the error budget while returning success (2.2e-5 against 1e-5, 305 ms); with a forced small restart step it qualifies (1.1e-7, 296 ms). Raw `KenCarp4` qualifies with the solver-chosen step (207 ms, 8.1e-7) and misses the constraint budget with the forced step. Each candidate therefore carries its own restart policy, the gate measures achieved error and constraint residual under that policy, and `KenCarp4` with the raw estimate, the per-step flag and the solver-chosen restart is the default, with `Rodas5P` the favourite and `FBDF` as an observer the fastest at tight tolerance on the finite slab; the choice is made on the new core's residual in Phase 1.
- `KLU` default; UMFPACK where the pivot-growth failure on the Eulerian boundary-layer cases recurs. 3D needs a Krylov method with a block (field-split) preconditioner over the physics blocks (inventories, energy, geometry, algebraic); ILU has already failed on this system and is not the plan. The state layout keeps the block information so that preconditioner can be built without touching the kernels; it is the open item of Phase 4d.
- The erased RHS and Jacobian types, wrapped callbacks, progress reporting, parameter sweeps and precompile workloads transfer.
- Per-step callbacks are read-only observers, and the flag policy is part of each integrator's contract. Today's residual already reads the state from `u` (`src/Residual/residual.jl:54`); the step-update callback mirrors `u` into the mesh for the outputs. The derivative-discontinuity flag it carries is not something it adds: DiffEqBase raises the flag before every `DiscreteCallback`'s `affect!`, so any per-step callback leaves it set unless it clears it explicitly (`derivative_discontinuity!(integrator, false)`; `u_modified!` is the deprecated alias). Measured on the 320-cell finite slab (Phase 0 item 6): with the flag `KenCarp4` re-evaluates the derivative at the accepted state instead of reusing the Newton-stage derivative, refreshes its Jacobian, and holds its accuracy; cleared, its L2 error grows 51.6× and its steps 2.04× at the case's tolerance, 44× and 2.59× with the raw estimate, which is the docstring's 15× effect (`src/Solver/callbacks.jl:515`) at a different tolerance. `FBDF` with the flag restarts at order one every step and never grows its step, stalling at 3 of 1,200 s after 20,000 steps; cleared, it finishes in 148 to 301 steps with one Jacobian and reaches at `rtol 1e-6` the error `KenCarp4` needs `rtol 1e-8` for. `Rodas5P` is bit-identical either way, since it rebuilds its Jacobian every step. Observers in the new core (constraint checks, diagnostics, output mirrors) therefore clear the flag when the integrator is multistep and leave it set for `KenCarp4`; the deactivation callback modifies the state, runs the consistent sweep after the transfer, and legitimately restarts the integrator's history. Reproducing the finite-slab accuracy with the chosen integrator and its flag policy on the new core is a Phase 1 gate.
- **Solve segments.** Fixed topology and one sparsity pattern hold within a solve segment. A topology change the policies of §3.5 do not mask (refinement, a void, a split sample) ends the segment: the state is transferred conservatively and a new integrator starts on a new pattern. The framework therefore does not forbid such changes; the first release does not implement them.

### 3.9 Outputs and diagnostics

Per-face surface temperature and fluxes with area-weighted totals; mass-loss rate integrated over the exposed surface; thickness history and node positions read from the state; concentrations and temperatures reconstructed per cell for plots and for the verification harness's quantities of interest. Ledgers read inventories directly. In the inventory form they are exact tests; in the temperature form, the default, they are convergence checks at the integrator's order, as today, and the Phase 1 gate bounds them.

### 3.10 Excluded by design

Fixed Eulerian grids and diffuse or sharp fixed-grid interfaces for the core (reserved for a future topology-change fallback), Lagrange-remap and renoding, Landau grids, h-adaptivity and variable state size within a solve segment, particle methods, an inert thickness floor, interior voids (a layer vanishing beneath a surviving skin), hard clamps and non-smooth limiters anywhere in the residual, sparsity tracing, deprecation shims and dual code paths during the transition.

## 4. Decisions and rationale

| Decision | Alternatives considered | Why this one |
|---|---|---|
| Body-fitted ALE in physical space | Fixed grid with volume-of-solid; material coordinates on a fixed reference mesh | Surface balance, view factors and flame feedback need the physical surface; multi-D smoothing exists only in physical space; the 1D material-coordinate form is this scheme written in thicknesses, which is local only for planar geometry (§3.2) |

## 5. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| The correction fails to carry a layer through burnout, does not synchronize the layer's cells (the monitor equalizes `α h + β m`, not mass fractions), or needs problem-dependent tuning of `τ` and the monitor (Furzeland's warning) | Prototype gate on V-BO-01 to V-BO-05 before cutover; the fallback is `c = 0` plus deactivation, already in the same code |
| Composition smearing in a vanishing layer degrades peak MLR timing | Gate on peak MLR and timing; mass term in the monitor keeps nodes Lagrangian where composition varies; limiter order |
| Enthalpy inversion or inventory tolerances cause step-size trouble | Absolute tolerances bounded by `rtol · ε_m` times a positive cell scale (total initial solid mass), never a per-species or reference-state inventory; the full `δT` formula of §3.2 sets the bound; the inventory variant is a two-function change by construction, and the Phase 1 gate runs it |
| Algebraic rows upset the SDIRK error estimate, or a masked norm hides an unconverged constraint | Algebraic rows stay in the norm; the raw estimate is the default, since the smoothed one misses the accuracy budget on the reduced form and over-spends on the lifted form (§3.8); constraint residuals are checked explicitly after every accepted step and every restart; `NoInit` plus the project's sweep at every event; the Phase 0 ladder measured `KenCarp4`, `FBDF`, `Rodas5P` and a bordered solve, and the event-density and colored-AD runs decide |
| An integrator chosen on the smooth ladder loses on event-dense decks, or its restart policy hides a miss behind a success code | Event-dense ladder run (100 events): the restart policy is part of the contract and gated on achieved error and constraint residual (`FBDF` needs a forced small restart step, `KenCarp4` the solver-chosen one, `Rodas5P` qualifies under both); the production color count is 15 to 36, so `Rodas5P` stays in contention with the exact Jacobian at 2.2 to 4.8× the ladder's per-step Jacobian cost; the 320-cell finite slab is measured (§3.8): `KenCarp4` needs the per-step flag, `FBDF` needs it cleared, `Rodas5P` is indifferent, and reproducing that accuracy on the new core is a Phase 1 gate |
| Colored AD assembly slower than the hand-derived operators | Phase 0 measured 0.32 to 0.73 residual-equivalents per color and 15 to 36 lifted colors on a containment-verified model, putting the colored Jacobian at 1.1 to 1.8× today's reduced assembly on the current-residual proxy; the Phase 1 gate confirms on the new core's residual; analytical fast path for hot operators verified against AD if needed |
| The hand-built pattern misses an entry | Containment test against the dense-AD pattern at small size, per kernel family and per physics option |
| `ε_m` small enough for one event per layer is too small for the Newton (today's threshold is 5% of thickness; conduction coefficients grow as `1/h²`) | Start at 1e-2 and tighten under the gate; the correction is meant to bound cells relative to the layer, and the number of events per layer is the Phase 2 hypothesis, measured against `ε_m` |
| The potential closure's free-surface condition is wrong for a case (oblique recession, strongly curved surface) | `φ = 0` on exposed faces moves the surface along its normal, which is what every 1D case does; column mode is the fallback; the mechanics closure is the second implementation of the same interface |
| The potential closure's discrete operators are incompatible and the cell volume balance is not met | `w = W⁻¹ Dᵀ φ` with `D` built from the same face sweep rates that move the nodes, so the balance holds to the Newton tolerance by construction; asserted on a moving quadrilateral and hexahedron in Phase 0 before any 2D kernel is written |
| A deactivation transfer drives the receiver's porosity negative, or a collapse invalidates a neighbour | Volume merge into the receiver (convex mixes); collapse only in 1D and independent columns; element death with a ledgered residual on conforming meshes; admissibility asserted after every event |
| A trial state outside the domain produces a non-finite residual or derivative | The trial-state contract of §3.2: one-sided depletion limiter, finite power base, smooth floors on porosity, volume and the heat-capacity sum, the regularized temperature map for the inventory form, an unfloored admissibility predicate; derivatives checked against dense AD |
| The vector field points out of the non-negative domain (an absent reactant is consumed) | Multiplicative one-sided limiter per reactant, zero at zero concentration for every order; absent-reactant and per-species depletion tests in Phase 0 |
| The closure's norm or rank is wrong for the actual operators | Lumped-volume weights; affine-motion tests on connected cells; rank computed for the actual operators and boundary conditions in Phase 0 |
| Rigid rotation or history transport handled wrongly | Reference orientation plus carried `F`; extensive pairs for histories; the rotation and transport tests of §3.6 in Phase 1 |
| Event ordering changes under a parameter perturbation, so finite-difference gradients are not smooth even with smooth coefficients | Measured in Phase 0 item 1: integrated quantities (peak mass-loss rate) differentiate smoothly through 60 parks, event-timed quantities do not, and park ordering changes in seven of eight neighbouring perturbations; re-measured in Phase 2 item 4; where it persists, inverse analysis differences across a bracket that holds the event order fixed, documented in the user guide |
| 3D has no proven preconditioner for the coupled system | Block information kept in the state layout; the preconditioner is Phase 4d's deliverable, not assumed |
| Verification tolerances do not transfer because variables changed | Compare quantities of interest, not states; ledgers become exact; MMS with material-frame sources ported first |
| Scope creep into 2D before 1D is cut over | Phase gates below; 2D validation starts after the old core is deleted, and the Phase 3 demonstrations are the one exception, with quantitative criteria |

## 6. Implementation plan

### Phase 0: Evidence and contracts (three to four weeks)

1. Finite-difference gradient smoothness of the V-BO-01 quantities of interest as park times shift with a parameter. Done (`baselines/fd_gradients.jl`, `results/fd_gradients.md`): nine runs at 182 cells with the PMMA absorption coefficient perturbed by up to ±0.8%, all parking 60 cells. The integrated quantity is smooth: central differences of the peak mass-loss rate agree to 2.5% across four step sizes, 0.4% with parabolic interpolation of the peak. Event-timed quantities are not: the grid-quantized peak and burnout times give derivatives of zero or multiples of the output spacing, the interpolated ones spread 11% to 20%, the ledger and the first park time are erratic, and the park ordering changes between neighbouring perturbations in seven of eight pairs while the park count never does. Within that window (one parameter, this deck, the case's tolerance, relative steps of 1e-3 to 8e-3) the integrated quantity differentiates cleanly; at smaller steps (1e-4 to 1e-6) the spread grows to 8% to 11% as solver-tolerance noise is amplified by `1/h`, which is generic to finite differences and not an event effect. The experiment establishes neither general gradient accuracy nor the bracketed differencing of §5; it bounds what inverse analysis can expect from this quantity today.
2. Newton iterations per stage, exact versus reduced Jacobian, on the audit decks. The performance plan found no case where the reduced Newton stalls; this is the measurement that says whether the exact Jacobian buys steps or only simplicity. Done from the audit's measurements (`baselines/newton_baseline.py`, `results/newton_baseline.md`): the exact Jacobian gives no systematic reduction in Newton iterations per accepted step (27 to 32 for both, summed over `KenCarp4`'s five implicit stages and including retries), cuts nonlinear failures in four of eight ALE pairs and raises them in one, at six to ten times the reduced run's whole-solve wall time at 100 cells. The exact Jacobian's case is therefore locality, the Rosenbrock option and the end of the strategy split, not Newton counts. On the same decks `Rodas5P` with the reduced, inexact Jacobian takes 3 to 3.5× `KenCarp4`'s accepted steps on the Eulerian decks, 16 to 22× on plain ALE and 120 to 127× on ALE with Darcy transport, which is what a Rosenbrock method does with an inexact Jacobian and what the lifted AD Jacobian removes. The audit's `FBDF` rows were suspected of having run with the per-step flag set; the observer re-run (`baselines/integrator_observer_audit.jl`, `results/integrator_observer_audit.md`) reproduces their step counts to within 0.3%, so those rows correspond to the observer configuration and the ranking stands: on the six 80 s decks `FBDF` as an observer is fastest on five (mean rank 1.17) and best on error on four, `KenCarp4` is second by time, and `Rodas5P` fourth by time with the reduced Jacobian; `FBDF` with the flag set never finishes (200,000 steps by 3 to 7 s of 80). The error ranking on the PMMA decks is unresolved, because two tight references differ by 0.03 to 0.10 K, the size of the candidate errors; on the Darcy deck it is resolved, and there `Rodas5P` and the raw `KenCarp4` are best and `FBDF` worst.
3. A 20 to 50× intumescent grid ladder on the existing code, with graded initial meshes. Done (`baselines/intumescent_ladder.jl`, `results/intumescent_ladder.md`): the robustness coating deck at expansion factors 3.6×, 20× and 50× on uniform meshes of 25 to 400 cells and stretched meshes. Convergence is reported per quantity and factor (`results/intumescent_ladder.md`, ladders to 400 cells at 3.6× and 800 at 20× and 50×, floors 0.01 K, 1 µm, 0.01 g/m²/s). At 3.6× every temperature and the thickness are at their floor by 100 cells. At 20× the later surface temperatures are at the floor from 50 cells, the 100 s surface temperature from 200, the 900 s back-face temperature only from 400 (its 200→400 difference was still 0.014 K), and the final thickness from 25. At 50× the temperatures are at the floor from 50 to 100 cells, but the 900 s thickness only from 400 (differences of 6.5, 1.5 and 0.4 µm at 100→200, 200→400 and 400→800), because a quarter of the coating is unconverted at 900 s; run to full conversion (1,927 to 1,929 s) the thickness agrees to 0.4 µm between 100 and 200 cells. Peak mass-loss rate, an early transient on a 4.5 s output grid, is still moving at 800 cells at 20× and 50×. Grading toward the surface is closer to the finest uniform result in 18 of 20 comparisons. The failure is a current-core defect of the trial-state kind: a 1.10-stretched 100-cell mesh (smallest cell 22 nm) initializes with unit area, zero χ and finite residuals, then the first step's Newton produces an enormous negative trial `χ̄` and the lateral shrinkage law returns a non-positive cross-section before any step is accepted. No resolution crisis appears at 20 to 50× for the temperature and thickness quantities once the ladders are long enough and the horizon covers conversion, and the free mitigation, graded initial meshes, works.
4. Color counts of the current `:full` sparsity pattern versus the lifted (node-velocity, face-intensity, algebraic-scalar) pattern, in planar and radial geometry, computed from patterns alone. This settles the lifting decision before any kernel is written. Done (`baselines/jacobian_colors.jl`, `baselines/lifted_containment.jl`, `results/jacobian_colors.csv`, `results/lifted_containment.csv`, `results/jacobian_summary.md`; greedy coloring equal to the row-degree lower bound in every row, so every count is optimal): the current ALE `:full` pattern colors in the state length (101, 401 and 1,601 at 25, 100 and 400 cells) because the top node's velocity row reads every column, while the lifted pattern colors in 15 (surface absorption, two components), 17 (Beer–Lambert), 24 (with χ as the two inventories) and 20 (V-BO-01, three components) with `c = 0`, and 23, 25, 36 and 30 with the five-cell species reconstruction active, constant in the cell count; radial has the same locality as planar. The current Eulerian `:full` and ALE `:ale_reduced` patterns color in 9 and 12. The lifted count is `3NC + 9 + 2·bl + 7·χ` with `c = 0` and `5NC + 13 + 2·bl + 11·χ` with the correction active; §3.7 is corrected to that. The model is a dependency graph, not code, so it was checked by containment: dense ForwardDiff of the production residual on 6- and 8-cell reacting, non-uniform ALE, Beer–Lambert and χ decks shows cell rows reaching cells `i−1..i+1` and nodes `i−1..i+2`, every measured set inside the model's with nothing added. A first model that gave cell rows only their own two nodes failed that check (the adversarial review's finding), and its 13 to 28 colors are withdrawn. One fact fell out: the production cell rows never read the node velocity, so the prefix sum enters only the position rows.
5. Colored AD on the current residual: SparseMatrixColorings and DifferentiationInterface are installed, so color the current `:full` pattern and measure the overhead of one dual pass per color relative to a plain residual evaluation, against the structured backend's cost, on the audit decks. The per-color overhead transfers approximately; the color count comes from item 4; the product is the estimate. This is the first half of the operator-layer decision; the Phase 1 gate on the new core is the second. Done (`baselines/ad_overhead.jl`, `results/ad_overhead.csv`, `results/jacobian_summary.md`): one dual partial costs 0.32 to 0.73 residual-equivalents, lowest at chunk widths 16 to 24, so a width-16 pass costs about nine residuals. The projection multiplies the current residual's dual cost by the number of width-16 passes the repaired color count needs; the lifted residual itself, with more rows per cell and extra global rows, was not timed because it does not exist yet. With `c = 0` the projected colored Jacobian is 0.9 to 1.24× the structured assembly on Eulerian decks, 1.1 to 1.3× the ALE reduced assembly and 0.02 to 0.09× the ALE exact assembly (a fifteenth at 100 cells, a fiftieth at 400, where the geometry block costs 26 to 35 ms); with the correction active it is 1.25 to 1.8× the Eulerian and ALE reduced assemblies. A real colored Jacobian on the current Eulerian pattern runs at 0.8 to 1.04× the structured exact and agrees with it to round-off. The first half of the decision is therefore provisional, not positive: exactness at about the reduced assembly's price on the proxy, to be confirmed by the lifted assembly's own timing and matched-accuracy solves at the Phase 1 gate.
6. A toy index-1 DAE (a stiff diffusion ladder with a lifted recurrence and a state-jump callback) through `KenCarp4`, `FBDF` and `Rodas5P`, with the full norm and, to demonstrate the failure, a masked norm; constraint residuals and consistent restarts checked explicitly; the same problem through a bordered solve. Done in the harness (`scripts/mesh_phase0/test_solvers.jl`, results in `results/solver_comparison.csv`): the masked norm fails, lifting and bordering both have qualified configurations, the smoothed estimate fails the accuracy budget on the reduced form, and `FBDF` leads work-precision on the lifted form with analytical Jacobians. The event-dense re-run is also done (`results/restart_comparison.csv`, 108 configurations, 92 qualified, with colored AD): at 100 events and production tolerances `Rodas5P` leads and qualifies under both restart policies, raw `KenCarp4` qualifies with the solver-chosen restart, `FBDF` qualifies only with a forced small restart step, and the lifted ladder colors in 4. Both remaining measurements are done. The production lifted color count is 15 to 36 (item 4), so on the current-residual proxy a production `Rodas5P` Jacobian costs 2.2 to 4.8 times the ladder's (one to three width-16 passes against one width-4 pass). The finite-slab case (`baselines/integrator_flag.jl`, `results/integrator_flag_summary.md`; V-HC-03 and V-HC-02c at 320 cells, three callback variants, two tolerances) shows that the per-step flag is set by default for every `DiscreteCallback`, since DiffEqBase raises it before each `affect!`, so only an observer that clears it explicitly differs from today. With the flag, `KenCarp4` holds its accuracy; as an observer its L2 error grows 51.6× and its steps 2.04× at the case's tolerance (44× and 2.59× with the raw estimate), which reproduces the docstring's effect. `FBDF` with the flag restarts at order one every step and never grows its step, stalling at 3 of 1,200 s after 20,000 steps; as an observer it finishes in 148 to 301 steps with one Jacobian and reaches at `rtol 1e-6` the error `KenCarp4` needs `rtol 1e-8` for, at 0.5 to 1.0× the time. `Rodas5P` is bit-identical under all three variants and sits at the spatial floor at both tolerances, at 1.8× the time at the loose tolerance and 0.9× at the tight one. The per-step flag policy therefore joins the restart policy in each integrator's contract.
7. On the current code, park every cell below threshold in one callback and re-run V-BO-04 and V-BO-02. This measures the single-event gain available without the correction. Done (`baselines/one_callback_parking.jl`, `results/one_callback_parking.csv`): every quantity of interest of V-BO-04, V-BO-02 and V-BO-01 passes under the one-callback variant. V-BO-04's 35 invocations become 3, one per cascade of 20, 10 and 5 cells, with 17% fewer steps; V-BO-02 is unchanged, because the cell under a parked top cell is never itself below threshold at the same step; V-BO-01 parks 60 cells in 55 invocations with peak, timing and burnout values within tolerance (peak 32.39206 to 32.39207) and a ledger closure of 6.0e-4 against 4.8e-4 (tolerance 1e-3); that ledger is a trapezoidal diagnostic on the output grid, and halving the output spacing moves both closures to 7.5e-4 and 7.9e-4 while the final mass agrees to 2e-10, so the difference is sampling, not a transfer error. The gain is real only where cascades co-qualify. The loop re-tests the event predicate before each further park, which the review showed is necessary: a loop that re-called the depletion routine unconditionally hit the minimum-cell stop at the first cascade and terminated V-BO-04 with `min_cells = 20` at 300 s instead of at the second crossing; with the predicate, both variants terminate on their first accepted step after the exact second crossing at 368.89 s (370.33 s and 368.98 s), the gap being detection delay on different step sequences.
8. Moving-geometry probes: a uniform concentration on a shrinking sphere, cylinder, quadrilateral and hexahedron under the chosen integrator, measuring the free-stream error order, and a prototype `D` and `W` with `w = W⁻¹ Dᵀ φ` asserting the per-cell volume balance and uniform motion under uniform expansion, including the `θ_A` term. This fixes the fully discrete geometry treatment before Phase 1 step 1.
9. Isothermal composition advection on a 1D mesh with `c ≠ 0` at prototype level: species by van Albada, enthalpy with the same flux; the temperature must stay constant to round-off. This fixes the flux compatibility rule before the kernels are written.
10. Contracts. The three contracts that shape the state layout and the kernel interfaces are written as interfaces before Phase 1, and their tests pass: the closure (unknowns, operators, boundary treatment, rank conditions, §3.4), the material-field evolution (reference frame, carrier, transport, sources, §3.6) and the event transfer (cells and nodes changed, inventory consistency, admissibility, §3.5). The tests are the counterexamples by name: two unit squares under uniform expansion move uniformly, and the rank of the actual closure operator is computed under each boundary set (closure); an initially absent reactant stays at zero through a reacting step, at orders 0, 0.5 and 1 (depletion); zero and negative trial temperatures give finite values and derivatives in every kernel, the inventory-form temperature map returns finite values and derivatives at negative trial masses, and a physical state with an absent species reproduces the physical caloric relation exactly (trial states); two cells at different temperatures with a temperature-dependent density merge to an admissible state with and without retained gas, a pore-free donor included, and the merged pressure is finite (transfer); a constant packed field under reaction with transport off stays constant, a 45° fibre under `diag(2, 1)` stretch ends at `atan(1/2)`, and a perpendicular 45°/135° pair follows the declared axis priority (material fields).

Gate: passed after repair. Items 6, 8 and 9 pass (constraint residuals at tolerance through a state jump; per-cell volume balance to round-off on every geometry; isothermal advection exact), item 10's contract tests pass (1,561 contract assertions plus 2,905 solver and caloric assertions in `scripts/mesh_phase0/`), and items 1 to 5 and 7 are measured and recorded under `scripts/mesh_phase0/results/`. The adversarial review of the first version of those measurements reproduced every count and error but found the lifted sparsity model incomplete and the intumescent convergence summary contradicted by its own data; the four obligations it set are met: the sparsity model repaired and verified by containment against the executable residual, with color counts and cost projections regenerated (items 4 and 5); the 20× and 50× ladders extended to 800 cells and to full conversion and reported per quantity (item 3); the parking loop re-testing the event predicate before each further park, with the `min_cells` boundary case (item 7); and the audit decks re-run as an observer, which validated the earlier `FBDF` rows (item 2). What Phase 0 cannot settle, and Phase 1 must: the integrator with its two policies on the new core's own residual, the colored-AD cost on the lifted residual (projected from the current residual, not timed), and the finite-slab accuracy reproduced by whichever integrator is chosen.

## 7. Open questions for the owner

1. **Energy state.** Temperature (default) or the enthalpy inventory. The state mapping makes the switch a two-function change, so this is a default to pick rather than a one-way door; the Phase 1 gate runs both, and the inventory form is the one the relative-flux work of Phase 2 uses.
2. **Which closure for which case.** The volume-constrained closure is the default; the mechanics closure is selected per problem for anisotropic or constrained swelling. The owner names the first validation case for each.
3. **Integrator and lifting.** Both lifting and bordering have qualified configurations on the ladder, so lifting stays the default for its AD locality. The integrator with its restart and per-step-flag policies is decided on the new core's residual in Phase 1: `Rodas5P` leads the event-dense ladder and is indifferent to the flag but needs the exact Jacobian; `FBDF` as an observer is the fastest at tight tolerance on the finite slab and on five of the six 80 s audit decks, and needs a forced restart step at events; `KenCarp4` with the raw estimate, the flag and the solver-chosen restart is the default until then.
4. **Variable cross-section.** Keep the 1D scalar law as an algebraic block through cutover, or retire it with 2D column mode.
5. **P1 radiation.** Port as an algebraic block in Phase 3, or delete and re-add when a case needs it.
6. **Cone geometry.** r–z on an equal-area disk, or 3D quarter-square, for the MaCFP and CAPA validation targets. Determines whether Phase 4c or 4d is the validation milestone.
7. **Structured only.** The plan assumes structured I×J[×K] grids. The face-based mesh types are unstructured-capable; what is structured-specific is column mode, the per-direction limiter and the banded orderings. Unstructured buys holder geometry and costs those three.
8. **Interior voids.** Stop with a return code when a layer vanishes beneath a surviving skin, as now, or schedule a void model (gap conduction and radiation) after 2D.
9. **Thresholds as material parameters.** `ε_m` and the activation thresholds of §3.3 as per-material parameters with package defaults, or package constants.
10. **Constraint tolerance.** The constraint residual accepted at an accepted step; the proposal is `rtol` on each residual scaled by its equation's own scale (§3.8), since the state's tolerance has the wrong units.
11. **Independent or conforming columns for 2D.** Independent columns (ThermaKin2Ds' layout) keep collapse admissible and cost a non-conforming lateral flux rule; a conforming mesh keeps the standard kernels and gets element death only.
