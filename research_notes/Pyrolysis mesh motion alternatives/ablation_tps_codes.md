# Mesh motion, surface recession and mesh management in ablation / TPS material-response codes

Scope note for the report writer. The user listed "ICARUS" with a pointer to Schrooyen. These are two different codes. **Icarus** is the NASA Ames 3D unstructured finite-volume material-response code (Schulz, Stern, Palmer, Muppidi, Schroeder). Schrooyen's unified flow/porous-material DG solver lives in **Argo** (Cenaero/UCLouvain). Both are covered below. Source access: most AIAA/JTHT papers were paywalled. Facts below come from NTRS copies, author preprints, theses and workshop abstracts that I opened (noted where only an abstract or search snippet was seen).

## Q1. Which codes use which mesh/coordinate strategy (fixed grid, Landau/front-fixing, ALE with mesh deformation, node dropping, surface-attached coordinates), and how is the GCL handled?

### Takeaway
Across 60 years the community has used five families. (1) Surface-attached translating grids with node dropping (CMA). (2) Uniformly contracting "linear-elastic" or Landau front-fixing 1D grids (SODDIT, FIAT, Blackwell/Hogan, Amar, MOPAR). (3) Fixed grids with cell splitting or deactivation at the front (ITRAC), plus element cutting with near-surface remeshing (Hero). (4) Multi-D ALE, where the boundary is driven by recession and interior nodes are redistributed by a pseudo-elastic, Laplacian, spring-analogy or RBF solve (CHAR, Sandia Coyote/new FEM, PATO, KATS, Icarus, Dec & Braun, ABAQUS-ALE, CHyPS), or by structured "recession paths" (TITAN, 3dFIAT). (5) Fixed-mesh volume-averaged, level-set or element-death methods (Argo/Schrooyen, Beauvois, Sandia element death). In every production code I found, mesh nodes are surface-driven and arbitrary, not attached to solid mass. CHAR says so explicitly. No code was found that integrates node positions as error-controlled ODE states the way Pyrolysis.jl does. Explicit GCL treatment is rarely documented; CHyPS's "conservative direct ALE" is the one explicit case found.

### Cited Findings

**Taxonomy statements from code developers**
- In the CHAR team's survey, CMA "implemented a translating and node-dropping scheme... the first N − 1 mesh elements translated, without volume reduction, at the surface recession rate. The last, Nth, element contracts until reaching a critical minimum volume, at which time it is eliminated from the domain." The survey adds: "Since the development of CMA, the more common approach has been to treat the one-dimensional domain as a linear elastic solid," and cites SODDIT, FIAT and Amar/Blackwell/Edwards. With this approach "relative element spacing and overall element count is maintained." — [Amar, Cooper, Oliver, Salazar, Agricola, "Mesh Deformation Boundary Conditions for Three-Dimensional Ablation Solvers," AIAA 2022](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20210022987/downloads/AIAA_2022_Mesh_Deformation.pdf)
- From the same survey: TITAN (2D) and 3dFIAT (3D) "rely on an initial structured mesh definition to provide recession paths along which the grid can move." Other 2D/3D codes treat the domain as an elastic solid (Hogan/Blackwell/Cochran, CHAR, Sandia). PATO uses "Laplacian smoothing... through the use of the mesh motion capabilities in OpenFOAM." KATS uses a spring analogy, "where neighboring nodes are assumed to be connected by elastic springs." Icarus uses RBF interpolation. Hero uses "an element cutting and near-surface re-meshing approach" that "does not move and redistribute nodes throughout the domain." — [Amar et al. 2022](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20210022987/downloads/AIAA_2022_Mesh_Deformation.pdf)
- Sandia's 2014 taxonomy of FE options for surface motion:
  - Element birth/death: "Allows arbitrarily large surface deformations; modest to poor surface resolution; Computationally fast."
  - Moving mesh: "PDE based method using an elastic mesh motion; Requires coupling with thermal field solution; ALE methods; Large surface deformations are problematic; Computationally complex."
  - Level sets: listed as the third option.
  The same slides give a history: CMA = "1D finite difference, node-dropping scheme"; Hogge & Gerrekens (1982, 1985) = "1D and 2D finite element, deforming mesh with spines"; Blackwell & Hogan (1994, 1996) = "1D and 2D CVFEM with Lynch & O'Neill elastic mesh motion"; FIAT = "1D CV... grid compression"; Kuntz, Hassan & Potter (2001) = "2D/3D FEM with elastic mesh motion coupled with FV hypersonic CFD code"; Amar, Blackwell & Edwards (2006) = "1D CVFEM with contracting mesh scheme"; Lachaud et al. (2008-09) = "3D ALE-FVM using OPENFOAM"; Dec & Braun (2009) = "3D GFEM with elastic mesh motion." — [Hogan, Kuntz, Howard, Gartling, Blackwell, "Development of Modeling Capabilities for Thermal Protection Systems," SAND2014-2970C, 6th Ablation Workshop](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/1142845)
- Amar, Calvert & Kirk (2011) list "improved mesh motion schemes over the translating/node-dropping scheme in CMA" as a recurring improvement. Blackwell, Hogan & Cochran "introduced the use of the control volume finite element method (CVFEM) and linear elastic mesh motion to solve one and two dimensional ablation problems." Dec and Braun "use linear elastic mesh motion." — [Amar, Calvert, Kirk, "Development and Verification of the Charring Ablating Thermal Protection Implicit System Solver," AIAA 2011](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20110000471/downloads/20110000471.pdf)

**1D: CMA, Landau/contracting grids, ITRAC**
- CMA's discretization "is based on a transformed energy equation for the ablating material with special treatment of a transition node between ablating and nonablating materials. The transition node changes in size to accommodate surface recession and is dropped when the size reaches a specified minimum." — [Ewing, Laker, Walker (ATK), "Numerical Modeling of Ablation Heat Transfer" (NTRS copy)](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20140008557/downloads/20140008557.pdf)
- Blackwell & Hogan, "One-dimensional ablation using Landau transformation and finite control volume procedure," JTHT 8(2):282-287, 1994 (bibliographic record only). — [CiNii record](https://cir.nii.ac.jp/crid/1363388843479716736)
- Amar et al. are described as using "a front-fixing method with a Landau transformation... a contracting grid that does not require nodal dropping at the interface." — [Ewing et al.](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20140008557/downloads/20140008557.pdf)
- ITRAC (ATK) is deliberately front-tracking on a fixed grid. "The grid is partitioned into active and inactive parts... Solutions are then performed only on the active portion of the grid, which continuously decreases in size as control-volumes are deactivated." Four cases (surface above, on, or below a node, or on an interface) split or reposition the surface CV and node. The surface location for each step "is determined based on the calculated ablation rate from the previous time step." The scheme "allows simple implementation of complex erosion models, such as spallation, since nodes (and control-volumes) are removed from the front surface in a manner that mimics the actual physical process. Material swelling is also easily implemented." It supports planar, cylindrical and spherical coordinates. — [Ewing et al.](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20140008557/downloads/20140008557.pdf)
- MOPAR (U. Michigan, A. Martin) is "a one-dimensional implicit solver... [using] CVFEM to model surface ablation with wall recession." (Search snippet of a 2011 workshop abstract.) — [UKnowledge, 4th Ablation Workshop 2011](https://uknowledge.uky.edu/ablation/2011/Intercalibration/4)

**TITAN / 3dFIAT (NASA Ames, Chen & Milos)**
- TITAN: "The governing equations... with a moving grid, are discretized using a finite-volume approximation and general body-fitted coordinates." The energy equation includes a "convection rate of sensible energy due to coordinate system movement." "The computational grid is compressed during the course of computation to account for surface recession." "TITAN uses an external zone with a moving grid and internal grid that is fixed. This two-zone approach can handle some simple geometries but is not sufficiently flexible to model a complicated interior structure." That limitation led to coupling with MARC FE for the substructure. Pyrolysis gas flow "is assumed to be one-dimensional along the surface-normal direction." — [Chen & Milos, "Thermal Response Modeling System for a Mars Sample Return Vehicle," TFAWS 2001 / NTRS 2002](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20030000742/downloads/20030000742.pdf)
- 3dFIAT ("3-dimensional Finite-volume alternatively directional Implicit Ablation and Thermal response code") is "solved with a moving grid system to simulate the shape change due to surface recession." It gained "a multi-block moving grid system." The authors observed that "computations using an axisymmetric three-dimensional grid system with a singular line may result in anomalous predictions around the singular line," so they built a three-block grid with no singular line. Stagnation recession is "exactly the same as that by TITAN and FIAT, because the ablation models adopted in these three codes are identical." — [Chen, Milos, Gokcen, "Validation of a Three-Dimensional Ablation and Thermal Response Simulation Code," AIAA 2010](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20100035735/downloads/20100035735.pdf)

**CHAR (NASA JSC)**
- CHAR "uses a continuous Galerkin finite-element spatial discretization with an Euler implicit time discretization and solves the governing equations coupled and implicit with a full-matrix (whole domain) linear system solver."
- ALE form: energy and gas-mass equations are written with ∂/∂t|node and −v_m·∇(·) terms, "where the node subscript denotes conservation with respect to a constant location in the moving coordinate frame." The solid mass equation "is solved in a stationary reference frame and treated as a constitutive relation."
- Mesh motion solves linear elasticity, "used solely for mesh motion... these parameters are fixed at λ = 0 and µ = 1." It is quasi-steady and applied every step with the surface displacement as a Dirichlet BC. "The mesh nodal location history is stored at two time levels... used to compute the 'mesh velocity' terms... via finite-differencing."
- Source: [Amar, Oliver, Kirk, Salazar, Droba, "Overview of the CHarring Ablator Response (CHAR) Code," AIAA 2016](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20160005889/downloads/20160005889.pdf)
- In CHAR "the nodal mesh velocities internal to the mesh are arbitrary in that a given node is not attached to a specific point of mass." Mesh velocity v_m = (x^{n+1} − x^n)/Δt. Surface recession velocity is ṡ = ṁ/ρ_s along the normal. — [Amar et al. 2022](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20210022987/downloads/AIAA_2022_Mesh_Deformation.pdf)
- The 2022 boundary scheme fits RBFs (biharmonic φ(r)=r) to define the receding and sliding geometry. Domain-corner nodes come from a constrained optimization (Lagrange multipliers, Newton with analytic Jacobians). Edge nodes come from a 1D constrained spring analogy or a uniformly contracting scheme. Face nodes come from a 2D constrained spring analogy or manifold elasticity. Interior nodes come from any mesh-motion method. — [Amar et al. 2022](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20210022987/downloads/AIAA_2022_Mesh_Deformation.pdf)
- CHAR uses libMesh adaptive mesh refinement together with recession (feature-based, on β between thresholds) in this sequence: "Iteratively converge nonlinear system → Refine Mesh → Iteratively converge nonlinear system on new mesh." — [CHAR overview 2016](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20160005889/downloads/20160005889.pdf)

**Sandia (Chaleur, Coyote_q, new multi-D FEM)**
- Sandia tools: "Chaleur – 1-D non-isothermal, reacting porous media with moving mesh and surface recession"; "Coyote_q – multi-D ... with moving mesh, surface recession, and enclosure radiation."
- New FEM capability: the recession rate sets displacement BCs, d = ṡΔt n̂. The mesh moves by solving a Laplacian-type boundary-value problem for d (Kanchi & Masud, IJNMF 2007) with "a spatially varying weighting parameter that controls mesh distortion," computed per element from element volumes (Velem, Vmin, Vmax). The exact formula is garbled in the slide text. Volume-based weighting of this kind conventionally stiffens small elements; that reading is mine, not stated in the slides. Planned additions: "elastic, deforming mesh capability" and "remeshing in parallel." Element death was used separately for foam decomposition: it "creates void volume."
- Source: [Sandia SAND2014-2970C](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/1142845)
- An earlier Sandia 2D code "uses the finite-control-volume method with unstructured grids. In this code, mesh motion (owing to ablation) is modeled by assuming the mesh behaves as a linear elastic solid." — [Chen & Milos TFAWS 2001](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20030000742/downloads/20030000742.pdf)

**PATO (OpenFOAM)**
- In 2010, PATO's first module was "implemented in 3D... The surface recession is handled using the OpenFoam moving mesh library. The surface recession velocity is deduced either from a surface temperature law (new dynamicFvMesh class), or from state-of-the-art equilibrium ablation tables." For volume ablation, "we plan to use a volume of fluid approach instead of a moving mesh." — [Lachaud & Mansour, OpenFOAM Workshop 5 abstract, 2010](https://www.jeanlachaud.com/research/Lachaud2010_AbstractOFWS5.pdf)
- PATO "uses the finite-volume method... equations are solved sequentially (as opposed to block-matrix resolution) at each time step. Each equation is solved implicitly and first-orders schemes (in time and space) are used by default as they have been found to be sufficiently accurate." The fiber-scale module COACO handles in-depth oxidation volume-averaged: "carbon fibers clearly oxidize via a progressive reduction of their diameter. The overall material recession occurs when the fibers are consumed." — [Lachaud & Mansour, "Porous-material Analysis Toolbox based on OpenFOAM and Applications," JTHT 28(2) 2014, preprint](https://jeanlachaud.com/research/Lachaud2014_JTHT_preprint)
- The full-scale MSL heatshield used about 600k cells (monolithic) and about 2M cells (113 tiles plus gap filler). "PATO adopts an unstructured moving mesh technique for the material response. PATO's moving grid system allows for shape changes due to surface recession." Interpolation to the DPLR CFD grid used OpenFOAM's Arbitrary Mesh Interface. — [Meurisse et al., "Multidimensional material response simulations of a full-scale tiled ablative heatshield," AST 2018, preprint](https://jeanlachaud.com/research/Meurisse2018_preprint.pdf)
- For mechanical erosion in PATO, the material grid is deformed "following the recession rates applied to all the failing faces using OpenFOAM dynamic mesh solvers." (Search snippet only.) — [NTRS abstract, AIAA Aviation 2022](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20210023834/downloads/Mechanical_Erosion_Modeling_of_TPS_Materials_AIAA_Aviation2022_Abstract.pdf)

**KATS / KATS-MR (U. Kentucky, Martin group)**
- KATS-MR features "modules for mesh motion, interface to external flow solvers." — [Zibitsker, PhD thesis, U. Kentucky 2024](https://uknowledge.uky.edu/me_etds/228)
- Fu (2018) added a finite-volume structural solver to KATS with quasi-static two-way thermo-mechanical coupling. "A moving mesh scheme is also developed and incorporated into the solver"; "Grid deformation is transferred among different modules in the form of variations of geometric parameters and strain effects." — [Fu, "Thermo-Mechanical Coupling for Ablation," PhD thesis 2018](https://uknowledge.uky.edu/me_etds/111)
- Cooper, Schroeder, Weng & Martin, "Implementation and Verification of a Surface Recession Module in a Finite Volume Ablation Solver" (AIAA 2018-3272) is the KATS recession-module paper cited by CHAR for the spring analogy. The full text was not accessed. — [cited as ref. 12 in Amar et al. 2022](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20210022987/downloads/AIAA_2022_Mesh_Deformation.pdf)
- The KATS fluid module uses "a fully implicit first-order backward Euler method for the time integration." — [H. Zhang, PhD thesis 2015](https://uknowledge.uky.edu/me_etds/64)

**Icarus (NASA Ames)**
- "Radial basis functions (RBF) are used to deform the computational mesh as the material recedes... particularly well-suited for unstructured grids since it is indifferent to grid connectivity... when paired with a selective algorithm for determining control points, offers superior efficiency compared with linear elasticity methods." Control points are "the centroids of the face elements on the ablating surface." The Wendland C2 compact-support RBF gives sparse systems, and the support radius is set "to a characteristic length scale of the problem." — [Schulz, Stern, Muppidi, Palmer, Schroeder, "Development of a Three-Dimensional, Unstructured Material Response Design Tool," AIAA 2017](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20180002975/downloads/20180002975.pdf)
- Icarus: finite-volume formulation, "Implicit Euler – Primitives." It is coupled to US3D through the Ares tool. — [Schulz et al., "Overview of the material response code Icarus," SciTech 2023 slides](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20230000472/downloads/IcarusOverview-Final.pdf)

**Amaryllis (Samtech/SAMCEF, Astrium)**
- "AMARYLLIS... 1D, arbitrary axis-symmetric or 3D meshes" (FEM). Ongoing development of an ALE method is meant to handle reacting regions and the ablating moving surface in swelling problems, with thermo-mechanical co-simulation between AMARYLLIS and the MECANO mechanics module through a SUPERVISOR synchronisation module. Status in 2012: under development. — [Pinaud (Astrium ST), "Thermo-Chemical and Mechanical Coupled Analysis of Swelling, Charring and Ablative Materials," 5th Ablation Workshop 2012](https://uknowledge.uky.edu/ablation/2012/Coupling/1)
- Samtech's 1994 SAE paper describes a continuously deforming FE grid to track boundary recession. (Search snippet only.) — [Gerrekens et al., SAE 941372](https://saemobilus.sae.org/papers/finite-element-simulation-thermal-ablation-processes-941372)

**Fixed-mesh / immersed approaches**
- Schrooyen et al. (IJHMT 2016): "the volume averaged Navier-Stokes equations are used to model both phases in the same computational domain... The porosity is itself a variable of the computation, allowing to take volumetric ablation into account through adequate source terms." The solver is a high-order, fully implicit DG solver. (Abstract-level.) — [UCLouvain record, "Fully implicit discontinuous galerkin solver to study surface and volume ablation competition in atmospheric entry flows"](https://research.dial.uclouvain.be/handle/2078.5/48818)
- Beauvois (2022) built a material-only DG solver in Argo. "The recession of the material is accounted for using the immersed level-set method from Argo." The coupled flow-material approach was called "accurate, but computationally expensive." — [Beauvois, UCLouvain master's thesis 2022](https://thesis.dial.uclouvain.be/entities/masterthesis/0783d95d-d5de-4d14-afee-9812dbbe0017)

**Other ALE implementations**
- CHyPS (UIUC, MFEM, DG): "An Arbitrary Lagrangian Eulerian (ALE) used to allow moving mesh. Employ the conservative direct ALE method of Ivančić et al. 2019." Forms are assembled at mesh positions t, t+Δt/2 and t+Δt through a "Multi-Stage BilinearForm." A test showed "deformation results coincident with fixed mesh." Time integration: Crank-Nicolson, forward Euler or backward Euler (custom integrator). — [Chiodi, "CHyPS: An MFEM-Based Material Response Solver," LLNL FEM seminar 2022](https://mfem.org/pdf/seminar/chiodi.pdf)
- ABAQUS: Wang et al. used five user subroutines with "the ALE adaptive remesh algorithm" (UMESHMOTION). "The entire computational domain is remeshed with the ALE adaptive remesh algorithm" at each increment. The 1D TACOT case matched FIAT, with about 3 min run time on a laptop. Common blocks limit it to a single CPU. — [Wang et al., JTHT 32(2) 2018](http://composites.syr.edu/MAAM_files/Wang%20JTHT%202018.pdf); [Wang et al., AST 91 (2019)](http://composites.syr.edu/MAAM_files/Wang%20AST%202019.pdf)
- A Thales Alenia 3D model (2010) wrote the energy and mass equations in integral moving-control-volume form with explicit −∮ρh v_mesh·n dS terms (following Amar 2006). — [ESA Thermal Workshop 2010 slides](https://exchange.esa.int/download/thermal-workshop/workshop2010/parts/Ablatherm-mi.pdf)

### Inferences
- Pyrolysis.jl's choice (solid-attached nodes, positions as ODE states, mesh velocity from cumulative strain) is unlike every production TPS code found. In production codes, mesh velocity is a kinematic convenience driven only by the surface. CHAR states that its nodes are not tied to mass. Pyrolysis.jl's nodes carry physical meaning: the solid skeleton moves with them, so swelling and shrinkage enter naturally. Production codes lack this, and it is why swelling in their multi-D tools is bolted on through a separate mechanics solver (Amaryllis+MECANO, KATS-Fu, PATO-RTV).
- In 1D the two dominant production choices are uniform contraction and translate-plus-drop. Both are purely recession-driven. Neither handles in-depth volume change unless an extra mechanism is added. ITRAC's fixed grid with CV deactivation is the only 1D production scheme explicitly marketed as supporting swelling and spallation.
- The multi-D state of practice is "ALE with a pseudo-solid/Laplacian/spring/RBF interior solve driven by surface recession BCs." A path to 2D for Pyrolysis.jl would most naturally reuse that machinery for the surface-recession part. The in-depth strain-driven (Lagrangian) part would need either a mechanics solve or a strain-compatibility solve, because 1D cumulative strain from the back face has no unique multi-D analogue.
- GCL handling is essentially undocumented in production TPS papers. CHAR's v_m = Δx/Δt with backward Euler is the standard first-order approach. Whether it satisfies the discrete GCL depends on how the ALE terms are integrated, which is not stated. OpenFOAM moving-mesh FV conventionally uses swept-volume face fluxes (general OpenFOAM knowledge, not verified for PATO). Pyrolysis.jl's explicit DGCL guarantee appears stronger than what is published for these codes.

### Gaps
- FIAT's own 1D grid details (Chen & Milos, JSR 1999) could not be accessed. "Grid compression"/contracting is attributed by Sandia and the CHAR team, not read from the FIAT paper. How FIAT behaves near complete burn-through is unknown.
- The KATS recession module (Cooper et al. 2018) full text was not accessed. KATS's GCL treatment and whether the spring-analogy mesh motion is inside the implicit solve are unknown.
- MOPAR-MD (multi-D MOPAR, apparently Colorado/Michigan) was named in one acknowledgment, but no description of its mesh method was found.
- PATO's exact OpenFOAM motion solver (displacementLaplacian vs. velocityLaplacian, diffusivity choice) and GCL treatment come only from the CHAR authors' attribution ("Laplacian smoothing"). Not confirmed in PATO docs.
- The final status of Amaryllis's ALE (planned in 2012) is unknown.
- I found no source on Hero beyond the one-line description in the CHAR 2022 survey (ATK report "Hero 4.1," 2015).

## Q2. How do codes handle (a) recession to complete burn-through, (b) char swelling or in-depth volume change, (c) multi-D shape change? What breaks?

### Takeaway
(a) Burn-through is barely discussed in the published literature. Node dropping (CMA), CV deactivation (ITRAC), element cutting (Hero), element death (Sandia) and volume-averaged or level-set fixed meshes (Argo) can in principle remove arbitrary material. Uniformly contracting or Landau grids cannot reach zero thickness. (b) Swelling is handled by (i) 1D variable grids (ITRAC), (ii) heritage intumescence models (Russell/Strobel; Henderson & Wiecek), or (iii) separate thermo-mechanical solvers co-simulated with the thermal solver (Amaryllis+MECANO, KATS+FV structural solver, PATO thermo-poro-mechanics, MIT Lagrangian DG, CEA). (c) Multi-D shape change is where ALE codes break. Prescribing all displacement components at corners or curved boundaries tangles meshes. Structured recession paths limit geometry. Large deformations are "problematic" for elastic mesh motion. The remedies are smarter boundary constraints (CHAR RBF), remeshing (Hero, Sandia plans, ABAQUS), and immersed/level-set methods.

### Cited Findings
**(a) Burn-through / large recession**
- Front-fixing (Landau) methods "can result in awkward interfacing between ablating materials and nonablating substrate materials. In addition, modeling of instantaneous spallation is not possible since the advective term in the transformed energy equation becomes infinite." ITRAC's deactivation scheme removes CVs "from the front surface in a manner that mimics the actual physical process." — [Ewing et al. (ATK)](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20140008557/downloads/20140008557.pdf)
- Element birth/death "allows arbitrarily large surface deformations; modest to poor surface resolution; computationally fast." Elastic moving mesh: "Large surface deformations are problematic." — [Sandia SAND2014-2970C](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/1142845)
- PATO's COACO fiber-scale model: "The overall material recession occurs when the fibers are consumed." Recession emerges on a fixed mesh from volume-averaged porosity. — [Lachaud & Mansour 2014 preprint](https://jeanlachaud.com/research/Lachaud2014_JTHT_preprint)
- CMA's last element "contracts until reaching a critical minimum volume, at which time it is eliminated from the domain, thereby reducing the total element and node counts by one." — [Amar et al. 2022](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20210022987/downloads/AIAA_2022_Mesh_Deformation.pdf)

**(b) Swelling / in-depth volume change**
- ITRAC "supports various models for thermochemical and mechanical ablation, in-depth material pyrolysis, and material swelling"; "Material swelling is also easily implemented." It cites Russell & Strobel, "Modeling Approach for Intumescing Charring Heatshield Materials," JSR 43(4), 2006. — [Ewing et al.](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20140008557/downloads/20140008557.pdf)
- Russell's 2002 UAH dissertation, "Analytic Modeling and Experimental Validation of Intumescent Behavior of Charring Heatshield Materials," is cited in Army AMRDEC slides with swell and decomposition zones for FIREX RX2390. Its coordinate treatment was not found. (Search snippet.) — [AMRDEC slides](https://mineralstech.com/docs/default-source/refractories-documents/pyrogenics/firex/us-army-rdecom---amrdec.pdf)
- Astrium built "a thermo-mechanical model of intumescing charring and ablative materials" with a global swelling model fitted to plasma wind tunnel and IR furnace tests. It is coupled through AMARYLLIS–SUPERVISOR–MECANO, with ALE in development. — [Pinaud 2012](https://uknowledge.uky.edu/ablation/2012/Coupling/1)
- KATS: a moving mesh scheme was added so that "thermal expansion can be captured," with two-way thermo-mechanical coupling. — [Fu 2018](https://uknowledge.uky.edu/me_etds/111)
- PATO RTV intumescence (2026): "a novel model was introduced in... PATO, to account for pore-pressure buildup within both closed- and open-pores... assuming linear elasticity for the charring silicone." "Results showed good agreement in intumescence height and temperature profiles at all heating rates." Validation was against in-situ micro-CT. The abstract does not describe the mesh-motion method. — [Oruganti, Fraile Izquierdo, Mansour, Panerai, 16th Ablation Workshop 2026](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20260007012/downloads/Oruganti_AblationWorkshopAbstract_2026_FP2.pdf)
- CHAR's thermoelastic solver is one-way coupled: "There is no feedback from the thermoelastic solver to the energy equation either in the form of solid deformation or thermoelastic coupling terms." — [CHAR overview 2016](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20160005889/downloads/20160005889.pdf)
- MIT (Quinn & Radovitzky 2023) couples heat and mass transfer, pyrolysis, "finite elastic and inelastic deformation as well as damage" in "a Lagrangian, Discontinuous-Galerkin finite element formulation," plus "a mesh recession algorithm... to explicitly account for changes in geometry due to material ablation," verified with patch tests. It is staggered: semi-implicit heat/mass, dynamic relaxation for mechanics. — [Quinn, MIT thesis 2023](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/152825)
- CEA (Cas, Baranger, Beaugendre, Peluchon): pyrolysis-thermal model "preserving mass and energy conservation during deformation," covering thermal expansion, swelling, shrinkage and ablation. The numerical scheme was not given in the abstract. — [ESA workshop abstract, June 2026](https://indico.esa.int/event/608/contributions/12320/). Their 2024 abstract cites Henderson & Wiecek (J. Compos. Mater. 21(4):373-393, 1987) as the swelling model basis. — [CANUM 2024 abstract](https://canum2024.math.cnrs.fr/programme/soumission/4dd96a15-e930-4a53-8f38-e9992239e520/abstract.pdf)

**(c) Multi-D shape change / mesh quality**
- CHAR: "Previous versions of CHAR have utilized the discrete recession velocities to enforce Dirichlet conditions on the boundary node displacements (u = ṡΔt). This approach works fine for many simple topologies. Once corners, highly-curved geometries, and non-uniform recession are introduced, enforcement of all three components of the displacement vector on a receding surface can become overly constraining and lead to poor quality or tangled meshes." The sliding BC "fails and will not maintain shape if the sliding boundary were curved." The RBF method "is shown to be robust for highly-curved geometries with large domain deformations" (BOLT flight-experiment geometry). The stated "primary drawback... is speed" (dense biharmonic RBF systems). — [Amar et al. 2022](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20210022987/downloads/AIAA_2022_Mesh_Deformation.pdf)
- CHAR 2016: "the simple boundary conditions first implemented... are insufficient for many problems, especially those with large mesh deformations." Mesh motion can be restricted to a subdomain (ablator moves, fixture fixed). Contact interfaces "must remain fixed during the solution process although the material is ablating." Future work names biharmonic mesh smoothing and non-PDE methods (Luke et al.) "for speed and mesh quality under large deformations." — [CHAR overview 2016](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20160005889/downloads/20160005889.pdf)
- TITAN's moving outer zone plus fixed inner zone "can handle some simple geometries but is not sufficiently flexible to model a complicated interior structure." — [Chen & Milos 2001/2002](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20030000742/downloads/20030000742.pdf)
- The 3dFIAT 2010 validation deliberately limited itself to "conditions with surface recession but without significant shape change." It found that axisymmetric grids with a singular line give anomalous predictions. — [Chen, Milos, Gokcen 2010](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20100035735/downloads/20100035735.pdf)
- Sandia mesh motion uses a volume-weighted Laplacian-type BVP (Kanchi & Masud) "that controls mesh distortion," with "remeshing in parallel" planned. — [Sandia 2014](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/1142845)
- Icarus: RBF results depend on the support radius ("Increasing the support radius results in a broader deformation"). Adding control points reduces that dependence. — [Schulz et al. 2017](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20180002975/downloads/20180002975.pdf)
- Differential recession at tile gaps in the MSL heatshield was captured with PATO's moving grid on a 2M-cell tiled mesh. The gap filler was meshed "with two cells of 1 mm" and treated as non-receding. — [Meurisse et al. 2018 preprint](https://jeanlachaud.com/research/Meurisse2018_preprint.pdf)

### Inferences
- Pyrolysis.jl's burnout approach (merge plus park in a fixed-size state) belongs to the node-dropping family (CMA, ITRAC). Keeping the state vector at fixed size is a practical variant that I found nowhere in the literature. Contracting/Landau grids, the commonest 1D alternative, would not fix burnout and would make it worse: as thickness goes to 0 every cell shrinks together and the transformed advection term grows without bound.
- For 2D burnout, the evidence points away from merging and toward one of three options: (i) element death/deactivation with a void (Sandia), (ii) remeshing or element cutting near the surface (Hero, ABAQUS), or (iii) fixed-mesh volume-averaged or level-set methods where porosity → 1 (Argo, PATO-COACO). Each sacrifices either surface resolution or smoothness. Element death and remeshing are discontinuous events, which conflicts with Pyrolysis.jl's differentiability goal. Volume-averaged/immersed approaches are the most "smooth" but smear the surface.
- Intumescence in 2D in this community is consistently a mechanics problem: displacement from a (poro-)elastic solve feeds the thermal mesh. Pyrolysis.jl's 1D cumulative-strain kinematics is the 1D degenerate case of that (stress-free, uniaxial). A 2D extension would need either an isotropic/anisotropic strain-compatibility solve or a quasi-static mechanics solve like KATS-Fu or PATO-RTV.
- None of these codes splits cells under strong swelling. The only explicit refinement while moving is CHAR's libMesh h-AMR on reaction-front features and the CEA constant-connectivity r-adaptation (see Q5). Cell stretching under swelling is therefore an unsolved issue in the literature too.

### Gaps
- I found no paper quantifying burn-through robustness or cost for any code (CMA, ITRAC, FIAT, CHAR).
- Russell & Strobel (2006) and Henderson & Wiecek (1987) were not accessed. Whether they use material (mass-attached) coordinates for swelling is unverified.
- An Aerotherm "TRIM" code (CMA modified for intumescing materials) appeared in one search summary without a verifiable primary link, so it is excluded from the findings.

## Q3. Is time integration monolithic/implicit with mesh motion in the Jacobian, or operator-split? Any adaptive error control?

### Takeaway
Implicit backward Euler (or first/second-order implicit) is universal, but mesh motion is almost always lagged or segregated. CHAR applies the recession from step n at step n+1 and explicitly claims "a significant speed-up over a fully implicit approach." Sandia segregates mesh motion. PATO segregates every equation. TITAN/3dFIAT use line relaxation/ADI. The one documented 1D exception is Amar, Blackwell & Edwards (2006). Their full Newton included surface-recession-rate sensitivities, which "improves convergence and reduces total computation time." Adaptive local-error control is essentially absent: CHAR runs a user time-step schedule, ITRAC fixed Δt, and only Sandia mentions "predictor-corrector with adaptive timesteps." Explicit (time-lagged) flow/surface coupling is documented to oscillate and even fail at high heating, which motivated implicit coupling.

### Cited Findings
- CHAR: "The mesh motion scheme in CHAR is explicit. The surface recession distribution is calculated at time level n, then it is applied and held constant at time level n + 1. For typical ablation problems, the recession rate changes slow enough that this assumption does not greatly impact accuracy while it provides a significant speed-up over a fully implicit approach." The physics equations are "coupled and implicit with a full-matrix (whole domain) linear system solver." Jacobians come from complex-step differentiation (perturbation ~T×10⁻¹⁰⁰, giving "exact Jacobians"). Decomposition kinetics are integrated "by treating the temperature constant over a time step as proposed by Moyer and Rindal." — [CHAR overview 2016](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20160005889/downloads/20160005889.pdf)
- CHAR's PICA validation used a prescribed piecewise-linear time-step schedule (0.005 s → 0.25 s → 0.5 s). "The temperature solutions were all shown to be spatially and temporally grid converged." — [CHAR overview 2016](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20160005889/downloads/20160005889.pdf)
- CHAR precursor (2011): "first and second order implicit time integrators... fully coupled and... solved in parallel via Newton's method... GMRES." Its verification covered only stationary-grid terms ("there are no moving mesh ablation problems in the current study"). Newton showed second-order convergence. — [Amar, Calvert, Kirk 2011](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20110000471/downloads/20110000471.pdf)
- Amar, Blackwell & Edwards (2006): an implicit 1D CVFEM code with "Newton's method applied to the entire system of equations." It showed "significant time savings over the property-lagging approach when accuracy is considered," and "including sensitivities to the surface recession rate" in Newton "improves convergence and reduces total computation time." (Abstract-level.) — [Sandia publication page, SAND2006-3242C / AIAA 2006-2910](https://www.sandia.gov/research/publications/details/one-dimensional-ablation-using-a-full-newtons-method-and-finite-control-vol-2006-01-01/)
- Sandia's new FEM (2014): "Fully coupled FEM solution using implicit time integration for energy and flow; Segregated solution for mesh motion; surface recession rate derived from surface energy balance"; "Chemical kinetics are solved at element integration points using an operator split and stiff ODE solver"; "Predictor-corrector with adaptive timesteps for advancing time." — [Sandia SAND2014-2970C](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/1142845)
- PATO: "equations are solved sequentially (as opposed to block-matrix resolution) at each time step. Each equation is solved implicitly." This was chosen for modularity: "each equation is easily modified without impacting the stability of the whole solver." — [Lachaud & Mansour 2014](https://jeanlachaud.com/research/Lachaud2014_JTHT_preprint)
- TITAN/3dFIAT: "Time-accurate solutions are achieved by an implicit time marching technique using Gauss-Siedel line relaxation with alternating sweeps." — [Chen & Milos 2001/2002](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20030000742/downloads/20030000742.pdf)
- 3dFIAT/DPLR coupling: "the time-lagged explicit approach is sufficient... at low surface heating conditions... At elevated surface heating conditions, the implicit approach has to be taken, because the carbon ablation rate becomes a stiff function of the surface temperature... resulting in severe numerical oscillations." "The time step size for updating the interface boundary conditions had to be reduced... the total computational time... increased significantly." An explicit trajectory simulation "failed to converge around the peak heating point due to excessive numerical oscillation." Implicit coupling took "about 3 to 5 cycles of iterative loop" per exchange step. — [Chen & Gokcen, "Implicit Coupling Approach for Simulation of Charring Carbon Ablators," 2013](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20140005552/downloads/20140005552.pdf)
- Icarus: implicit Euler on primitive variables (ρ_s, p, T). — [Icarus overview 2023](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20230000472/downloads/IcarusOverview-Final.pdf)
- ITRAC: the surface location per step comes from "the calculated ablation rate from the previous time step," which is lagged. Verification used fixed Δt (0.01 s, 1000 elements) against CMA (600 elements, max Δt 0.1 s). Extent-of-reaction integration with frozen temperature becomes "unrealistic for time steps beyond 0.1 sec" in one example. — [Ewing et al.](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20140008557/downloads/20140008557.pdf)
- A 2024 review: "Most strong coupling methods traditionally use a explicit coupling mechanism... freezing the ablation boundary condition... these explicit coupling mechanisms introduce numerical instabilities due to the abrupt change of interfacial quantities and time discretization errors." Chen et al. used "an adaptive time-step approach using a proportional-integral-derivative (PID) controller" for coupled fluid-thermal-structural wing problems. — [Zhang, Nie, Cai, Pan, arXiv 2410.16601 (2024)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.16601)
- U. Florida DG ablation solver (MIG lineage): implicit time integration with Newton, "where the required jacobians are calculated numerically... Analytical approach to evaluate the jacobians... is very cumbersome, and causes serious convergence issues." — [Bhatia, Roy, Gosse, AIAA 2011-145](https://faculty.eng.ufl.edu/aprg/wp-content/uploads/sites/729/2019/01/Bhatia-Roy-Gosse-AIAA-2011-145.pdf)
- MIT Lagrangian DG: "semi-implicit time integration scheme for the nonlinear heat and mass transfer problems," dynamic relaxation for mechanics, staggered coupling. — [Quinn 2023](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/152825)
- CHyPS: backward Euler / Crank-Nicolson / forward Euler, with a custom integrator ("would like to return to MFEM ODE solvers"). — [Chiodi 2022](https://mfem.org/pdf/seminar/chiodi.pdf)

### Inferences
- Pyrolysis.jl's monolithic adaptive integration (mesh in the Jacobian, local-error control) is more tightly coupled than any production TPS code found. The one comparable data point (Amar 2006, 1D) supports putting recession sensitivities in the Jacobian for both convergence and cost. The 3dFIAT experience supports implicit treatment of stiff surface recession. Together these suggest the community's explicit mesh lag is a cost compromise, not an accuracy optimum. CHAR's speed-up claim is qualitative; no numbers were given.
- The community's lack of adaptive error control means published "accuracy per cost" comparisons are confounded by hand-tuned time-step schedules. Pyrolysis.jl's adaptive control is a genuine differentiator for inverse estimation, where step-size schedules would otherwise have to be re-tuned per parameter set.
- For differentiability, the CHAR-style explicit mesh lag and CMA/ITRAC-style discrete node events both introduce nonsmooth or lagged dependence of the solution on parameters. Pyrolysis.jl's park events have the same issue at burnout. No surveyed code addresses smoothness for inverse problems. CHAR does have an inverse mode, but its algorithm is described by Oliver and was not reviewed here.

### Gaps
- No quantitative cost comparison of explicit-lagged vs implicit mesh motion was found (CHAR's claim is qualitative).
- KATS's and PATO's time-step selection (fixed vs adaptive) is not documented in sources accessed. OpenFOAM supports Courant-based adaptive Δt, but its use in PATO material response is unverified.
- The details of Sandia's "predictor-corrector with adaptive timesteps" (error estimator, tolerance) are not given in the slides.

## Q4. Code-to-code comparisons (Ablation Workshop / TACOT cases) showing accuracy or cost differences attributable to mesh strategy?

### Takeaway
Ablation Workshop test cases (series 1-3, TACOT) and bilateral comparisons show agreement at the 1-3% level between codes with very different mesh strategies. Examples: PATO vs FIAT within 2%, Icarus vs FIAT under 3%, ITRAC vs CMA "excellent," 13 codes consistent with FIAT in the 2011 case. Residual differences are attributed to physics choices or input interpretation, never to the mesh strategy. I found no study that isolates mesh-strategy effects on accuracy or cost. Series 3 (2D axisymmetric/3D iso-q) exists, but no published quantitative recession comparison across codes was found.

### Cited Findings
- 2011 test case (4th AFOSR/NASA/SNL workshop), 14 participants: "13 codes (type 1 and/or type 2) gave results in agreement with FIAT... Some differences attributed to: numerical errors (+/- 1%) and misinterpretation of the data for 2 codes (+/-6%)"; "1 code out-of-range (difference > 200%)." — [Lachaud, Magin, Cozmuta, Mansour, "A Short Review of Ablative-Material Response Models and Simulation Tools," 2011](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20110014340/downloads/20110014340.pdf)
- The same review's table of 25 tools lists Amaryllis, CAMAC, CAT, CHALEUR, CHAP, CMA (and SCMA/KCMA variants), COYOTTE2, FABL, FIAT, FIAT3D, HERO, ITARC, libAblation, MIG, MOPAR, NEQAP, NIDA, PATO, PRESENT, STAB, TITAN, TMU and US3D, with owners and users. — [Lachaud et al. 2011](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20110014340/downloads/20110014340.pdf)
- Test-case series: series 1 = in-depth heat transfer, series 2 = convective BC with recession, series 3 = "2D-axisymmetrical and 3D modeling capabilities." They were designed with PATO, FIAT (1), MOPAR (1, 2) and Amaryllis (2, 3). "Extensive comparisons of PATO/PAM_2 with FIAT (series 1), MOPAR (series 1 and 2), and Amaryllis (series 1, 2) have been done with an excellent agreement." For TC 2.3 (5 cm TACOT, 60 s convective heating with recession, then cool-down), "the agreement between PATO/PAM_2 and Amaryllis is excellent for all data" (plot only). — [Lachaud & Mansour 2014](https://jeanlachaud.com/research/Lachaud2014_JTHT_preprint)
- PATO vs FIAT (NASA/CR-2015-218960): "when using the exact same physical models, material properties and boundary conditions, the two codes give results that are within 2% of errors." The remaining gap is attributed to gas-phase heat capacity in PATO's energy equation; one case had RMS error 0.42%. — [NASA/CR-2015-218960](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20160006963/downloads/20160006963.pdf)
- Icarus vs FIAT on Ablation Workshop TC1 (TACOT, 1D, no recession): "The percent error difference between the two codes is less than 3 precent." — [Schulz et al. 2017](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20180002975/downloads/20180002975.pdf)
- ITRAC (fixed-grid front tracking) vs CMA (translate + node drop) on workshop TACOT cases, including thermochemical erosion (case 5): "Excellent agreement is seen between the two models." Both were "evaluated to ensure discretization convergence" (ITRAC: 1000 elements, 0.01 s; CMA: 600 elements, ≤0.1 s). MMS verification showed expected convergence rates. — [Ewing et al.](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20140008557/downloads/20140008557.pdf)
- MOPAR in the 2011 inter-calibration: in-depth temperatures, surface temperatures and blowing rates matched FIAT. (Snippet.) — [UKnowledge 2011](https://uknowledge.uky.edu/ablation/2011/Intercalibration/4)
- ABAQUS-ALE vs FIAT (1D TACOT, 600 s): recession histories compared with two mesh sizes (minimum 2 mm vs 0.06 mm). Good agreement with FIAT was reported in plots. — [Wang et al. 2018](http://composites.syr.edu/MAAM_files/Wang%20JTHT%202018.pdf)
- 3dFIAT vs arc-jet (PICA iso-q): total stagnation recession under-predicted by less than 6%. Recession identical to TITAN and FIAT because the ablation models are identical. — [Chen, Milos, Gokcen 2010](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20100035735/downloads/20100035735.pdf)
- CHAR vs PICA arc-jet: centerline recession predicted 0.378 cm vs measured 0.406 cm, a 7% under-prediction. — [CHAR overview 2016](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20160005889/downloads/20160005889.pdf)
- Ablation Test-case series #3 definition (2D axisymmetric and 3D iso-q geometry), v2.0, Feb 2014. — [Ablation Test Case #3 document](https://www.jeanlachaud.com/research/AblationTestCase%233.pdf)

### Inferences
- When codes are converged in space and time, mesh strategy does not show up as a distinguishable accuracy difference in 1D benchmarks. Node dropping, uniform contraction, fixed-grid front tracking, ALE remeshing and FV moving meshes all reproduce each other to about 1-3%. The differentiators are therefore cost per accuracy, robustness and the regimes these benchmarks do not exercise: burnout, strong swelling, large multi-D shape change. No comparative data was found for any of those.
- Published comparisons use fixed, hand-chosen Δt and element counts (e.g., 1000 elements at 0.01 s vs 600 elements at ≤0.1 s). This makes them uninformative on cost per accuracy. Pyrolysis.jl would need its own benchmarks (e.g., TACOT TC 2.x at matched error) to make a claim of superiority.

### Gaps
- No quantitative series-3 (2D/3D) cross-code recession comparison was found in accessible sources. Workshop slide decks on UKnowledge (e.g., KATS series-3 results) were not readable.
- No timing data comparing codes on the same test case was found.

## Q5. Has anyone in this community used mass-based (Lagrangian mass / von Mises) coordinates or moving-mesh PDEs (r-adaptation) for material response? Results?

### Takeaway
I found no TPS/ablation material-response code that uses mass (von Mises/Lagrangian-mass) coordinates, and none that uses Huang-Russell-type moving-mesh PDEs. The closest analogues are four. First, CEA's 2024 work on "mesh adaptation at constant connectivity based on refinement in zones of strong density variation" to track the pyrolysis front, which is r-adaptation, reported only as an abstract. Second, MIT's Lagrangian (material-frame) DG solver, with a separate mesh-recession algorithm, for deformation and damage. Third, Sandia's element-volume-weighted mesh-motion BVP, a quality control rather than a solution-adaptive monitor. Fourth, CHAR's h-AMR on reaction-front features combined with ALE motion. Pyrolysis.jl's solid-attached nodes are effectively a 1D Lagrangian (material) coordinate, which appears to be unusual in this community.

### Cited Findings
- CEA (Cas, Beaugendre, Baranger, Peluchon), translated from French: "The improvement of the pyrolysis-front tracking will be presented using a mesh-adaptation method at constant connectivity based on refinement in zones of strong density variations." The work compares numerical methods for the strongly coupled nonlinear PDE system "to obtain a consistent and robust method," and plans to add swelling following Henderson & Wiecek (1987). — [CANUM 2024 abstract](https://canum2024.math.cnrs.fr/programme/soumission/4dd96a15-e930-4a53-8f38-e9992239e520/abstract.pdf). Follow-up (June 2026): a model "preserving mass and energy conservation during deformation," including swelling, shrinkage and ablation. — [ESA abstract 2026](https://indico.esa.int/event/608/contributions/12320/)
- MIT: "a Lagrangian, Discontinuous-Galerkin finite element formulation of the coupled multiphysics problem," with "a mesh recession algorithm... to explicitly account for changes in geometry due to material ablation," verified by "patch tests," demonstrated on a PICA arc-jet test. — [Quinn (adv. Radovitzky), MIT 2023](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/152825)
- Sandia mesh motion: the Kanchi & Masud BVP with "a spatially varying weighting parameter that controls mesh distortion" based on element volumes. This is mesh-quality weighting, not a solution monitor. — [Sandia 2014](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/1142845)
- CHAR: feature-based h-refinement where β is between thresholds, combined with recession mesh motion; "the adapted region is contained between these two [β = 0.02, 0.98] contours." — [CHAR overview 2016](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20160005889/downloads/20160005889.pdf)
- CHAR explicitly states that its ALE nodes are "not attached to a specific point of mass," i.e., not Lagrangian. — [Amar et al. 2022](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20210022987/downloads/AIAA_2022_Mesh_Deformation.pdf)
- A targeted search for moving-mesh-PDE/equidistribution with ablation/pyrolysis returned no TPS application, only generic r-adaptive literature (e.g., MMPDElab, Cao-Huang-Russell). — [MMPDElab intro, arXiv 1904.05535](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.05535)

### Inferences
- An equidistribution-based r-adaptation (density- or reaction-rate-gradient monitor) layered on Pyrolysis.jl's Lagrangian solid motion would be novel for this community. It could address cell stretching under swelling and front resolution without changing connectivity, which preserves smoothness and fixed state size. The CEA abstract is the only sign that others are pursuing this, and no results are published yet. Note the tension: r-adaptation makes nodes no longer solid-attached, which turns Pyrolysis.jl's Lagrangian solid into an ALE solid. The solid (and its species) would then need advective fluxes across moving faces, which Pyrolysis.jl currently avoids.
- Lagrangian (mass-attached) solid meshes appear in the community only where mechanics is solved (MIT, KATS-Fu, PATO-RTV, Amaryllis+MECANO). In those cases recession is handled by a separate mesh-recession or remeshing algorithm. That is the same hybrid as Pyrolysis.jl (Lagrangian interior, surface handled by merging), so the open problem of combining Lagrangian interiors with large surface removal is shared.

### Gaps
- Henderson & Wiecek (1987) and Russell (2002) may use material coordinates for expanding composites. Their full texts were not accessed.
- The CEA r-adaptation method (monitor function, PDE vs algebraic, results) is unpublished beyond abstracts.
- I found no evidence on von Mises or mass-coordinate transformations in any TPS code, but absence in accessible sources does not prove non-use in older literature (e.g., 1960s-70s Langley/Aerotherm reports).
