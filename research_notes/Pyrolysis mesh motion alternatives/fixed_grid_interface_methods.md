# Fixed-grid and implicit-interface methods for receding, charring and swelling solids (vs. a Lagrangian moving-mesh pyrolysis solver)

Scope note: the notes cover how each fixed-grid / implicit-interface family represents the receding or swelling solid and its surface boundary condition, and what evidence exists on accuracy, cost, smoothness and multi-D extension. The comparison baseline is Pyrolysis.jl (1D cell-centred FV, solid-Lagrangian nodes as ODE states, monolithic adaptive implicit integration, burnt cells merged/parked as discrete events). Search coverage: Wiley Scholar Gateway (weak on Elsevier/AIAA content), Consensus, web search, and full-text reads of Maes et al. 2022, Caraccio et al. 2025, Bayat et al. 2022, plus several abstracts. Many key JCP/IJHMT/AIAA papers could only be read at abstract level; where that limits a claim, it is flagged.

## 1. Level-set (and other implicit-surface) methods for receding surfaces in ablation/pyrolysis: how the sharp surface BC is applied on a fixed grid, and accuracy vs. moving-mesh results

### Takeaway
At the macroscale (heat-shield, char layer) the ablation community almost always uses moving or contracting body-fitted meshes. Implicit-surface fixed-grid methods (level set, marching cubes on voxel data, ghost-fluid) are used mainly at the micro/meso scale (fibre and preform recession) and in the gas-side CFD. No study found compares level-set and moving-mesh material response head-to-head on surface temperature or mass-loss rate. General sharp-interface Stefan solvers on fixed grids do reach second-order temperature gradients, but in coupled problems the interface position converges at only about first order, and they need extrapolation, reinitialisation and stencil switching.

### Cited Findings
- **Macroscale TPS codes use moving meshes.** TITAN (2D charring ablation) "solves the surface energy balance condition with a moving grid to calculate the shape change due to surface recession", using body-fitted finite volumes and implicit time marching — [Chen & Milos 2001, JSR](https://doi.org/10.2514/2.3724). Hogan et al. displace the interior mesh "as if the body was a linear elastic solid" and verify recession rates against an analytical solution — [Hogan et al. 1996, JTHT](https://doi.org/10.2514/3.789). A contracting-grid CVFEM 1D ablation code was formally verified at second order in space and first order in time; including recession-rate sensitivities in the Newton Jacobian cut run time compared with lagging the grid-convection terms — [Amar 2006 (NCSU thesis)](https://consensus.app/papers/details/5c291575a33453169c70ef75ec4fa497/). A 2023 Lagrangian DG thermo-chemo-mechanical solver also uses "a mesh recession algorithm" — [Quinn et al. 2023, AIAA SciTech](https://doi.org/10.2514/6.2023-0197).
- **PATO and other OpenFOAM codes.** PATO (OpenFOAM) solves volume-averaged equations inside the material. Recession at the macroscale is done by mesh motion: a 2022 PATO erosion model identifies failed surface regions and "the mesh is moved accordingly to remove the failed material", computing recession from oxidation plus shear erosion — [Fraile Izquierdo, Meurisse et al. 2022 AIAA abstract (NTRS)](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20210023834/downloads/Mechanical_Erosion_Modeling_of_TPS_Materials_AIAA_Aviation2022_Abstract.pdf). The PATO surface BC uses the boundary-layer approximation (B′-type) through Mutation++, giving the ablation rate and surface element fractions — [Lachaud et al. 2015, IJHMT](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijheatmasstransfer.2015.05.106). A separate OpenFOAM TPS oxidation code sets the surface velocity to v = −ṁ/ρ and solves a Laplacian mesh-motion equation with diffusivity ∝ 1/d² to the exposed surface, so the "cell distribution within the domain remains almost constant" — [Le Maout et al. 2024, J. Am. Ceram. Soc.](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/ai/10.1111/jace.19980).
- **Icarus.** Icarus is listed with CHAR as one of the "3D refined" ablation material response models — [Liang et al. 2023, Polymer Composites](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/ai/10.1002/pc.27312). (The brief's "Schrooyen ICARUS volume-averaged approach" appears to conflate two things: Schrooyen's unified approach is a separate DG solver; see Q2.)
- **Microscale implicit-surface methods.** Lachaud and Vignoles' C/C ablation simulator couples random-walk gas transport with 3D surface recession on a fixed voxel grid, using a "Simplified Marching Cube" discretisation, and validates against analytical models — [Lachaud & Vignoles, Brownian-motion technique (HAL)](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-00410192). DSMC ablation codes store grid values and triangulate the surface with marching cubes, and "current ablation algorithms can suffer from numerical and geometric issues which distort the receding surface", which affects near-surface flow and ablation rate. Fixes include several values per cell and mass-flux-preserving decrements — [Hong et al. 2024, Phys. Fluids](https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0245713). A follow-up "mass-consistent marching cubes" enforces consistency between recession rate and mass flux — [Hong et al. 2025, Phys. Fluids](https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0284168).
- **Sharp level-set Stefan solvers (general accuracy evidence).** Bayat, Egan, Bochkov, Sauret & Gibou impose interfacial conditions sharply on adaptive quadtrees "to avoid O(1) error in gradients typical when numerically smearing the solution profile near the boundary". Their Poisson/heat solvers on irregular domains give second-order-accurate solutions and gradients in L∞. In the coupled Stefan + Navier–Stokes test, temperatures and velocities converge at "between first and second order", and the interface velocity and level set at "approximately first order". The method needs level-set reinitialisation every step and PDE-based quadratic extrapolation of fields across the interface — [Bayat et al. 2022, JCP 471:111627](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2022.111627).
- **How the ghost-fluid method applies interface conditions.** The heat equation is discretised by finite differences, and "wherever computational stencils cross the interface, ghost values are constructed by extrapolation which implicitly satisfy the jump condition" — [arXiv 1704.08897 (describing Gibou et al.)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1704.08897). Gibou, Min & Fedkiw's 2013 J. Sci. Comput. review covers level-set/ghost-fluid Stefan, Poisson and diffusion solvers and "common misconceptions" — [Gibou publications list](https://sites.engineering.ucsb.edu/~fgibou/Publications.html) (full text not retrieved).
- **Fixed solid mesh with a moving interface (propellants).** A gas/heterogeneous-solid phase-change solver keeps "both the unstructured solid phase mesh and the Cartesian gaseous phase mesh … fixed during simulation to avoid mesh reconstruction", which the authors present as an efficiency advantage — [Cang et al. 2021, IJNME](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/ai/10.1002/nme.6887).

### Inferences
- In 1D, a solid-Lagrangian mesh already puts the surface exactly on a face. A level-set or ghost-fluid method on a fixed 1D grid would add extrapolation and stencil switching and would at best match that accuracy. The Bayat et al. evidence (interface only ~first order once coupled) suggests it would lose accuracy per cell. No 1D accuracy-per-cost advantage is plausible.
- The radiative/convective surface energy balance is easy to apply in sharp form only when the interface is a mesh face or a reconstructed sub-cell location. On a fixed grid it becomes a Robin condition at an off-grid point, imposed through ghost values. Emissivity and absorptivity enter the same nonlinear surface Newton, but the surface temperature becomes an extrapolated quantity rather than a state.
- The macroscale TPS community's consistent choice of moving or contracting meshes (TITAN, PATO, KATS-type codes, Abaqus ALE) is circumstantial evidence that fixed-grid level sets have not proven better for material response with surface energy balances.

### Gaps
- No paper found reporting surface temperature or mass-loss-rate errors of a level-set material response solver against a moving-mesh solver for the same charring problem.
- "Ferguson" and "Cooper" level-set ablation papers named in the brief were not located. Ferguson's PuMA voxel-based oxidation and any Cooper level-set work remain unverified. J. Cooper et al. 2023 (JSR) is about decoupled aerothermal surface balances, not level sets — [Cooper et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.2514/1.a35433).
- The UVM dissertation on level-set plus immersed-boundary ablation (scholarworks.uvm.edu/graddis/397) could not be retrieved (HTTP 403).

## 2. Volume-averaged / porosity-based "diffuse surface" approaches (solid fraction → 0 without removing cells): surface energy balance, in-depth radiation, known artefacts

### Takeaway
Unified volume-averaged methods (Schrooyen et al.'s DG Navier–Stokes–Darcy–Brinkman solver; PATO's preform-scale volume ablation; micro-continuum DBS/VoS methods) remove the sharp surface. The flow boundary layer is resolved into the porous region, and recession becomes a front where porosity → 1. That handles burnout and topology change with no events. The cost is that the surface energy balance is no longer imposed: it emerges from resolved transport. With localisation tricks, the reactive surface area can be off by a factor of 2–4. Errors versus ALE references range from a few percent (improved formulations, refined grids) to 60–78% (standard VoS). Interface smearing spans many cells.

### Cited Findings
- **Schrooyen et al. 2016.** Their continuum approach goes "progressively from a plain fluid region to a porous medium in order to capture interactions between ablation and the boundary layer" with high-order DG, fully implicit. It is verified on cases of increasing complexity and then applied to the NASA Ames flow-tube carbon-fibre experiment. "Comparison with experimental results shows the limits of current models to capture the competition between surface and volume ablation", and a new micro-scale fibre-oxidation model was needed for agreement — [Schrooyen et al. 2016, IJHMT 103](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijheatmasstransfer.2016.07.022) ([UCLouvain record](https://research.dial.uclouvain.be/handle/2078.5/48818)). Porosity is a solution variable, so volumetric ablation enters through source terms — [Schrooyen et al., APS DFD 2014](https://meetings.aps.org/Meeting/DFD14/Session/H3.9).
- **Charring extension.** The same unified DG framework was extended to charring ablators with Goldstein pyrolysis and compared code-to-code with "a more conventional thermal response solver", with "good agreement" but no numbers in the abstract — [Coheur, Turchi, Schrooyen & Magin 2017, AIAA Thermophysics](https://difusion.ulb.ac.be/vufind/Record/ULB-DIPOT:oai:dipot.ulb.ac.be:2013/392749/TOC).
- **PATO volume ablation.** PATO's applications include a "volume-averaged study of the oxidation of a carbon-fiber preform under dry air". That is volume ablation without explicit surface tracking, alongside its conventional moving-surface mode — [Lachaud & Mansour 2014, JTHT (PATO)](https://doi.org/10.2514/1.T4262).
- **Micro-continuum DBS for TPS.** Hybrid micro-continuum (Darcy–Brinkman–Stokes-type) methods have been applied to 3D carbon-fibre ablation on fixed grids. They reproduce needle-like morphology and give a non-linear recession rate (peak 13.05 μm/s, declining to 8.35 μm/s) — [Zhang et al. 2026, Adv. Aerodyn.](https://doi.org/10.1186/s42774-026-00257-4); [Zhang et al. 2025, Phys. Fluids](https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0279817).
- **DBS framework.** One equation covers free flow and porous aggregates — [Soulaine & Tchelepi 2016, TiPM](https://suetri-b.stanford.edu/publications/journal-articles/micro-continuum-approach-pore-scale-simulation-subsurface-processes); validated against a microfluidic calcite-dissolution experiment — [Soulaine et al. 2017, JFM](https://cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-fluid-mechanics/article/mineral-dissolution-and-wormholing-from-a-porescale-perspective/82CE22EF080CA51DF1C2BEECA4C582DD).
- **Quantified artefacts (Maes, Soulaine & Menke 2022), ALE as reference:**
  - The standard VoS interface is diffuse and "spreads across a large number of layers in the computational grid".
  - The localisation function ψ used to sharpen it underestimates reactive surface area by a factor of 2–4.
  - Refinement did not remove the error: VoS-ψ converged to 5% error in grain volume and 10% in grain area on the calcite-post benchmark.
  - In a 2D micromodel, permeability differed from ALE by 60% (VoS-ψ), 40% (VoS-ψ′) and 13% (iVoS). The uniform-regime macro reaction constant differed by 78%, 8% and 2% respectively.
  - Their improved iVoS formulation at 5 μm was within 1% of experiment, like ALE, but at 20 μm differed significantly.
  - A discretisation failure: with centred gradients, a cell with ε < 1 surrounded by ε = 1 cells gets zero reaction rate, so dissolution is incomplete. Decentred gradients fix this but add numerical diffusion.
  - Source: [Maes, Soulaine & Menke 2022, Front. Earth Sci.](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/feart.2022.917931/full)
- **Calibrated exchange coefficient.** A volume-averaged diffuse-interface dissolution model "does not require an explicit treatment of the dissolving interface, e.g., the use of ALE". Its solid–liquid exchange coefficient had to be chosen ("optimum expression") by comparison with ALE reference solutions — [Luo et al. 2012, Comput. Geosci.](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10596-012-9295-1).
- **Biomass-particle VOF/porosity model (fire-relevant).**
  - A single-grid Eulerian model carries a VOF indicator plus a porosity field. Shrinkage velocity comes from a Poisson equation, with a closure Z that splits conversion between volume change and internal porosity change. The authors say Z "strongly affects results and lacks a physical basis".
  - The surface energy balance is a radiative jump condition (emissivity 0.9) with explicit interface exchange sources, equivalent to Neumann conditions. Convection is resolved through the boundary layer, not correlations.
  - Geometric VOF/PLIC advection gives second-order convergence for a fixed interface but only first order with shrinking. Darcy drag (k ≈ 1e−14 m²) is very stiff and handled semi-implicitly, with small oscillations in interfacial cells.
  - Results: char yield within 2%, but ~10% average error in final particle shape.
  - Source: [Caraccio, Cipriano, Frassoldati & Faravelli 2025, Chem. Eng. J. (arXiv 2510.17588)](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17588v2)

### Inferences
- Diffuse-surface methods win where Pyrolysis.jl is weakest: burnout and topology. A burnt-out cell simply holds ε_s → ε_min, gas and radiation pass through it, and no merge/park event is needed. They lose where Pyrolysis.jl is strongest: a sharp, exactly placed surface energy balance (re-radiation at T_s, absorptivity, convective h) and gradient accuracy near the surface. Bayat et al.'s "O(1) error in gradients" when smearing (Q1) and Maes et al.'s factor 2–4 area errors both point the same way.
- For 1D cone-calorimeter-type problems, where surface temperature and MLR are the main outputs, a pure diffuse-surface method would need several cells across the smeared front to approach moving-mesh accuracy, so cost per accuracy is worse. As a burnout-only treatment (keep the Lagrangian mesh, but let fully converted cells stay in the state as smooth near-void cells instead of being merged), it may complement the moving mesh. This is an extrapolation, not something tested in the literature found.
- Near ε_s → 0, any formulation that divides by solid mass, density or heat capacity becomes stiff or singular. The practical evidence is the need for floors, semi-implicit drag and localisation functions (Caraccio, Maes). Floors are smooth if implemented as smooth clamps, which matters for differentiability (see Q7).

### Gaps
- No source found on how in-depth radiation (Beer–Lambert / two-flux) is treated across a diffuse, partially void front in volume-averaged ablation codes. Schrooyen 2016 and Coheur 2017 full texts were not read.
- No source found on FireFOAM or other fire pyrolysis models using a solid-fraction-to-zero fixed-grid approach for flame-spread solids. The fire evidence found is the biomass-particle VOF model above.
- "Staircase heat flux" artefacts were not documented in a source. The closest is the interfacial-cell oscillation in Caraccio et al. and the incomplete-dissolution artefact in Maes et al.

## 3. Phase-field and enthalpy (fixed-domain) methods for Stefan-type ablation, melting and regression

### Takeaway
Enthalpy and phase-field methods are the classic fixed-domain alternatives for Stefan problems. They are simple to implement and handle topology change naturally. The literature consistently reports lower accuracy than sharp methods (interface conditions are not strictly enforced), and standard enthalpy schemes show temperature and front oscillations ("staircasing"). In ablation, phase-field is used at the fibre/char microscale (PICA char oxidation, woven fibres in MOOSE). No macroscale multi-reaction charring/swelling phase-field solver with quantitative comparison to front tracking was found.

### Cited Findings
- **Diffuse-interface accuracy.** Diffuse-interface (phase-field) models treat the interface as a mushy zone, so "boundary conditions are not strictly enforced at the interface". This "can potentially lead to low-order accuracy" — [Tchinda Ngueyong, Urquiza & Martin 2023, IJNME](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/ai/10.1002/nme.7408).
- **Enthalpy oscillations.** A fixed-grid finite-difference phase-change method reports "no oscillations of temperature and phase front, which are commonly observed with the typical enthalpy method", with close agreement to exact 1D solutions — [KAIST fixed-grid FD phase-change paper](https://koasas.kaist.ac.kr/handle/10203/67726).
- **Deforming-mesh enthalpy.** An enthalpy formulation on an arbitrarily deforming mesh frames the field as fixed-grid enthalpy (front tracked through a liquid-fraction variable) versus front tracking. It reports accurate, non-oscillatory predictions at a cost close to an efficient fixed-grid scheme — [Computational Mechanics 1994 (UMN record)](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/an-enthalpy-formulation-based-on-an-arbitrarily-deforming-mesh-fo/).
- **Phase-field ablation at the microscale.** A phase-field model of PICA char oxidation coupled to heat transport (2D microstructures) shows endothermic oxidation compensating the heat flux "until the char is fully consumed and then the temperature increases" — [Sessim et al. 2024, Comput. Mater. Sci.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.commatsci.2023.112713). Follow-on TMS abstracts extend it to 3D woven fibres in MOOSE/Macaw with Air-Carbon Ablation chemistry — [TMS abstract](https://www.programmaster.org/PM/PM.nsf/ApprovedAbstracts/45CBB3EBCC4127F785258CA5004DB870).
- **XFEM/CutFEM context.** Convergence results for XFEM/CutFEM Stefan methods "are very limited… often restricted to first-order accuracy in the L² norm using P1" elements, "far from the expected theoretical order (second order)". Optimal rates have been shown for the one-phase Stefan–Signorini problem — [Tchinda Ngueyong et al. 2023](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/ai/10.1002/nme.7408).

### Inferences
- Pyrolysis is not a Stefan problem with one sharp isotherm. Multi-step Arrhenius kinetics already give distributed reaction zones that a solid-fixed (Lagrangian) mesh resolves directly. Phase-field adds nothing for in-depth pyrolysis. It would matter only for the surface (recession or melting) and for swelling, where an extra order-parameter equation and interface-width resolution would be needed.
- Regularised (smoothed) enthalpy or phase-field formulations are differentiable in the parameters. Unregularised enthalpy methods produce staircase time histories at fixed probe points, which give noisy objective functions in inverse estimation. That last link is inferred, not sourced.

### Gaps
- No quantitative cost/accuracy comparison found between phase-field and front-tracking/ALE for ablation with radiative surface BCs.
- No phase-field formulation found for multi-reaction charring with swelling.

## 4. Immersed-boundary, cut-cell and embedded-boundary methods (incl. XFEM, CutFEM) for receding solids with surface fluxes; ablation, charring and wood applications

### Takeaway
On the gas side, conservative cut-cell IB methods match body-fitted grids for ablative and reacting walls. Ghost-cell IB methods show conservation errors at walls with large temperature or concentration gradients, and Cartesian IB has heat-flux reconstruction problems on blunt bodies. For the solid side, CutFEM/XFEM Stefan solvers exist, but convergence results are limited and the small-cut-cell problem needs stabilisation (ghost penalty, cell agglomeration). No IB/cut-cell charring material response or wood-combustion solid solver with validation against moving-mesh results was found.

### Cited Findings
- **Cut-cell vs ghost-cell IB.** One conservative cut-cell and two non-conservative ghost-cell IB methods (with AMR) were compared against analytical, literature and body-fitted reference solutions for atmospheric entry, including ablative BCs. Ghost-cell methods show discrepancies with large wall temperature or concentration gradients, attributed to mass-conservation errors. "Only the strictly conservative cut-cell method matches body-fitted grid methods" — [Başkaya, Capriati, Turchi, Magin & Hickel, arXiv 2309.09657 (2023; reported in Computers & Fluids 2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.09657).
- **Cartesian IB heat flux.** For a ghost-cell / level-set Cartesian method, the lack of wall alignment "results in significant reconstruction errors of surface temperature gradients and in turn, heat loads for blunt body configurations" — [JCP 2023 ghost-cell/level-set method](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0021999123008057).
- **Hybrid flow/material coupling.** A coupled hypersonic-flow/material framework uses an overset near-body + Cartesian AMR flow solver, but models the material as "a network of one-dimensional rays" with heat conduction and surface balances. It matches stagnation-point recession and shape change for camphor ablation — [Zibitsker et al. 2024, IJHMT](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijheatmasstransfer.2023.124728).
- **Unfitted methods.** Embedding geometry in a static background grid avoids remeshing for dynamic boundaries with topology changes. XDG with cell agglomeration addresses small cut cells — [Toprak, Rieckmann & Kummer 2025, IJNME](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/ai/10.1002/nme.70013). A Nitsche-based CutFEM two-phase Stefan solver must account for the front position at both t_n and t_{n−1} when integrating the mass/capacity term over cut cells, citing Fries & Zilian on the same XFEM issue — [Tchinda Ngueyong et al. 2023](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/ai/10.1002/nme.7408).
- **XFEM for ablation.** A targeted search found no XFEM application to charring ablation. Results were body-fitted FE with progressive surface removal or ALE adaptive meshing, e.g. [Wang et al. 2019, Aerosp. Sci. Technol. (Abaqus, PICA; recession over-predicted 6.4× (equilibrium) to 14× (frozen) despite fair T_s; no single h matched both)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ast.2019.05.039) and [Ge et al. 2022, ALE adaptive meshing for C/C–SiC laser ablation](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ceramint.2022.08.039).

### Inferences
- The cut-cell evidence is gas-side. For Pyrolysis.jl, the relevant point is that a conservative cut-cell FV for the solid would keep mass and energy ledgers exact. Ghost-cell or ghost-fluid approaches would not, which matters given the project's energy-ledger verification cases.
- Cut cells create a variable-volume small cell whenever the surface nears a face. Fixes are agglomeration or cell merging (a discrete event, like the current merge/park) or ghost-penalty stabilisation. A cut-cell solid solver therefore does not remove the burnout or small-cell event problem; it moves it from "burnt cell" to "small cut cell".
- For a 2D path, the Zibitsker design (multi-D flow, 1D ray material) shows that many coupled aerothermal codes still use quasi-1D material response. Pyrolysis.jl's 1D Lagrangian core could serve a ray-network approach before a full 2D solid solver is needed.

### Gaps
- No IB, cut-cell or XFEM application found for charring wood, wildland fuel, or fire-pyrolysis solids.
- No quantitative surface-temperature or MLR accuracy comparison between IB and moving-mesh material response.

## 5. Swelling and intumescence on a fixed grid: solid moving into empty cells (Eulerian / VOF-like advection), numerical diffusion and conservation

### Takeaway
No published fixed-grid (Eulerian, VOF or level-set) intumescent-coating model was found. Intumescence models use moving or stretching meshes, or a fixed mesh in material coordinates, which is a Lagrangian formulation. The fire-relevant fixed-grid evidence is the biomass shrinkage VOF model: geometric VOF/PLIC is conservative, but shrinkage reaches only first-order convergence and depends on an ad hoc closure. Particle (SPH) attempts at intumescent bubble growth have not reached realistic 10–100× expansion. Eulerian large-deformation solid methods (reference map technique) exist but need extrapolation into empty cells.

### Cited Findings
- **Fixed mesh in material coordinates.** A planar FE intumescent model expands an element instantaneously, by a fixed factor, when its average temperature passes the expansion threshold. The authors also reformulated it in material (undeformed) coordinates so "the finite element mesh remains unchanged during the thermal analysis, while the extension of the coating is fully accounted for". With coarse meshes or large time steps, the discrete expansion events produce "non-physical, yet very small oscillations"; 30 elements and Δt = 2 s were needed — [Ogrin, Saje & Hozjan 2017, Fire Mater.](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/ai/10.1002/fam.2466).
- **Mesh stretching near the substrate.** A 1D finite-difference (Crank–Nicolson) intumescent model represents swelling by expanding the mesh near the substrate–coating interface. The swelling rate dominates, and coating thermophysical properties are secondary — [Lucherini et al. (UCL Discovery)](https://discovery-pp.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10157241).
- **Expansion ratios.** Intumescent coatings can swell "up to 100 times" their initial thickness — [Cirpici, Wang, Rogers & Bourbigot 2016, Polym. Eng. Sci.](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/ai/10.1002/pen.24308).
- **SPH bubble growth.** SPH (SPHysics, with particle insertion/shifting for gas mass transfer) reproduced Young–Laplace and the initial single-bubble expansion rate. Realistic 10–100× expansion with thousands of growing and merging bubbles "remain[s] beyond the current method". The gas/polymer density ratio (~1000) causes pressure noise at interfaces and very small time steps — [Cirpici 2015, PhD thesis, Manchester](https://research.manchester.ac.uk/en/studentTheses/simulating-the-expansion-process-of-intumescent-coating-fire-prot).
- **3D kinetic swelling model.** NIST's 3D time-dependent swelling model (Butler, Baum & Kashiwagi) couples swelling dynamics, heat transfer and gasification chemistry. The abstract does not give its interface method — [NISTIR 5499, 1994](https://www.nist.gov/node/587441).
- **VOF shrinkage of a pyrolysing solid (closest fixed-grid analogue).** Geometric VOF/PLIC advection, shrinkage velocity from a Poisson equation, first-order convergence under shrinking, ~10% final shape error — [Caraccio et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17588v2). The same authors note that a mobile-mesh 3D biomass model (Gentile et al.) needed separate solid and gas grids, which they associate with instabilities and mass-conservation issues — [same source](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17588v2).
- **Reference map technique.** The reference map (inverse motion) allows an Eulerian, fixed-grid, explicit finite-difference method for finite-strain solids, with level sets distinguishing phases — [Kamrin, Rycroft & Nave 2012, JMPS](https://www.math.mcgill.ca/jcnave/papers/elasticity.pdf). Derivatives require extrapolating reference-map values from solid-occupied to unoccupied cells. Extended to multiple deformable solids in fluid on one fixed grid — [Rycroft, Wu, Yu & Kamrin 2020, JFM](https://people.math.wisc.edu/~chr/publish/JFM2020_Rycroft.pdf).

### Inferences
- For 10–50× expansion, a fixed grid has to mesh the whole future char envelope at the resolution needed in the char. The swollen layer advects into cells that were empty, with VOF or level-set transport: geometric VOF is conservative but first order in motion, and level set is smooth but not conservative. A Lagrangian mesh instead keeps a fixed number of cells and lets them stretch. The fixed-grid cost scales with the final expanded size, so it is worse when the expansion ratio is large.
- Cell stretching under strong swelling is better addressed with ALE-type r-adaptation inside the solid (redistribute nodes in material coordinates and add relative-velocity advection terms, which Pyrolysis.jl already has in its ALE machinery) than with a fixed Eulerian grid. Ogrin et al. show that a fixed mesh in material coordinates is the same Lagrangian idea.
- Swelling driven by a kinetic closure (a volume-change ratio per reaction) is a smooth source in a Lagrangian formulation. A VOF formulation adds a Poisson solve for the expansion velocity plus PLIC reconstruction, which is piecewise and non-smooth.

### Gaps
- No Eulerian, VOF, level-set or phase-field intumescent coating model was found despite targeted searches.
- No source quantifies numerical diffusion of solid fraction under VOF-like advection for large expansion ratios in pyrolysis.
- The Butler/NIST 3D model's discretisation could not be determined (full report not retrieved).

## 6. Space-time finite elements and mesh-free / particle methods (SPH, MPM, DEM, peridynamics, PFEM) for charring, intumescence or ablation

### Takeaway
Particle and mesh-free methods appear in ablation (peridynamics, with bond breaking and material-point deletion) and in polymer melt/drip fire problems (PFEM). Their selling point is fracture, cracking, dripping and free-surface topology, not accuracy per cost for in-depth pyrolysis. Recession is handled by discrete deletion events. No MPM or DEM charring or intumescence application was found. Space-time FE for moving domains exists, both body-fitted (DSD/ST) and unfitted with ghost penalties, but no ablation application was found.

### Cited Findings
- **Peridynamics, C/C ablation.** Surface recession is simulated through "the breaking of bonds and the deletion of material points", with surface energy balance BCs and B′-type mass flux; validated against 1D experiments and applied to a nose tip — [Huang et al. 2023, Acta Astronaut.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.actaastro.2023.10.017).
- **Peridynamics, charring.** A 3D peridynamic model of charring materials (H41N, AVCOAT 5026) couples nonlinear conduction with surface recession, thermal expansion and pyrolysis shrinkage strain. It recovers literature temperatures and captures shrinkage and cracking — [Zhang et al. 2023, Eng. Comput.](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00366-023-01796-w).
- **PFEM for polymer fire.** PFEM nodes "can freely move and even separate from the main analysis domain", representing melting and dripping. It has been applied to melting, burning and flame spread of polymers, with NIST and UL-94 comparisons — [Oñate et al., CAMES (PFEM polymers in fire)](https://cames.ippt.gov.pl/index.php/cames/article/view/64).
- **SPH for intumescence.** SPH was not feasible for full expansion (see Q5) — [Cirpici 2015](https://research.manchester.ac.uk/en/studentTheses/simulating-the-expansion-process-of-intumescent-coating-fire-prot).
- **Space-time FE.** An unfitted, fully coupled space-time method for parabolic problems on moving domains uses SUPG in time and ghost penalties for small cut cells — [arXiv 2511.10242 (2025)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.10242). High-order "universal mesh" FE for prescribed moving boundaries (including Stefan in 1D/2D) adjusts a background mesh so the boundary lies on element faces. This introduces ALE-type convective terms and keeps an exact boundary representation — [Gawlik et al., arXiv 1405.2107](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1405.2107).

### Inferences
- Peridynamics and PFEM bring discrete deletion or remeshing events and far more degrees of freedom per unit accuracy for smooth thermal fields than a 1D FV Lagrangian mesh. They would complement it only for cracking, delamination or dripping, which are outside Pyrolysis.jl's current scope.
- Space-time FE is effectively a high-order ALE in disguise when body-fitted (Gawlik et al.). When unfitted, it inherits the cut-cell small-cell issues. Neither gives event-free burnout for free.

### Gaps
- No MPM or DEM application to charring, intumescence or ablation found.
- No SPH application to charring or ablation solids found (only intumescent bubble growth).
- Tezduyar's DSD/ST space-time formulation was seen only via a secondary copy, so it is not cited.

## 7. Which methods keep the discrete solution a smooth function of parameters (no discrete events), and which extend most easily to 2D/3D?

### Takeaway
Smoothness ranking from the evidence, partly inferred:
1. Smooth: diffuse-interface methods — volume-averaged/DBS with a smooth ε floor, regularised enthalpy, phase-field, level set without reinitialisation switches. No topology events, but lower near-surface accuracy.
2. Piecewise smooth: Lagrangian or ALE mesh without merging, and fixed-grid sharp methods (ghost-fluid stencil switches, cut-cell volume changes, small-cell agglomeration).
3. Explicitly event-driven: cell merging/parking, element-wise expansion, peridynamic point deletion, voxel removal, remeshing.

For 2D/3D, fixed-grid methods are the easiest to extend (no mesh generation; topology handled naturally). That is the main reason to consider them. But macroscale multi-D TPS material response codes still choose moving meshes, and conservative cut cells are needed to match body-fitted accuracy.

### Cited Findings
- **Smoothness of embedded methods.** Accurate gradients with embedded-boundary Cartesian methods "require careful treatment of cut-cells", including surface triangulation and the CAD interface — [NASA embedded-boundary adjoint paper (NTRS)](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20040087116/downloads/20040087116.pdf). A Stanford thesis targets "embedded boundary methods with smoothness guarantees" (abstract only) — [Stanford PURL](https://purl.stanford.edu/tw878pr4695).
- **Discrete events cause oscillations.** Element-wise instantaneous expansion events produce small non-physical oscillations unless mesh and time step are refined — [Ogrin et al. 2017](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/ai/10.1002/fam.2466). Marching-cubes recession on voxel grids distorts surfaces and needs mass-consistent corrections — [Hong et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0245713). Peridynamic recession deletes material points — [Huang et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.actaastro.2023.10.017).
- **Smooth but low-order diffuse interfaces.** Diffuse-interface methods handle "topology changes … naturally without the need for any special procedure", but at the cost of not strictly enforcing interface BCs — [Tchinda Ngueyong et al. 2023](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/ai/10.1002/nme.7408). Even the best VoS variant needed fine grids to match ALE within ~1% — [Maes et al. 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/feart.2022.917931/full).
- **Sharp level-set machinery.** Sharp level-set methods use per-step reinitialisation and extrapolation (non-ODE operators) — [Bayat et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2022.111627).
- **Multi-D extensibility.** Unfitted methods are motivated by avoiding remeshing for "dynamic boundaries undergoing topological changes" — [Toprak et al. 2025](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/ai/10.1002/nme.70013). Conservative cut-cell IB matches body-fitted results in multi-D hypersonic tests — [Başkaya et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.09657). The fixed-grid biomass VOF model runs on quad/octree AMR and is shape-independent, though the published cases are axisymmetric — [Caraccio et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17588v2). The 3D micro-continuum ablation runs are fixed-grid — [Zhang et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1186/s42774-026-00257-4). Macroscale 2D/3D TPS codes (TITAN, PATO, OpenFOAM SiC code) recede by moving the mesh — [Chen & Milos 2001](https://doi.org/10.2514/2.3724); [Fraile Izquierdo et al. 2022](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20210023834/downloads/Mechanical_Erosion_Modeling_of_TPS_Materials_AIAA_Aviation2022_Abstract.pdf); [Le Maout et al. 2024](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/ai/10.1111/jace.19980).

### Inferences (overall assessment against Pyrolysis.jl goals)
- **Accuracy per cost (1D):** no fixed-grid method in the evidence is likely to beat a solid-Lagrangian mesh for surface temperature or MLR in 1D. Sharp fixed-grid methods at best match it and add machinery (interface ~first order when coupled). Diffuse methods lose near-surface gradient accuracy and need refinement.
- **Error control:** a monolithic adaptive stiff ODE integrator works best with RHS functions that are smooth in the state. Diffuse methods with smooth floors fit this well. Sharp fixed-grid methods introduce RHS discontinuities whenever the interface crosses a node or face, so the error controller sees them as events, much like parking does today. Level-set reinitialisation is not an ODE operator.
- **Differentiability for inverse estimation:** the only route to event-free burnout in the literature is a diffuse void: keep fully converted cells as near-void cells with a smooth solid-fraction floor, as in Schrooyen/PATO/DBS. Combining this with the existing Lagrangian mesh (no merge, no park; cells shrink to a floor thickness, or keep the mesh and let ε_s → ε_min) is the most promising complement suggested by this survey. It would trade a small, controllable smearing at burnout for smoothness and 2D compatibility. The stiffness of near-empty cells is the known cost.
- **Intumescence (10–50×):** the evidence favours Lagrangian/material-coordinate formulations plus node redistribution (ALE r-adaptation) over fixed grids. No fixed-grid intumescent model exists in the literature found, and fixed-grid shrinkage/expansion is first order with closure sensitivity.
- **2D/3D:** fixed grids extend most easily (no remeshing, topology changes handled), and this is their strongest case. Conservative cut-cell FV or diffuse volume-averaged formulations would be the candidates for a 2D Pyrolysis.jl. Ghost-cell methods are disfavoured by the conservation evidence. A cheaper intermediate path, also seen in practice, is a 1D-ray network coupled to multi-D flow (Zibitsker et al.).
- **Net judgement:** none of the fixed-grid families is clearly better than the moving-mesh approach for the 1D accuracy-per-cost and surface-BC fidelity goals. The diffuse-void idea is a useful complement for burnout robustness and smoothness, and cut-cell or diffuse methods are the natural route for multi-D receding geometry.

### Gaps
- No study found that measures parameter-sensitivity smoothness (e.g. finite-difference noise in objective functions) for ablation/pyrolysis solvers of any type. The smoothness ranking above is reasoned from method structure, not measured.
- No benchmark compares fixed-grid and moving-mesh solvers on a common charring/burnout problem with radiative surface BCs.
- The cost (wall-clock per accuracy) of diffuse or cut-cell material response relative to moving-mesh material response is unreported in the sources found.
