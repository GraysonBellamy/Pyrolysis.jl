# Moving-mesh and adaptive-mesh numerics for a Lagrangian, monolithic-MOL pyrolysis solver

Scope: numerical-methods options (continuous ALE, Lagrange-remap, MMPDE/r-adaptation, hybrid Lagrangian+correction, front-fixing, h-adaptivity) judged against the current Pyrolysis.jl design. That design is 1D cell-centred FV. Node positions z_i are ODE states, and the mesh velocity is the solid velocity (cumulative volumetric strain from the back face). Everything is integrated monolithically with SDIRK/Rosenbrock and adaptive error control, and the discrete GCL holds. Burnt-out cells are merged and "parked", and each merge restarts dt at 1e-6 s. Where a statement maps literature onto Pyrolysis.jl, it is under "Inferences", not "Cited Findings". Sources were read in full text where noted (Budd–Huang–Russell Acta Numerica preprint, Furzeland–Verwer–Zegeling 1990, Javierre et al. 2005/06 report, Huang–Kamenski 2018 preprint, ARKODE paper, CHAR and Ewing NASA papers). Everything else comes from abstracts or search snippets and is flagged as such.

## 1. True ALE with conservative rezone + remap (Lagrange-remap vs. continuous ALE)

### Takeaway
"Indirect" ALE (a Lagrangian step, then a rezone, then a conservative remap) is the workhorse of multi-D hydrocodes, ocean models and large-strain poromechanics. The remap is conservative by construction when written in flux (swept-region or intersection) form. It is second order with limiters (sign- or positivity-preserving), and each remap adds numerical diffusion that falls with reconstruction order but never vanishes. In a monolithic adaptive-implicit MOL code the discrete remap is an event outside error control, and the literature that compares it with moving-grid MOL shows that frequent regridding destroys the integrator's efficiency. "Continuous" ALE fits the current design far better: the mesh velocity is a smooth function of the state, remap becomes an advective flux across faces, and everything stays inside the ODE.

### Cited Findings
- **Classical ALE steps.** The textbook ALE algorithm has three steps: (1) a Lagrangian step that advances the physics and the mesh; (2) a rezone step that improves mesh geometric quality; (3) a remap step that conservatively transfers all quantities to the rezoned mesh — [Barlow, Maire, Rider, Rieben & Shashkov 2016, JCP 322:603–665, review of indirect ALE (abstract)](https://laro.lanl.gov/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Arbitrary-LagrangianEulerian-methods-for-modeling-high-speed/9916361457803761); [Combined swept-region/intersection remap, IJNMF, doi 10.1002/fld.4384 (abstract)](https://doi.org/10.1002/fld.4384)
- **Swept-region vs. intersection remap.** For single-material remap the process "can be represented in a flux form, with fluxes approximated by integrating a reconstructed function over intersections of neighboring computational cells on the original and rezoned computational mesh". Exact intersection is complex and expensive, so a simpler swept-region approach (regions swept by cell edges during rezoning) is often used. That simplification "can lead to distortion of the solution symmetry" — [doi 10.1002/fld.4384](https://doi.org/10.1002/fld.4384)
- **Margolin & Shashkov 2003.** "Second-order sign-preserving conservative interpolation (remapping) on general grids" (JCP 184:266–298) was designed specifically for *continuous-rezone* ALE simulations (bibliographic summary only) — [Margolin listing](https://independent.academia.edu/margolinlen); follow-on MPDATA-based second-order sign-preserving remaps for unstructured ALE — [Loughborough repository](https://repository.lboro.ac.uk/articles/thesis/MPDATA_remapping_for_unstructured_meshes/9577613)
- **What a remap must deliver.** A good remap should be "conservative, high order accurate, essentially non-oscillatory, positivity-preserving for certain physical variables and efficient" (Gu, Li, Cheng & Shu, high-order positivity-preserving conservative remap; search-snippet level) — [NSF PAR copy](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10420257)
- **Rezone criteria.** Most ALE codes rezone to keep the mesh quality high. An alternative "error-minimization" rezone minimizes the L2 norm of the solution error while keeping the mesh smooth — [Numer. Methods PDE 2005, doi 10.1002/num.20113 (abstract)](https://doi.org/10.1002/num.20113)
- **Ocean models (Lagrangian-remap).** In Lagrangian-remap ocean models (MOM6-type), regrid/remap steps "are needed for Lagrangian methods in order for the grid to retain an accurate representation of the ocean state", and "conservative remapping [is] required to preserve integrated scalar contents (e.g., potential enthalpy, salt mass, and seawater mass)" — [Griffies, Adcroft & Hallberg 2020, JAMES primer, doi 10.1029/2019MS001954](https://doi.org/10.1029/2019MS001954)
- **Remap order vs. spurious diffusion (ocean models).** White, Adcroft & Hallberg (2009, JCP) found conventional remapping "generally too numerically diffusive and inaccurate" for long integrations. PPM is "notionally third order", while PQM is fifth order (cell mean, two edge values, two edge slopes), and its limiter drops degree only at extrema. High-order reconstruction in boundary cells is "crucial" to reduce spurious diffusion near boundaries, and limiters that fall back to low order near boundaries are a diffusion source. Volume and tracers are conserved to machine precision — [Princeton record](https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/high-order-regridding-remapping-schemes-for-continuous-isopycnal-/); [LOM 2009 PQM slides](https://www.coaps.fsu.edu/LOM/2009/pdf/LOM_2009-PQM_remapping.pdf). Adcroft's later slides: diffusion decreases with reconstruction order but does not vanish — [Adcroft, ECMWF 2020](https://events.ecmwf.int/event/167/contributions/1377/attachments/796/1415/AS2020-Adcroft.pdf)
- **Remap smooths history variables (geomechanics).** "The remapping of state variables in the convective phase of the remeshing and ALE method introduces smoothing", and intermittent remeshing "introduces extra computational effort" — [Engin et al. 2015, IJNAMG, doi 10.1002/nag.2376](https://doi.org/10.1002/nag.2376)
- **Operator-split ALE for porous media.** Large-strain ALE exists for saturated porous media with solid–fluid coupling, the closest structural analogue to a deforming porous solid with Darcy flow. Each step is an updated-Lagrangian solve followed by an Eulerian step: smooth the mesh, then transfer the state variables. It was verified against the analytical 1D finite-strain consolidation solution — [Di, Yang & Sato 2007, IJNAMG 31:1375, doi 10.1002/nag.601](https://doi.org/10.1002/nag.601). Nazem et al. extend ALE to large-strain consolidation and discuss pore-pressure convection, permeability and void-ratio updates under convection, and mesh refinement — [Nazem, Sheng, Carter & Sloan, IJNAMG 32:1023, doi 10.1002/nag.657](https://doi.org/10.1002/nag.657)
- **Conservative vs. non-conservative interpolation.** In moving-mesh methods solved "alternately" (mesh step, then interpolation, then PDE step), interpolation "often has to be done very carefully to preserve conservation laws". Non-conservative interpolation "leads to unsatisfactory results" for conservation laws. Tang & Tang's leading-order conservative interpolation, (X^{n+1}_{j+1}−X^{n+1}_j)û_{j+½} = (X^n_{j+1}−X^n_j)u_{j+½} + Δt((Ẋu)_{j+1}−(Ẋu)_j), "automatically conserves discrete mass". In other words, the remap is written as a flux of u carried by the mesh velocity — [Budd, Huang & Russell, Acta Numerica 18 (2009), §1.3 and §3 (preprint full text)](https://people.bath.ac.uk/mascjb/Papers09/acta.pdf); [Cambridge record](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/acta-numerica/article/adaptivity-with-moving-grids/3F7AA4F21C502125FBC832CF9EF84820)
- **Discrete GCL (Farhat, Geuzaine & Grandmont 2001, JCP 174:669).** Later work reports that for ALE finite-volume schemes "a discrete maximum principle is satisfied if and only if the D-GCL is satisfied", so the DGCL is a nonlinear-stability condition. Its effect on stability has been called "unclear and somewhat contradictory" (Étienne et al. 2009, as cited). Secondary sources only: [Total energy conservation in ALE schemes, EJCM](https://journals.riverpublishers.com/index.php/EJCM/article/view/1663). In ALE-DG on moving simplices the GCL holds if the RK time integrator's order is at least the spatial dimension — [ALE-DG on moving simplex meshes, arXiv 1812.10364](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1812.10364)
- **Matrix symmetry.** Moving the grid via the ALE convection term makes an implicit discretization non-symmetric, whereas the interpolative moving-grid variant keeps symmetric matrices — [Javierre, Vuik, Vermolen & van der Zwaag, TU Delft report 05-03 / JCAM 192 (2006), §3](https://diamhomes.ewi.tudelft.nl/~kvuik/papers/Jav05VVZ.pdf)
- **Frequent regridding with stiff MOL integrators (direct evidence).** Furzeland, Verwer & Zegeling compared moving-grid methods with BDF integrators (DASSL-type). Method I (Petzold-type, regrid between steps and interpolate) "keeps regridding, which means that very many restarts are made with the first-order implicit Euler rule". On a flame problem it took 663 steps, 709 Jacobians and 1845 back-solves, with 259 error-test failures, "the greater part" directly after regridding. Regridding at only ~100 prescribed times cut this to 331 steps, 98 Jacobians and 739 back-solves at comparable accuracy, with DASSL then "mostly order 3". The authors also note that "many successive interpolations can still cause a perceptible loss of spatial accuracy" — [Furzeland, Verwer & Zegeling 1990, JCP 89:349–388, §4 and §5 (CWI full text)](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/1640/1640D.pdf)

### Inferences
- Pyrolysis.jl is already a continuous-ALE scheme in the degenerate case w = v_solid. The relative velocity (w − v_s) is zero for the solid, so solid species and solid enthalpy are transported exactly (no numerical diffusion). Any ALE variant that chooses w ≠ v_s gives up that exactness: a face flux ρ_k(w − v_s) appears for every solid species. Solid species have **zero physical diffusivity**, so all of that flux's numerical diffusion is artificial smearing of char/virgin and composition fronts. Temperature is different: the thermal cell Péclet number of the correction velocity is tiny. For example, |w − v_s| ~ 2e-5 m/s (a PMMA-like regression rate), Δz ~ 1e-4 m and κ ~ 1e-7 m²/s give Pe_cell ≈ 0.02. The practical rule is therefore first-order upwinding is acceptable for energy but questionable for solid composition. Use a limited second-order reconstruction (MUSCL/van Leer, or PPM per the ocean-model evidence) for species, or keep |w − v_s| small.
- **Lagrange-remap (discrete rezone events) is a poor fit for this code.** Each remap is a state jump the error estimator never sees. With BDF it forces an order/history restart (Furzeland). With the one-step SDIRK/Rosenbrock already in use it at least discards the FSAL/dense-output state. And every remap adds diffusion, which accumulates with the remap count (ocean and geomechanics evidence). It would recreate exactly the event cost the merge/park mechanism already pays.
- **Continuous ALE is the variant that preserves the current strengths.** It keeps one ODE system, global error control over mesh motion, a DGCL that holds by construction if cell volumes are computed from the z_i states, and no events. Implementation cost on top of the current design:
  - choose w (Sections 2 and 3);
  - add a relative-velocity face flux for every per-volume conserved quantity (solid species, energy, gas mass);
  - add its Jacobian entries (z_i already enters the Jacobian, so the sparsity pattern changes little: still block-tridiagonal in 1D).
- Remap order needs care where energy is concerned: remap enthalpy or ρc_pT (conserved), not T. The ocean-model finding that boundary-cell reconstruction order dominates spurious diffusion applies directly to the heated surface cell.

### Gaps
- I found no study that pairs continuous ALE (free mesh velocity, relative advective flux) with an *adaptive, error-controlled* SDIRK/Rosenbrock integrator and reports cost and accuracy against a pure-Lagrangian baseline. The closest evidence is Furzeland et al. (BDF, 1990) and the quasi-Lagrangian MMPDE literature (Section 2).
- The full texts of Barlow et al. 2016 and Margolin & Shashkov 2003 were not retrieved, so specific convergence-order tables for remap are not quoted here.

## 2. Moving-mesh PDEs / r-adaptation / equidistribution (Huang–Russell MMPDE, de Boor, Dorfi–Drury, Moving Finite Elements)

### Takeaway
In 1D, MMPDE-type r-adaptation coupled "simultaneously" with the physics in a method-of-lines code with a stiff implicit integrator (the quasi-Lagrangian formulation: Huang, Ren & Russell 1994; MOVCOL with DASSL) is mature and "the method of choice". This is structurally what Pyrolysis.jl already does, except that its mesh equation is physical (solid kinematics) rather than an equidistribution relaxation. Documented gains range from orders of magnitude (81 moving points beating 10^5 uniform points on self-similar blow-up) down to factors of about 2 in time steps for moderately thin flame fronts. The costs are a stiffer system, a smoothing parameter τ (plus spatial monitor smoothing), and tuning that is problem-dependent. Equidistribution (Dorfi–Drury/MMPDE) needs less tuning than Moving Finite Elements. Mesh crossing is impossible for the exact MMPDE and provably avoided for the semi-discrete one under a coercivity condition.

### Cited Findings
- **Pros and cons of r-adaptivity.** Fixed N and fixed topology give constant sparsity and no hanging nodes or nested data structures. The costs are auxiliary mesh PDEs that "can often be very stiff and thus expensive to solve", and r-methods "get the best estimates for a given N rather than errors necessarily lower than a specified tolerance" — [Budd, Huang & Russell 2009, Acta Numerica 18, §1.2](https://people.bath.ac.uk/mascjb/Papers09/acta.pdf)
- **Efficiency example.** A 1D nonlinear Schrödinger focusing problem with N = 81 moving points "is able to resolve singular structures with a length-scale of 10^−5 and out performs discretisations on uniform meshes with 10^5 mesh points" — [Budd et al. 2009, §1.2](https://people.bath.ac.uk/mascjb/Papers09/acta.pdf)
- **Mesh equations need not be solved accurately.** "A quite rough approximation to the solution of the moving mesh equations will often deliver a mesh more than adequate", and relaxed MMPDEs can even be advanced with a simple explicit method — [Budd et al. 2009, §1.2](https://people.bath.ac.uk/mascjb/Papers09/acta.pdf)
- **Simultaneous vs. alternating coupling.** Simultaneous (MOL) solution "avoids the need for any interpolation … but is usually associated with having to solve stiff differential equation". Alternating solution needs (conservative) interpolation and "can be associated with mesh tangling". The quasi-Lagrangian simultaneous approach "is generally the method of choice when used in calculations in one spatial dimension (Huang, Ren and Russell 1994)" but is "much harder to use in higher dimensions as the coupled system can be rather stiff and the equations are very nonlinear". Alternating "reduces the stiffness problems but can lead to a lag in the mesh movement" — [Budd et al. 2009, §1.3, §3](https://people.bath.ac.uk/mascjb/Papers09/acta.pdf)
- **Integrators used.** In MOVCOL (collocation physics plus finite-difference MMPDE, solved simultaneously) "the resulting ODEs are somewhat stiff and are typically solved using an appropriate stiff solver such as an SDIRK … or a BDF"; MOVCOL uses DASSL — [Budd et al. 2009, §3.1](https://people.bath.ac.uk/mascjb/Papers09/acta.pdf)
- **Choosing τ.** τ sets the mesh response time. Budd et al. recommend first relaxing the mesh to equidistribution with ε = 1, then running with ε = 0.01, so the mesh ODEs "are not especially stiff". The natural mesh timescale τ ≈ ε/M should be "smaller, but of the same order as the natural evolutionary time-scale of the underlying PDE". Their Theorem 2: for the *exact* MMPDE, x_ξ > 0 (no crossing) and an ε-close mesh stays ε-close; "if a too coarse discretisation is used … (iii) may be violated" — [Budd et al. 2009, §3, eq. 3.13](https://people.bath.ac.uk/mascjb/Papers09/acta.pdf)
- **Spatial smoothing.** The 2D MMPDE "is rather stiff and can also benefit from a degree of mesh smoothing", e.g. (1 − γ∂_ξξ)x_τ = ∇_ξ·(M∇_ξ x); averaging the monitor function also gives smoother meshes — [Budd et al. 2009, eq. 2.38](https://people.bath.ac.uk/mascjb/Papers09/acta.pdf)
- **Position-based vs. velocity-based methods.** Position-based methods (MMPDE, optimal transport) "tend to produce smoother meshes, and are much less prone to the problem of mesh tangling than velocity based methods" (GCL method, MFE, harmonic map). Pure Lagrangian mesh motion can tangle (vorticity) and "race". The authors' recommendation: "it is often optimal for the mesh points to move in a similar manner to the particles, but not to follow their motions too precisely" — [Budd et al. 2009, §2.3](https://people.bath.ac.uk/mascjb/Papers09/acta.pdf)
- **Three-method comparison with BDF (Furzeland et al. 1990).** The study compared MFE (Miller), Petzold's method and a Dorfi–Drury-based method, all with stiff BDF solvers. Its conclusions:
  - MFE needs penalty regularization "to avoid node overtaking and singular mass matrices", whose parameters are "problem-dependent", making it "troublesome"; its time stepping is "rather sensitive with respect to the grid movement".
  - The Dorfi–Drury/equidistribution Method II is "easier to work with and implement … and also more efficient", with grid-smoothing parameters (κ ≈ 1–2, temporal τ small but not zero) that were not troublesome.
  - Method II struggles with sharp corners where |u_x| is not large (spurious oscillations on a triangular pulse), which MFE handles well.

  [Furzeland, Verwer & Zegeling 1990, §5](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/1640/1640D.pdf)
- **Flame-front numbers (40 moving points, BDF).** On the combustion ignition/flame problem, Method II with temporal smoothing r = 1.0 (a non-moving grid) took 293 steps, 59 Jacobians and 807 back-solves. With r = 0.01 it took 197/44/571, with r = 0.001 169/38/516, and with r = 1e-4 150/34/450. The method kept BDF orders "up to three (occasionally four and five)", and "the grid follows the flame better and better and STEPS is steadily reduced, which nicely reflects the Lagrangian nature of the method". Caveat from the authors: the flame "is not very thin", and a uniform fixed mesh of about 40–100 nodes would also do. Moving-grid time-stepping costs "may still … be rather large compared with the costs of time-stepping in the conventional way on a non-moving grid" — [Furzeland et al. 1990, §4, Fig. 4.2, §5](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/1640/1640D.pdf)
- **General tuning warning.** "Most of the techniques … require some form of tuning … this additional tuning is to the detriment of reliability … the tuning, being rather problem-dependent, does not lend itself to automation" — [Furzeland et al. 1990, §1](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/1640/1640D.pdf)
- **Non-tangling theorem.** If the meshing functional satisfies a coercivity condition, the semi-discrete MMPDE mesh "is nonsingular for all time if it is nonsingular initially". Element altitudes and volumes are "bounded below by positive numbers depending only on the number of elements, the metric tensor, and the initial mesh". The result extends to fully discrete schemes if the time step is small enough and the integrator preserves monotone energy decrease. Huang's functional with p > 1 qualifies, and the result holds in any dimension — [Huang & Kamenski 2018, Math. Comp. 87:1887–1911 (arXiv 1512.04971)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1512.04971)
- **Groundwater fronts.** MMPDE "alternately" solved with the physics captured sharp moving fronts (advection-dominated reactive transport, nonlinear infiltration, density-dependent flow) "accurately and efficiently … with a relatively small number of grid points". Practical ingredients were an interpolation-error monitor, control of mesh concentration, and two-layer mesh movement — [IJNME 2002, doi 10.1002/nme.482 (abstract; authors not confirmed from snippet)](https://doi.org/10.1002/nme.482)
- **Failure case with a general implicit solver.** A coupled MMPDE plus precipitation–dissolution system solved fully implicitly with MATLAB's ode15i was "ineffective … it leads to a time stepping outside of machine precision". The authors resorted to adaptive sub-stepping of the mesh equation with explicit mesh-crossing checks — [IJNMF 2024, doi 10.1002/fld.5294](https://doi.org/10.1002/fld.5294)
- **Low-tier benchmark (convection–diffusion boundary layer).** MMPDE5/MMPDE6 reach L2 error 4.9e-4/4.7e-4 at N = 512 versus 9.95e-2 on a fixed mesh, with observed orders ≈1.8–2.0 versus 0.89 on the fixed mesh. Caveat: in the same table a cited reference method has errors about 100× smaller still, so treat this as illustrative only — [ZAMM 2025, doi 10.1002/zamm.70312](https://doi.org/10.1002/zamm.70312)

### Inferences
- **Pyrolysis.jl is already a simultaneous quasi-Lagrangian MOL moving-mesh code.** Moving to MMPDE r-adaptation would replace (or augment) the physical mesh equation ż_i = v_s(z_i) with a relaxation such as MMPDE5: τ ż = ∂_ξ(M ∂_ξ z), with boundary nodes pinned to the back face and the receding surface. The infrastructure is in place: z_i are states, the Jacobian includes mesh motion, and a DGCL holds. New pieces:
  - a smooth monitor M, e.g. α + arc-length of T, + |reaction rate|, + a material-mass term;
  - τ, and spatial smoothing of M;
  - relative-velocity advective fluxes for solid species and energy (Section 1).
- **Expected gains are modest, not orders of magnitude.** Big r-adaptive gains are documented for singular or self-similar structures. For a moderately thin front, where 40–100 fixed nodes already suffice, the evidence is about a 2× reduction in time steps and no reduction in node count (Furzeland). Pyrolysis reaction zones in cone-calorimeter conditions are typically resolved with tens to low hundreds of cells, so an MMPDE would most likely buy a factor of about 2–10 in cell count at equal accuracy for thick samples at high flux, at the price of τ/monitor tuning. This is a plausible upgrade for accuracy per cell, not a fix for burnout by itself.
- **Burnout-specific value.** The non-tangling and lower-bound-on-cell-size property (Huang–Kamenski; Budd Theorem 2) is the mechanism that *prevents* cells from collapsing. A pure-Lagrangian mesh has no such property: a cell whose solid is consumed collapses by construction. An MMPDE with a floor α in M keeps every cell at least of order (α/∫M)·L(t), where L(t) is the current domain thickness. This is the core idea of the hybrid in Section 3.
- **Stiffness.** MMPDE5 is a diffusion equation in ξ with an effective rate of about M/(τΔξ²). With SDIRK/Rosenbrock this stiffness is harmless. The real risk is monitor discontinuities (e.g. an abrupt char/virgin interface) causing mesh racing; monitor smoothing is mandatory.

### Gaps
- No source found applies MMPDE to condensed-phase pyrolysis or charring ablation specifically; evidence comes from combustion fronts, Stefan problems and groundwater transport.
- No quantitative cost comparison was found between MMPDE r-adaptation and a pure-Lagrangian mesh for a problem with a physically moving material (both would be "Lagrangian-like").

## 3. Hybrid: Lagrangian solid + mesh-velocity correction (continuous ALE w = v_s + c) so cells never collapse; how a cell's material goes to zero

### Takeaway
The hybrid is the continuous-ALE/MMPDE family with a material-following bias. It is explicitly recommended in the r-adaptivity review ("move in a similar manner to the particles, but not … too precisely"), and it is standard in large-strain poromechanics (updated-Lagrangian plus mesh smoothing, though usually operator-split). In ablation codes the boundary-driven variants dominate: elastic or Laplacian mesh motion driven by surface recession (CHAR, Blackwell–Hogan, Dec–Braun), contracting/Landau grids (Amar et al.), and translating grids with node dropping (CMA) or node removal (ITRAC). For material "going to zero", codes use either discrete removal/deactivation (FDS 3D burn-away, CMA node dropping, ITRAC node removal, masking in Gpyro3D) or a mesh that never isolates the vanishing material. I found no published 1D pyrolysis or ablation code that runs a monolithic continuous ALE with a collapse-preventing correction and documents burnout to zero residue.

### Cited Findings
- **Hybrid motion is the review's recommendation.** "In a purely Lagrangian setting … such a procedure can lead to mesh tangling … and mesh racing … In practice … it is often optimal for the mesh points to move in a similar manner to the particles, but not to follow their motions too precisely" — [Budd, Huang & Russell 2009, §2.3](https://people.bath.ac.uk/mascjb/Papers09/acta.pdf)
- **Continuous-rezone ALE.** Margolin & Shashkov's second-order sign-preserving remap targets "continuous rezone ALE", i.e. a mesh relaxed every step rather than at discrete events — [Margolin listing](https://independent.academia.edu/margolinlen)
- **Poromechanics.** Updated-Lagrangian plus mesh-smoothing ALE is used to stop element distortion in large-deformation saturated porous media. Without it, Lagrangian methods "are more likely to result in a negative Jacobian for an individual element" — [Sabetamal et al. 2015, IJNAMG, doi 10.1002/nag.2386](https://doi.org/10.1002/nag.2386); [Di et al. 2007, doi 10.1002/nag.601](https://doi.org/10.1002/nag.601)
- **CHAR (NASA JSC) mesh motion.** Mesh motion solves linear elasticity used "solely for mesh motion", with λ = 0 and μ = 1, and surface recession prescribed as a Dirichlet BC. Mesh velocity is obtained by finite-differencing two stored node-position levels. The scheme is explicit: recession computed at level n is held constant over n+1, which the authors say is a "significant speed-up over a fully implicit approach". Motion can be restricted to a subdomain (ablator on a rigid substructure). The authors note that the simple boundary conditions first implemented "are insufficient for many problems, especially those with large mesh deformations" — [Amar, Oliver, Kirk, Salazar & Droba, "Overview of the CHarring Ablator Response (CHAR) Code", AIAA 2016-3385, §VI (read from NTRS copy)](https://doi.org/10.2514/6.2016-3385). The 2022 follow-up uses radial basis functions to define the evolved surface, plus constrained optimization, elasticity and spring-analogy solvers to keep mesh quality; it is "robust for highly-curved geometries with large domain deformations" — [Amar et al., "Mesh Deformation Boundary Conditions for Three-Dimensional Ablation Solvers", AIAA 2022](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20210022987/downloads/AIAA_2022_Mesh_Deformation.pdf)
- **Ablation-code lineage.** CMA used a "translating/node-dropping scheme". Blackwell, Hogan and Cochran introduced CVFEM with linear elastic mesh motion in 1D and 2D. Dec & Braun used Galerkin FEM with linear elastic mesh motion in 1D and 3D — [Amar, Calvert & Kirk 2011, AIAA 2011-144 (NTRS 20110000471)](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20110000471)
- **How material is removed in ablation codes.** CMA's transition node "changes in size to accommodate surface recession and is dropped when the size reaches a specified minimum". Amar et al. used a Landau contracting grid "that does not require nodal dropping". ITRAC (ATK) uses a front-tracking variable grid in which "nodes (and control-volumes) are removed from the front surface in a manner that mimics the actual physical process", which also makes spallation and swelling "easily implemented" — [Ewing, Laker & Walker, "Numerical Modeling of Ablation Heat Transfer" (NTRS 20140008557)](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20140008557)
- **FDS 3D pyrolysis.** Moving boundaries are handled by "burn away — remove solid cells as cell density goes to zero" — [McDermott et al., FEMTC 2018 slides](https://files.thunderheadeng.com/femtc/2018_d2-07-mcdermott-presentation.pdf)
- **Gpyro3D.** Geometry is handled by masking grid cells on a regular Cartesian grid, i.e. fixed grid plus cell activity flags (snippet-level; the burnout logic was not confirmed) — [Lautenberger, Fire Safety Science 11 (2014)](https://publications.iafss.org/publications/fss/11/193)
- **Activation in particle methods.** The material point method uses a background mesh with "full, partly full (active) and empty elements (inactive), which can be deactivated or activated by the (freely) moving particles" — [Engin et al. 2015, doi 10.1002/nag.2376](https://doi.org/10.1002/nag.2376)
- **Moving grids and topology change.** Moving-grid (interface-tracking) methods find topological changes "difficult to model", whereas level set handles them easily — [Javierre et al. 2005/06, §5](https://diamhomes.ewi.tudelft.nl/~kvuik/papers/Jav05VVZ.pdf). An r-adaptive moving-mesh FE method on an *enthalpy* formulation (interface not mesh-tracked) handled 2D Stefan problems with cusp-shaped fronts and topology changes — [Beckett, Mackenzie & Robertson 2001, JCP 168:500–518 (abstract)](https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/2006/)

### Inferences
- **Concrete hybrid for Pyrolysis.jl.** Set ż_i = v_s(z_i) + c_i with c_i = (1/τ)·(MMPDE5 residual of an equidistribution monitor). Pin the back node, and move the surface node with the physical surface velocity (the top-face solid velocity, as now). Interior nodes are biased toward material motion by putting a "solid mass per length" term in M. With c = 0 this reduces exactly to today's scheme. Two design choices matter:
  - (a) A monitor floor α > 0 bounds the smallest cell at about (α/∫M)·L(t). Collapse of an individual cell is then *impossible while the domain has finite thickness* (Huang–Kamenski-type bound).
  - (b) The correction must be smooth in the state. A switch such as "c ≠ 0 only when Δz < Δz_min" is nondifferentiable and stiff, so use a soft (softplus or rational) activation, or simply always-on weak relaxation.
- **What burnout looks like under the hybrid.** Volumetric conversion shrinks the solid, so the total thickness L(t) = Σ Δz_i falls through the strain-driven surface velocity, exactly as now. A cell whose solid is consumed no longer collapses on its own: the correction redistributes the shrinkage over all N cells, and solid species leave or enter each cell through the relative-velocity face fluxes. A near-surface cell's solid inventory goes to zero *inside a cell of finite width*, which becomes gas/void-filled (porosity → 1). This "dead cell" stays in the state vector with no event. Requirements: property models that are well-defined at zero solid fraction, and positivity-preserving (upwind or limited) species fluxes.
- **The irreducible endpoint.** With zero residue, L(t) → 0 at complete burn-through and *every* body-fitted method (Lagrangian, hybrid, Landau, MMPDE) degenerates at that instant. The hybrid turns N merge events into one terminal event: stop the run, or stop at a minimum thickness. With char residue, L(t) stays finite and the hybrid needs no events at all.
- **Swelling/intumescence (10–50×).** The same equidistribution redistributes nodes toward the dense virgin layer and reaction front instead of letting swollen char cells stretch 10–50×. The price is relative advection of solid composition through faces (numerical diffusion of the char/virgin interface; see Section 1). That argues for the material term in M, keeping the mesh close to Lagrangian where there are composition gradients and relaxing it where the solid is homogeneous char.
- **Alternative with no correction velocity: fixed (Eulerian) domain with void cells.** Keep the original domain and let the solid fraction go to zero in place (the volume-fraction/"volumetric ablation" representation). The domain then never shrinks, so cells never collapse. But this cannot represent the free surface's position sharply. For shrinking (non-swelling) materials it replaces geometric recession with a porosity profile, and surface boundary conditions must then be applied at a diffuse interface. This is closer to how fixed-grid 3D codes (FDS burn-away, Gpyro3D masking) work, except that they *do* use discrete deactivation.

### Gaps
- I found no paper that documents continuous ALE (w = v_s + correction) in a pyrolysis or ablation code with burnout to zero thickness. Whether such codes exist under other names (e.g. "adaptive Lagrangian" in solid-propellant or hybrid-rocket fuel-regression models) was not established.
- How PATO (NASA/OpenFOAM) moves its mesh during recession was not found in the sources retrieved.
- Gpyro's 1D burnout and cell-removal logic was not confirmed from primary documentation.

## 4. Front-fixing / Landau transformation / boundary immobilization for receding surfaces

### Takeaway
Landau/boundary-immobilization maps the receding domain onto a fixed computational interval. It is simple, verified for charring ablation (Amar et al. "contracting grid", Blackwell–Hogan), and can reach second order in space and time with care (Mitchell & Vynnycky). It is the special case of a moving mesh whose velocity is linear in ξ. It has three drawbacks: (i) a singular advective term as thickness → 0 or for instantaneous material removal (spallation); (ii) awkward coupling to non-ablating substrate layers; (iii) for internal composition gradients, the grid is non-material everywhere, so solid species are numerically advected across grid lines. For a volumetrically reacting, nonuniformly straining solid it is strictly worse than the current Lagrangian mesh for composition and no better for burn-through.

### Cited Findings
- **How front-fixing works, and its drawbacks.** With front-fixing (Landau), "the ablating surface is fixed in the transformed coordinate … The influence of the moving boundary is accounted for in an additional advective term". It "can result in awkward interfacing between ablating materials and nonablating substrate materials", and "modeling of instantaneous spallation is not possible since the advective term in the transformed energy equation becomes infinite" — [Ewing, Laker & Walker (NTRS 20140008557)](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20140008557)
- **Use in ablation codes.** Amar et al. "used a front-fixing method with a Landau transformation to successfully solve pyrolysis and thermochemical ablation problems … using a contracting grid that does not require nodal dropping at the interface" — [Ewing et al.](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20140008557). Blackwell & Hogan, "One-dimensional ablation using Landau transformation and finite control volume procedure" (J. Thermophysics Heat Transfer), is cited as prior art — [Amar, Calvert & Kirk 2011 reference list (NTRS 20110000471)](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20110000471)
- **Second-order boundary immobilization.** Mitchell & Vynnycky combine boundary immobilization with the Keller box scheme for one-phase ablation-type Stefan problems. Second order in time and space needs "variable transformations that must be built into the numerical algorithm", and the ablation front initially moves as t^{3/2} — [Mitchell & Vynnycky 2012, JCAM 236:4181–4192 (abstract)](https://pure.ul.ie/en/publications/an-accurate-finite-difference-method-for-ablation-type-stefan-pro/). Their 2009 paper handles start-up from zero-thickness regions with second order in both time and space — [Mitchell & Vynnycky 2009, Appl. Math. Comput. (abstract)](https://pure.ul.ie/en/publications/finite-difference-methods-with-increased-accuracy-and-correct-ini/). A 2016 paper extends this to a two-phase Stefan problem "with phase formation and depletion" — [U. Limerick record](https://pure.ul.ie/en/publications/on-the-accurate-numerical-solution-of-a-two-phase-stefan-problem-/)
- **Accuracy in a 1D Stefan comparison.** The moving-grid (front-tracking) method and level set gave comparable accuracy on 1D Stefan melting. The paper reports "first order convergence in the interface position" with N = 100–1600 and Δt refined. Note: the moving-grid errors in its own Table 1 (7.27e-4 → 4.95e-4 → 3.43e-4 → 2.39e-4 → 1.68e-4 per doubling of N) imply an observed order of about 0.5, not 1, so the claim and the table disagree. Phase field was more accurate but needed much finer interfacial resolution and had parameters hard to derive from physical properties — [Javierre et al., TU Delft report 05-03, §4 Table 1, §5](https://diamhomes.ewi.tudelft.nl/~kvuik/papers/Jav05VVZ.pdf); [journal record JCAM 192 (2006)](https://research.tudelft.nl/en/publications/a-comparison-of-numerical-models-for-one-dimensional-stefan-probl)
- **Equivalence to moving meshes.** For PDEs with scaling symmetries, r-refinement "becomes equivalent to the use of an appropriate coordinate transformation" — [Budd et al. 2009, §1.2](https://people.bath.ac.uk/mascjb/Papers09/acta.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Landau is the hybrid with c chosen to keep the spacing uniform.** Concretely, w(ξ) = z_back + ξ·ż_surf with v_s ignored inside. For Pyrolysis.jl it is therefore the cheapest "never-collapse" mesh to implement: one line for the target velocity plus the relative-velocity fluxes from Section 1. But it puts nonzero relative velocity wherever the internal strain is nonuniform, which is everywhere during pyrolysis (shrinking reaction zone, swelling char, inert virgin layer). That smears solid composition fronts and gives up the current exact solid transport. MMPDE-with-material-monitor (Section 2/3) is a strictly better version of the same idea.
- **Burn-through.** The transformed advective term scales as ż_surf/L(t) and diverges as L → 0. That is the same endpoint singularity every body-fitted approach has; front-fixing offers no advantage for complete burn-through. "Phase depletion" treatments (Mitchell & Vynnycky 2016) show the endpoint can be handled analytically for simple Stefan problems, but nothing comparable was found for reactive porous solids.
- **Composite samples.** Multi-layer samples (ablator on substrate, sample on an insulating backing) need piecewise transformations per layer with interface conditions, which is the "awkward interfacing" Ewing et al. cite. The current Lagrangian mesh handles layers naturally.

### Gaps
- No direct accuracy and cost comparison of Landau front-fixing vs. Lagrangian moving mesh for *volumetric* pyrolysis (as opposed to surface ablation) was found.
- The full text of Blackwell & Hogan and of Amar et al.'s contracting-grid verification was not retrieved here.

## 5. h-adaptivity (split/merge) in method-of-lines with implicit integrators; cost of changing state size; fixed-state-size alternatives

### Takeaway
Changing the mesh (regridding, splitting, merging) between steps is cheap in data-structure terms with one-step integrators (ARKODE "resize" keeps the temporal-adaptivity heuristics; Rosenbrock + adaptive Rothe codes such as KARDOS remesh every step). With multistep BDF it is expensive (history must be rebuilt or the order restarts at 1). The dominant measured cost is not the resize itself: it is the loss of step size and order after each event and the error introduced by interpolation. Furzeland et al. quantify this (frequent regridding roughly doubles steps and multiplies Jacobians about 7× versus infrequent regridding). Fixed-state-size alternatives are parking or ghost cells (current), activity masks (Gpyro3D, MPM), and r-adaptation.

### Cited Findings
- **ARKODE resize.** "Since ARKODE is built for one-step methods, it is natural to apply it to PDEs that adaptively refine the spatial grid. Here, the ARKODE 'resize' functions support simulations in which the number of equations and unknowns … change between time steps … without destroying the temporal adaptivity heuristics." By contrast, ARKODE re-initialization clears all history ("previous step sizes, temporal error estimates, stored solutions") — [Reynolds, Gardner, Woodward & Chinomona, ARKODE paper (arXiv 2205.14077), §4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.14077)
- **CVODE/CVODES resize.** SUNDIALS 7.x added resizing for CVODE/CVODES, which "requires a user supplied history of solution and right-hand side values at the new problem size" (CVodeResizeHistory). This reflects the multistep (BDF/Adams) history requirement. Search-snippet level, not verified on the exact docs page — [SUNDIALS docs](https://sundials.readthedocs.io/en/v7.3.0/ida/Introduction_link.html)
- **Regridding cost with BDF.** Frequent regridding forced implicit-Euler restarts and caused 259 error-test failures, mostly right after regridding, with "the extra cost of a Jacobian update after regridding". Limiting regridding to ~100 events halved steps (663 → 331) and cut Jacobians 709 → 98. Conclusion: "a significant improvement can be obtained when the number of regriddings is limited", and an advantage of Lagrangian schemes with fixed N is that "they do not require interpolation" — [Furzeland, Verwer & Zegeling 1990, §4–5](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/1640/1640D.pdf)
- **Rosenbrock + adaptive Rothe (KARDOS).** Linearly implicit Rosenbrock integrators are paired with adaptive FE in space. The one-step structure allows step-size changes and spatial refinement or coarsening at every time level, and the spatial discretization is treated as a perturbation controlled within each step. Snippet-level summary of the ZIB reports — [KARDOS user guide, ZIB-Report 02-42](https://opus4.kobv.de/opus4-zib/files/709/ZR-02-42.pdf); [Lang, ZIB SC-94-34](https://opus4.kobv.de/opus4-zib/files/162/SC-94-34.pdf)
- **SAMR with BDF and sensitivities.** Structured AMR has been combined with variable-order BDF (DASPK 3.0, with built-in sensitivity capability) for time-dependent PDEs — [Li & Petzold 2004, JCP 198:310–325](https://cse.cs.ucsb.edu/sites/default/files/publications/adjpde2.pdf); [Fields Institute talk](https://www.fields.utoronto.ca/talks/Implicit-Implementation-and-Sensitivity-Analysis-PDEs-Adaptive-Mesh-Refinement)
- **Connectivity overheads.** "In an h-adaptive mesh connectivity can be a significant issue and changes every time a local mesh refinement step is implemented", whereas r-methods keep constant sparsity — [Budd et al. 2009, §1.2, §2.3](https://people.bath.ac.uk/mascjb/Papers09/acta.pdf)
- **Fixed-state-size precedents.** Cell masking on a fixed Cartesian grid (Gpyro3D) — [Lautenberger 2014](https://publications.iafss.org/publications/fss/11/193). Active/inactive elements in MPM — [Engin et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1002/nag.2376)

### Inferences
- **Why parking/merging is expensive here.** Pyrolysis.jl's park/merge is h-coarsening at fixed state size. Its measured cost is the step-size restart, not the resize: the Furzeland mechanism exactly. With a one-step SDIRK/Rosenbrock integrator nothing *requires* restarting at 1e-6 s. ARKODE resizes one-step integrators while keeping the controller state. A modest cut (e.g. 0.1–0.5 × the pre-event dt), or a step proposed from the post-event Jacobian's stiffness, is the standard alternative. With growth factor q_max per accepted step, recovery from 1e-6 s to a typical dt of 0.1–1 s costs about log(10^5–10^6)/log(q_max) steps per event: about 5–6 steps for q_max = 10, about 17–20 for q_max = 2. Multiply by up to one event per burnt cell. This is a cheap experiment independent of any mesh redesign.
- **Merge interpolation error.** Merging two cells is a conservative first-order remap, i.e. one Lagrange-remap event. Its error is outside the error estimator, but it occurs only once per burnt cell, so the accumulated diffusion is bounded by N events.
- **Avoid h-adaptivity as the primary design.** It makes the state size variable, which hurts differentiability (Section 6) and works against SciML's fixed-size Jacobian sparsity and coloring. Its advantage, refining where needed, is better obtained via r-adaptation (Section 2) for the 1D solver.

### Gaps
- I found no published measurement of SciML/OrdinaryDiffEq `resize!` overhead or step-size behaviour after resizing in implicit solvers. Behaviour in Julia would need a local benchmark.
- The KARDOS solution-transfer details and their accuracy impact were not read in full.

## 6. Differentiability: which approaches keep the discrete solution a smooth function of parameters? Adjoint/sensitivity literature for moving and topology-changing meshes

### Takeaway
A fixed-size ODE system with a smooth, state-dependent mesh velocity (continuous ALE, MMPDE with a smooth monitor, Landau) keeps the semi-discrete solution a smooth function of parameters. Forward and adjoint sensitivities then follow from standard ODE sensitivity theory, with the mesh coordinates as ordinary states; the deforming-mesh adjoint literature shows the GCL/mesh variables need their own adjoint. Discrete events (merges, regrids, h-adaptation, cell deactivation) make the solution piecewise smooth. Sensitivities exist between events via jump conditions (Galán–Feehery–Barton) when event times vary smoothly with the parameters. The parameter-to-output map becomes nonsmooth wherever the number or sequence of events changes (Khan–Barton generalized derivatives), and AD through adaptive-mesh solvers needs special treatment (Li–Petzold ADDA).

### Cited Findings
- **Hybrid sensitivity functions (Galán, Feehery & Barton 1999).** The general equations for sensitivity functions of hybrid discrete/continuous DAE systems are derived, with sufficient existence and uniqueness conditions for ODEs and linear time-invariant DAEs, and implemented as a generic feature of a modeling environment — [Appl. Numer. Math. 31:17–47](https://yoric.mit.edu/?p=420)
- **Generalized derivatives (Khan & Barton 2017).** The framework is extended to L-smooth (generalized) derivatives, covering cases "where small parameter changes alter the sequence of discrete modes", with event-time sensitivities via implicit functions — [Khan & Barton, IEEE TAC 2017](https://yoric.mit.edu/biblio/generalized-derivatives-hybrid-systems)
- **Adjoints across events.** Adjoint sensitivities for hybrid multibody systems use a "jump sensitivity matrix" at event times to relate direct and adjoint sensitivities — [Multibody Syst. Dyn. 2020, doi 10.1007/s11044-020-09726-0](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s11044-020-09726-0)
- **AD through adaptive meshes (Li & Petzold 2004).** The ADDA hybrid of "adjoint of discretization" and "discretization of adjoint" "removes the obstacles of applying AD to adaptive methods" and needs only limited knowledge of the forward solver. It was applied with SAMR + BDF — [Li & Petzold 2004, JCP 198:310–325](https://cse.cs.ucsb.edu/sites/default/files/publications/adjpde2.pdf)
- **Static meshes simplify adjoints.** Static meshes "avoid complications such as the differentiation of mesh motion equations" in discrete adjoints for space-time adaptive solvers — [Alexe (Sandu group), Virginia Tech dissertation](https://vtechworks.lib.vt.edu/handle/10919/37515)
- **GCL needs its own adjoint.** For deforming domains, outputs "in general depend on both the state and GCL variable, so a separate adjoint equation must be solved for the GCL" — [Kast & Fidkowski 2013](https://public.websites.umich.edu/~kfid/MYPUBS/Kast_Fidkowski_2013.pdf); [Kast, Ceze & Fidkowski 2012](https://public.websites.umich.edu/~kfid/MYPUBS/Kast_Ceze_Fidkowski_2012.pdf)
- **Derivatives with respect to node positions.** Discrete-adjoint evaluation of FE derivatives with respect to mesh node positions is covered in [ETNA 32 (2008) pp. 134–144](https://etna.ricam.oeaw.ac.at/vol.32.2008/pp134-144.dir/pp134-144.pdf). PDE-constrained r-adaptivity has been posed as an optimization whose implicit gradient term comes from adjoint sensitivity, and it needed convolution-based gradient regularization for stability — [Kolev, Lazarov, Mittal, Schmidt & Tomov 2025, arXiv 2507.01917](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.01917)
- **One dynamical system.** "Under certain circumstances, the adaptive strategy when coupled with the PDE can be regarded as one (large) dynamical system, which may then be amenable to a combined analysis" — [Budd et al. 2009, §1.2](https://people.bath.ac.uk/mascjb/Papers09/acta.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Smoothness ranking for inverse analysis** (best first):
  1. Current Lagrangian mesh *without* any burnout event, or the hybrid/MMPDE/Landau continuous mesh with smooth monitor and soft floors. Smooth in θ; sensitivities are ordinary ODE sensitivities, with z_i included.
  2. The current scheme with merge/park events. Piecewise smooth: the merge time t_m(θ) is defined by a state threshold and is generically smooth in θ, so FD gradients are fine while the *set* of merges before each observation time is unchanged. A kink appears whenever a perturbation moves a merge across an observation time or changes the merge count. On top of that, the dt reset to 1e-6 s changes the whole downstream step sequence, adding FD noise of the order of the integration tolerance that does not shrink with the FD step.
  3. Discrete remap/regrid and h-adaptivity. Nonsmooth at every event, and state size may vary.
- **Hidden nondifferentiability.** Even "continuous" schemes become nonsmooth through min/max in limiters (minmod, van Leer), hard clamps (Δz_min, property brackets, positivity clips) and switch-activated corrections. For inverse work use smooth limiters, or first-order upwind for T, and soft activations. Since the code currently uses FD gradients (no AD sensitivity extension), the practical goal is to remove event-driven and clamp-driven noise. A continuous hybrid mesh removes the burnout events, which are the main source.
- **Error-control noise is mesh-independent.** Adaptive time stepping itself also causes FD noise. Tight tolerances, or freezing the step sequence for the perturbed solve, are the known mitigations (general practice; no specific source retrieved here).

### Gaps
- I found no paper on sensitivity or adjoint analysis for *cell-merging or deactivation* events in pyrolysis or ablation solvers, or for moving-mesh (r-adaptive MMPDE) MOL solvers specifically. Li & Petzold cover AMR. Kast & Fidkowski and the aerodynamics literature cover prescribed or elastic mesh deformation.
- The Galán et al. jump conditions were not read in full; only abstract-level confirmation was obtained.

## 7. 2D extensibility of each option

### Takeaway
Merging has no clean 2D analogue. The options that extend are:
- elastic, Laplacian or spring mesh motion driven by the boundary (ablation codes: CHAR in 3D, Blackwell–Hogan 2D, Dec–Braun 3D);
- variational MMPDE (nD theory, including non-tangling; simultaneous coupling becomes hard, so alternating plus conservative interpolation is typical);
- multi-D indirect ALE (mature in hydrocodes and poromechanics);
- fixed-grid deactivation or masking (FDS burn-away, Gpyro3D) for topology change and burn-through.

Front-fixing does not extend beyond simple shapes. A modelling point specific to Pyrolysis.jl: in 2D the solid velocity can no longer be obtained by integrating volumetric strain from a back face, so a displacement or mechanics solve is required anyway. That solve doubles as a natural mesh-motion operator.

### Cited Findings
- **State of the art by dimension.** Moving-mesh methods are "generally well understood in one spatial dimension"; in higher dimensions the simultaneous (quasi-Lagrangian) approach is "much harder … the coupled system can be rather stiff and the equations are very nonlinear". The alternating approach with conservative interpolation (Tang & Tang 2003) is used in 2D — [Budd et al. 2009, §1.3, §3](https://people.bath.ac.uk/mascjb/Papers09/acta.pdf)
- **Non-tangling in any dimension.** Huang–Kamenski's mesh-nonsingularity result for MMPDE holds in any dimension with geometric discretization, with element volumes bounded below — [Huang & Kamenski 2018](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1512.04971)
- **CHAR in 1D/2D/3D.** Mesh motion by linear elasticity on unstructured meshes, explicit, with subdomain restriction. The initial BCs were insufficient for large deformations, so 2022 added an RBF surface definition plus optimization/elasticity/spring redistribution, robust on a highly curved large-deformation case (BOLT) — [Amar et al. AIAA 2016-3385](https://doi.org/10.2514/6.2016-3385); [Amar et al. AIAA 2022](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20210022987/downloads/AIAA_2022_Mesh_Deformation.pdf)
- **Earlier multi-D ablation codes.** CVFEM with linear elastic mesh motion for 1D and 2D ablation (Blackwell, Hogan, Cochran); FEM with linear elastic mesh motion for 3D ablation including pyrolysis gas flow (Dec & Braun) — [Amar, Calvert & Kirk 2011](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20110000471)
- **Multi-D indirect ALE.** Indirect ALE on general polygonal meshes is reviewed in [Barlow et al. 2016](https://laro.lanl.gov/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Arbitrary-LagrangianEulerian-methods-for-modeling-high-speed/9916361457803761). Swept-region remap is cheaper than intersection remap but can break symmetry — [doi 10.1002/fld.4384](https://doi.org/10.1002/fld.4384). Operator-split ALE for porous media was demonstrated on a 2D embankment under earthquake loading — [Di et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.1002/nag.601)
- **2D Stefan problems.** An r-adaptive moving-mesh FE method on an enthalpy formulation handled 2D Stefan problems including cusps and topology changes — [Beckett, Mackenzie & Robertson 2001](https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/2006/). A later distributed moving-mesh ALE formulation solved one- and two-phase Stefan problems in 1D and 2D (authors not confirmed from snippet) — [CiCP 6(3):595–624, 2009](https://doc.journal.global-sci.org/intro/article_detail/cicp/7980.html)
- **ALE and topology.** The ALE formulation is "more convenient for higher dimensional problems", but topological changes are difficult for moving-grid methods — [Javierre et al.](https://diamhomes.ewi.tudelft.nl/~kvuik/papers/Jav05VVZ.pdf)
- **Fixed-grid topology handling.** FDS removes solid cells as density → 0 in 3D — [McDermott et al. 2018](https://files.thunderheadeng.com/femtc/2018_d2-07-mcdermott-presentation.pdf); Gpyro3D masks cells on a Cartesian grid — [Lautenberger 2014](https://publications.iafss.org/publications/fss/11/193)

### Inferences
- **Kinematics.** In 1D, cumulative volumetric strain from the back face uniquely gives the solid velocity. In 2D, a volumetric-strain-rate field does not determine a displacement field without extra assumptions: irrotational/potential flow, ∇·v = ε̇_v with a constraint, or a mechanics (elastic or viscoelastic) solve. A 2D Pyrolysis.jl therefore needs a "solid kinematics" solver regardless of mesh strategy. A CHAR-like elastic or Laplacian operator, with the volumetric strain as an eigenstrain or source, can deliver both the physical solid velocity and the mesh-smoothing correction: w = v_s + c, with c from a smoothing operator. This makes the 1D hybrid of Section 3 the natural precursor of the 2D design.
- **Burn-through/topology in 2D.** Holes forming, edges burning away and perforation are topology changes no body-fitted moving mesh can follow. The robust 2D route is a fixed or moving mesh plus *void cells*: solid fraction → 0 with properties defined at zero solid, or deactivation masks as in FDS/Gpyro3D. The 1D hybrid's "dead cell with zero solid inventory" is the 1D prototype of that representation and avoids a 1D-only merge mechanism.
- **Cross-option synthesis** (judgement based on the cited evidence; "cost" means work on top of the current code):

| Option | Burnout robustness | Swelling 10–50× | Solid composition accuracy | Error control / events | Differentiability | 2D path | Implementation cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Current Lagrangian + merge/park | events per burnt cell; dt restart dominates | cells stretch | exact (no relative flux) | events outside error control | piecewise smooth | merge has no 2D analogue | none |
| Lagrange-remap (discrete rezone) | removes collapse but adds events | fixes stretching | remap diffusion per event | events outside error control (Furzeland) | nonsmooth | mature (hydrocodes) | medium–high |
| Continuous ALE / hybrid w = v_s + c | no collapse while L > 0; one terminal event | fixes stretching | relative-flux diffusion (needs limited 2nd order for species) | all inside ODE error control | smooth (if soft monitor/limiters) | yes (elastic/Laplacian smoothing) | medium (relative fluxes + monitor) |
| MMPDE r-adaptation | as hybrid (floor in monitor) | fixes stretching, adds resolution | as hybrid; monitor can keep it near-Lagrangian | inside ODE; τ/monitor tuning | smooth | yes, harder (alternating typical) | medium–high |
| Landau front-fixing | no collapse until L → 0 | uniform cells only | relative flux everywhere | inside ODE | smooth | no (simple shapes only) | low–medium |
| h-adaptivity | refine/coarsen events | refine | interpolation per event | events; resize costs | nonsmooth | yes, complex | high |
| Fixed domain + void cells | no geometric collapse | no stretching but no geometric swelling | Eulerian advection of solid | inside ODE | smooth | yes (FDS/Gpyro3D-like) | medium; changes surface BC treatment |

### Gaps
- No source quantifies 2D cost and robustness of elastic mesh motion for *intumescent* (large-swelling) materials. CHAR's large-deformation work concerns recession, not 10–50× expansion.
- No source was found describing how the 1D "cumulative strain" kinematics generalizes in existing multi-D pyrolysis or intumescence codes. This is the key modelling prerequisite for a 2D path and should be researched separately (e.g. multi-D intumescent coating models, PATO's solid-motion treatment).
